# Story: from a topic to a 60-second experience

## 1. Research first (15–20 minutes; it prevents re-voicing)

- Read 3–5 solid sources: review articles, university or medical pages (NIH/NINDS, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, Sleep
  Foundation), a science journalist's explainer (BBC Future, Scientific American), and the Wikipedia article for its
  references. Use WebSearch/WebFetch.
- Write the claims table for the README's science notes before writing a word of script:
  - the claim;
  - its status: established, leading idea, debated, or myth;
  - its source.
  Anything below "established" is said as a hedge ("Scientists think…", "One idea…", "Some studies say… another says
  meh").
- Find the three things a curious friend would say "wait, what?" to:
  - the counter-intuitive mechanism (your body does it ON PURPOSE);
  - the proof (people with damaged finger nerves don't wrinkle);
  - the weird bonus fact (same pattern every time; hiccups are the same kind of twitch).

## 2. The angle and the hook

- **The strongest curiosity angle is almost always an experience the viewer has had**, told in second person: "You're
  drifting off to sleep… and your whole body JUMPS!" / "Stay in the bath long enough… and your fingers turn
  into… raisins."
- **The hook line is under 3 seconds, starts mid-action, and ends on a jolt, a reveal or a question.** Picture
  and sound hit on the same frame (the jolt, the splash, the strike).
- **Beat 2 answers the hook with a joke or a deadpan reaction** ("Wow. Thanks, body." / "raisins."). It buys the
  explanation 50 more seconds.
- **Then name it and make it relatable** ("That's a hypnic jerk. And up to seventy percent of people get them.").

## 3. The shape (what the three shipped Shorts share)

| Beat | Lightning (66 s) | Hypnic jerk (72 s) | Finger wrinkles (64 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | storm, the strike | drifting off → JUMPS | hand in the bath → raisins |
| Reaction / name | survival odds | "Wow. Thanks, body." → HYPNIC JERK, 70 % | THE OLD IDEA: sponge → "Nope." |
| Mechanism, shown | current through the body, nerves, heart | the awake→asleep handover, brainstem burst, "BAM" | pores → nerves → vessels squeeze → skin buckles |
| Twist / proof | why most survive | "are we FALLING?!" panic button | damaged nerves don't wrinkle → nerve test |
| Debate, hedged | — | tree-ancestor idea, stamped UNPROVEN | grip: "helps… meh. Science." |
| Bonus fact | — | same kind of twitch as hiccups | same pattern every time |
| Button | final strike | "goodnight. [whispers] Probably." + one last jolt | "snow tires" → dive back in (loop) |

- One idea per block and one visual per idea. Every 3–5 s there is a new question or reveal: "So what's going
  on?", "How do we know?", "So why bother?", "And the weirdest part?"
- Escalate. Each reveal should be stranger or bigger than the last. Put the weirdest true fact near the end,
  just before the button.
- Comedy comes from situation and timing, not from wacky voices:
  - a deadpan understatement after a big moment;
  - a callback (raisin → raisins → "turn into a raisin");
  - an anticlimax ("Unproven… but cool.");
  - the body as a clumsy coworker.
- The button: a last line that reframes the whole thing ("that's just your body putting on snow tires") or undercuts
  it ("Probably."), plus a final visual gag or loop. No call to action.

## 4. Picking topics

A good CurioPulse topic:
- is a question people Google or ask out loud;
- is about the body, the brain or everyday physics;
- has a mechanism you can SHOW inside something (a body, a cell, a cloud);
- has a funny human situation to hang it on.

`topics.md` holds the queue. Before adding one, check it isn't already on the channel (`videos.md`) or in the
queue. Prefer topics where the hiker can be the one it happens to.

## 5. The one-page plan (write it in the video README before any code)

1. The hook sentence and the button line.
2. The claims table (science notes).
3. The beat list, with a time estimate each (≈2.3 words/s at Jessica's pace plus the gaps).
4. For every beat:
   - the picture (world, camera move, what changes);
   - the sound (hit, bed, music state);
   - the caption emphasis.
5. The world files needed:
   - reuse from past videos where possible (`engine.md`, "Borrowing from past videos");
   - list what must be built new.
6. The cover frame: which moment, what text.
