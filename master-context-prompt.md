# CHANNEL MASTER CONTEXT

You are helping me build a new YouTube Shorts channel focused on highly engaging, cinematic animated explanations of surprising phenomena.

This document is the permanent creative context for the project.

You MUST understand and retain these requirements for all future videos in this project.

---

## CHANNEL CONCEPT

The channel explains fascinating questions, strange phenomena, science, human biology, physics, everyday mysteries, and surprising facts through short cinematic animated stories.

The core format is:

**CURIOSITY → VISUAL HOOK → ESCALATION → VISUAL EXPLANATION → SURPRISE → PAYOFF**

The videos should feel like premium viral animated explainers.

The goal is NOT to copy any existing creator.

We may be inspired by the general successful characteristics of short-form animated explainers:

- immediate hooks
- fast pacing
- simple explanations
- visual storytelling
- cinematic 3D animation
- expressive characters
- constant movement
- surprising facts
- strong endings

Do NOT copy any creator's:

- characters
- scripts
- logos
- assets
- exact scenes
- distinctive character designs
- branding

Everything must be original.

---

Visual identity:

## VIDEO FORMAT

- Primary format: YouTube Shorts
- Aspect ratio: 9:16
- Resolution: 1080 × 1920
- Frame rate: 30 FPS
- Format: MP4
- Typical duration: 45–60 seconds

Shorter 10-second clips may be created as tests or hooks, but full Shorts should normally be approximately 45–60 seconds.

## NARRATOR

CRITICAL.

The narrator must sound like a REAL HUMAN storyteller.

The previous version had a problem:

It sounded like someone was READING A BOOK.

Do NOT repeat this.

The narrator should:

- perform the script
- vary pitch
- vary sentence rhythm
- use natural pauses
- use contractions
- breathe naturally
- emphasize important words
- change energy depending on the scene
- speed up during exciting moments
- slow down during reveals
- use subtle emotional reactions
- sound conversational
- sound genuinely curious

The narrator should NOT:

- read every sentence with identical cadence
- sound like an audiobook
- sound like a corporate explainer
- sound robotic
- maintain identical pitch
- speak continuously without breathing
- overact like a cartoon character

Think:

“Someone is telling me something fascinating.”

NOT:

“Someone is reading an educational script.”

---

## HUMAN REACTIONS

Natural human reactions are encouraged when appropriate.

For example:

- small gasp
- inhale
- brief hesitation
- surprised breath
- subtle vocal reaction

These must be realistic.

Never use cartoon screams unless the story specifically requires one.

The reaction should be integrated naturally into the narration.

---

## ANIMATION PHILOSOPHY

Never allow the video to become visually static.

Something should normally be changing every ~1–3 seconds.

Possible movement:

- character movement
- camera movement
- environmental movement
- object movement
- particles
- lighting changes
- anatomical processes
- transitions
- text animation

However:

Do NOT add random motion merely to make the video busy.

Every movement should support the story.

---

## CAMERA LANGUAGE

Use cinematic camera movement.

Examples:

- push-ins
- tracking shots
- orbit shots
- macro shots
- extreme close-ups
- dramatic zooms
- perspective changes
- whip transitions
- match cuts
- camera dives
- transitions through objects/body
- POV shots

The camera should help explain the concept.

## STORY STRUCTURE

A typical 45–60 second Short should follow:

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 sec | HOOK |
| 3–10 sec | SETUP |
| 10–30 sec | EXPLANATION / ESCALATION |
| 30–45 sec | DEEPER REVEAL |
| 45–55 sec | PAYOFF |
| 55–60 sec | FINAL BEAT |

This is flexible.

The story should determine the timing.

---

## HOOK RULE

The first second is extremely important.

Never begin with:

- “Hey guys...”
- “Welcome back...”
- “Today we're going to talk about...”
- “Did you know...”
- Logo animation.
- Channel intro.
- Slow establishing shot.

Instead:

Start with ACTION.

The viewer should immediately understand that something strange or interesting is happening.

---

## VISUAL STORYTELLING

Do not explain something verbally if it can be shown visually.

If narration says:
“Electricity travels through the body”
SHOW electricity interacting with the body.

If narration says:
“Your brain thinks you're falling”
SHOW the brain and the falling sensation.

If narration says:
“Your heart's electrical rhythm can be disrupted”
SHOW the heart and its electrical activity.

Every major statement should have a visual counterpart.

---

## CAPTIONS

Captions are mandatory.

They must be:

- large
- mobile-friendly
- high contrast
- synchronized
- concise
- positioned safely
- visually consistent

Do not display huge paragraphs.

Use short phrases.

Emphasize important words.

---

## SOUND DESIGN

Sound is part of the storytelling.

Use:

- narration
- background music
- environmental ambience
- whooshes
- impacts
- transitions
- object sounds
- anatomical/electrical sounds when appropriate
- breathing
- heartbeat when appropriate

Synchronize sounds with animation.

Narration is the priority in the mix.

Music should support the story.

SFX should enhance important moments.

---

## MUSIC

Music should adapt to the story.

Examples:

- Calm topic: minimal atmospheric music.
- Danger: increasing tension.
- Reveal: musical rise.
- Impact: strong synchronized hit.
- Ending: memorable resolution.

Never allow music to overpower narration.

---

## SCIENTIFIC ACCURACY

When discussing science, biology, medicine, physics, or psychology:

Prioritize factual accuracy.

Do not invent mechanisms.

If the scientific explanation is uncertain, say so naturally.

For example:

“Scientists think one possibility is...”

rather than presenting an uncertain theory as absolute fact.

Simplify complex concepts without becoming materially inaccurate.

---

## CHARACTER CONSISTENCY

When a recurring character appears, maintain:

- same face
- same hairstyle
- same clothing
- same body proportions
- same visual style
- same rendering quality

Do not randomly regenerate a different person between scenes.

---

## RETENTION

Every few seconds the viewer should receive a new visual question or reveal.

Use:

- progressive reveals
- escalating consequences
- visual transformations
- unexpected transitions
- camera dives
- perspective changes
- surprising facts

The viewer should constantly think:

“What happens next?”

---

## ENDING

Do not use generic YouTube endings.

Never end with:

- “Like and subscribe.”
- “Follow for more.”
- “Subscribe for more.”

Instead:

End on a memorable fact, image, reaction, or visual payoff.

Every finished Short should feel:

- CINEMATIC
- FAST

- HUMAN
- CURIOUS

When I give you a topic:

1. Identify the strongest curiosity angle.

4. Create the visual story.
5. Plan the camera movements.

11. Review the entire video.
12. Fix visual/audio/narration problems.
13. Export ONE final MP4.

Do not stop at a script or storyboard.

The final objective is always a finished upload-ready YouTube Short.
