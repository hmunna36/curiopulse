A CURIOUS Short (the topic mix of 17 Nov 2026: the Sunday 23:30 slot, picture-led nature on a giant scale, the short arm
30–35 s, no pill, opening arm none). The hiker hugs one white quaking aspen in Pando, Utah, and the whole forest turns out
to be that tree. Three worlds built for it: the forest floor (`src/web/pd_world.js`: white trunks with black eye-shaped
scars that can open, round leaves that quiver, a floor of grass and wildflowers in perspective, a trail sign, his
backpack, a fallen log, and the same floor cut open on one glowing root network); the clone from the air
(`src/web/pd_land.js`: Pando as one patch of aspen crowns among other clones and dark firs, Fish Lake below with the slope
mirrored, glitter, a rowboat and reeds; every crown can turn to autumn on its own clock); and a meadow with a giant
balance (the whole forest on one pan, forty blue whales piling onto the other, one of them turning to aspen bark).
The cinematic look (`publish.json` "look": "cine"): the forest in daylight from the upper right (`L_FOREST`), the cutaway
a softer day light, the clone from the air with almost no pool, autumn a low warm sun from the left (`L_FALL`), the
meadow in daylight.

of the direction sheet (the skill's `reference/direction.md`), written as it was built.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the forest's leaves rustling like light rain (a gated hiss above 5 kHz that can run under words) and a soft
  wind under 260 Hz; a low rumble for the soil; high open air for the hillside and the meadow; all of it drops away for
  the full silence before the payoff.
- **Hits:** glints for the bark eye opening and shutting; the lasso's shimmer and the 1 TREE ding; the flash-forward's
  whoosh; the ground cracking (low) and the light running along the roots (glints) on "UNDERGROUND", the hitstop's
  layered hit and a warm bloom after it; the sucker's ratchet, the sapling's pop in the pause after "trunk", its riser and
  thump, the tag's slap after "sprout"; the count's ticks speeding up, a ka-ching after "of them"; the scanner's buzz and
  beeps, two MATCH dings after "DNA"; the squeeze's creak and the heart; every eye blinking open (a storm of glints) and a
  music-box run up for the hearts after "ALL"; the clones turning (sparse glints); the gold flip: a sparkle storm and a sub
  drop on "SAME", then the biggest hit (a soft-clipped whoosh, a steel-pan chord, a cymbal) in the pause after it; leaves
  raining on him; the balance creaking, a sub slam on "TONNES" and the chains and creak after it; forty whale plops and a
  ding as the beam levels, a little whale call; the bark crackling and the whale's blink; the arms' rubbery stretch and
  strain, and the TWANG as they snap back after "arms."
- **Score:** the loop score: 16 bars at 112.9 BPM (16 × 4 × 0.5312 s = 34.000 s), F–C–Dm–Bb. A light marimba groove
  for the hook, a low drone and bass for the ground opening, the full groove with a steel-pan hook and a shaker for the
  middle, a warm half-time groove for autumn, claps under the balance, and the light groove again on the button with a
  music-box pickup into bar 1. The forest's motif is a music box. Dead stops: "UNDERGROUND" and its hitstop, full silence
  before the payoff through "time.", "of tree.", and the button.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after
  AAC. Gags in their gaps: −17.4 dB (the gold flip's hit, the loudest), −17.6 (the twang), −18.8 to −22 (the others),
  the narrator's words at −16.3 median.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7). Hedged lines are said as hedges; a myth is said as a myth.

| Claim | Status | Sources (two, independent) |
|---|---|---|
| Pando, the forest, is one tree: a single male quaking aspen clone in Fishlake National Forest, Utah, whose stems are one genetic individual | established (DNA: one genotype in 141 of 209 stems sampled, plus a few single-allele variants, presumed somatic mutations; "same DNA" is the Short's plain wording) | Mitton & Grant 1996, *BioScience* 46:25–31; DeWoody et al. 2008, *Western North American Naturalist* 68:493–497 |
| "The real tree is underground": the trunks are stems that grow up from one root system | established that the stems are root suckers of one clone; the single connected root system is assumed, not mapped ("believed to have one massive underground root system") | US Forest Service, "How Aspens Grow"; USU Western Aspen Alliance, "Pando: sizing up" |
| "Every trunk is just a sprout" off its roots | established (aspen clones spread by suckering) | US Forest Service, "How Aspens Grow"; Mitton & Grant 1996 |
| "About forty-seven thousand" trunks (~47,000 on screen) | estimate (a 1970s count; many stems have died since) | DeWoody et al. 2008 ("around 47,000"); USU Western Aspen Alliance ("estimated 47,000"); Fishlake NF ("over 40,000") |
| Each autumn the whole clone turns gold at the same time (and neighbouring clones at other times) | established as a tendency (not infallible: sunny and shady sides can differ) | US Forest Service, "Fall Colors" ("The aspen trees of a particular clone will change color at the same time"); NPS Bryce Canyon, "Quaking Aspen" |
| It weighs "about six thousand tonnes" (~6,000 on screen) | estimate (1992 calculation, roots included; never weighed) | Grant 1993, *Discover*, "The Trembling Giant" ("in excess of 13 million pounds", about 5,900 t); Mitton & Grant 1996 ("more than 6 million kg") |
| That is "forty blue whales" | fair for big whales: a big Antarctic blue whale is about 150 t (6,000 ÷ 150 = 40); against the heaviest ever weighed (190 t) about 31 | NOAA Fisheries, "Blue Whale" ("more than 330,000 pounds"); Natural History Museum London ("up to 180 tonnes") |
| (picture) dark eye-shaped scars on aspen bark | established (where lower branches self-pruned) | NPS Bryce Canyon, "Quaking Aspen"; Minnesota DNR, *Minnesota Conservation Volunteer* 2022 |
| (description) "Pando" is Latin for "I spread" | established | Fishlake NF, "Pando"; Mitton & Grant 1996 |
| (description) its age: thousands of years; "80,000 years" is not supported | debated / unknown (recent genetic estimates 12,000–37,000 years, rated "incomplete") | USU Western Aspen Alliance FAQ ("No good method currently exists"); Pineau et al., bioRxiv 2024 / eLife reviewed preprint |
| Not said: "the heaviest living thing" | debated (the Oregon honey fungus covers more ground; its mass has never been measured) | National Geographic 2022; Ferguson et al. 2003 |

Research for this Short: a research agent's fact-check of 22 Nov 2026 (sources above; Fishlake NF read through the
Wayback copy of 21 Nov 2026).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
