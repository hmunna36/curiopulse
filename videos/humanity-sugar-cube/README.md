## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| An atom is mostly empty space: its nucleus is about 100,000 times smaller across than the atom (about 1 fm against 0.1 nm) and holds more than 99.9 % of its mass | established | Wikipedia "Atomic nucleus" and its references; Discover Magazine, "Why are atoms mostly empty space?" |
| Matter with the empty space removed (nuclear density) is about 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³, the same for every nucleus | established | Wikipedia "Nuclear matter" / "Atomic nucleus"; standard textbook R = 1.2 fm·A^⅓ |
| All of humanity would fit in a sugar cube | established as arithmetic (a popular thought experiment) | ~8.2 billion people (UN World Population Prospects 2024) × roughly 50–62 kg each ≈ 400–500 million tonnes; ÷ 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³ ≈ 2 cm³; a sugar cube is about 2.5–4 cm³. ScienceABC, "Can the entire human race fit inside a sugar cube?" gives the same result |
| An average adult weighs about 62 kg (the scale's reading) | established (2005 data, likely an underestimate now) | Walpole et al., "The weight of nations", BMC Public Health 2012 (LSHTM release) |
| One person squashed the same way would be a speck too small to see | established as arithmetic | 62 kg ÷ 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³ ≈ 2.7×10⁻¹⁶ m³, a cube about 6.5 µm across; the eye resolves about 0.1 mm at reading distance |
| "Cotton candy: all fluff, one tiny speck" | a picture, not a claim | the electron cloud as fluff, the nucleus as the speck (the speck is drawn far too big: the label says 100,000× smaller) |
| It's a thought experiment | said in the description and the pinned comment | ordinary atoms resist being squashed; neutron stars are the real case |

Not said, kept for the description and the comment: the neutron star (a teaspoon weighs about a mountain), and the
exact mass of humanity (sources vary).
