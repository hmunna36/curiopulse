## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** the tight arm, 25–30 s, 55–64 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: the 2026-11-12 11:30 IST slot; no pill: the pill
   test ended 8 Nov; topic kind TEST). 59 words → 28.6 s.
2. **The viewer's test:** move just ONE eye, only the left one. Nobody can on the first try (both eyes go, every time),
   it takes a second, phone in hand, and everyone has a "mine did / mine didn't" for the comments (the rules of 5 Nov: a
   face test most people can't do; the Queue's first line). Hook formula: **The Direct Address**, as a test ("Move just
   one eye. Only your left. Go!"): it confirms the title in its first four words. Candidates: The Question ("Can you move
   just ONE eye?": lick-elbow used it last), The Impossible Claim ("Your eyes are on a leash. Try moving just one.": the
   answer before the test, and the test starts later). The Direct Address won.
3. **Answer (starts 3.83 s):** "Can't? Your eyes are on ONE leash." — one picture for Hering's law: one handle, both eyes
   clipped to it. **Button:** "Now try the RIGHT one." → the loop: "Move just one eye. Only your left. Go!" (it sends the
   viewer back into the test with the other eye).
4. **Beats:** the test (0–3.1: his eyes jump together from butterfly to butterfly) → can't; your eyes are on ONE leash
   (3.8–6.8: he shakes his head, a brain pops up on his head with a leash handle, the lead zips down and clips onto both
   irises) → look left, look right, one tug, BOTH go (7.2–11.0: the viewer looks left and right with him; the brain tugs
   and both eyes go) → now close one eye, fingertip on the lid (11.3–13.9: the viewer does it) → look around, feel that?
   still walking... blindfolded (14.3–18.2: macro; under the shut lid the eye's bulge rolls under his fingertip, an x-ray
   iris still on its lead, a sleep mask pops onto it) → a chameleon gets TWO leashes: one eye forward, one eye back
   (18.6–23.1: its tiny brain holds a lead in each fist; he tries to copy and his eyes go together) → until it spots a fly
   (23.4–25.6: the fly lands on his nose, his eyes cross on it, both chameleon eyes lock on, SNAP, gulp) → now try the
   RIGHT one (26.3–27.9: back to frame 1). No science word is spoken (Hering's law is in the description).
5. **The 8–13 s rule:** 7.2–9.4 the viewer looks left, then right ("Look left. Look right."); 9.7–11.0 they look again
   while the tug shows both eyes going; 11.3–13.9 they shut one eye and put a fingertip on the lid ("Now close one eye.
   Fingertip on the lid."); 14.3–16.0 they look around and feel the shut eye move. The mechanism is ONE picture: two eyes
   on one leash.
6. **The caption's one-word question:** **"Did only one move? Yes or no?"**
7. **Ending:** no pill, nobody says "subscribe"; the button and the loop are the hook at negative time (`hookDraw(t -
   duration)`), so the last frame is frame 0's picture (0.70 s from the last word to the loop).
8. **World files:** `trail.js` (lick-elbow's copy of sky-blue's), `eyes.js` (eyes-shake's, without the family photo that

9. **Cover:** `SC.cover`: the leash moment (the brain yanking, both eyes pulled one way), JUST ONE / EYE?.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a warm breeze under 340 Hz, a far cicada above 6 kHz, a far bird in three pauses.
- **Hits:** a glass tick on every eye jump (above 5 kHz, on the bed bus), a strain ratchet (top only) under the test, the
  butterflies' wings (top only), a "nope" slide under 300 Hz after "Can't?", the brain's pop, the lead's zip, two clips
  and a clink after "leash.", a low whoosh on each tug, a boing on the yank, the lid's bloop, the fingertip's tap, a soft
  roll under the lid each time the eye passes the fingertip, the sleep mask's pop, the chameleon's eyes as servos (top
  only), the fly's buzz (under 250 Hz plus a fizz above 5.2 kHz), THWIP, SNAP, gulp.
- **Score:** a sunny pizzicato and marimba groove (116 BPM) in four sections; it drops out on "ONE leash", "BOTH go",
  "blindfolded", the snap, a stop-time before "one eye back", and into the loop.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qa.py`: 20/20 checks, content words 21.1 dB clear of the mix on average (none under 9.0 dB), whisper WER 0.0 %, -14.03 LUFS, -1.88 dBTP.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You can't move just one eye: both eyes get the same command for every look, and move as a pair ("your eyes are on ONE leash", "one tug, BOTH go") | established as a model (Hering's law of equal innervation, 1868); how exactly vergence is wired, and some small deviations, are still debated | Hering, *The Theory of Binocular Vision* (1868; Plenum 1977); Wikipedia, "Hering's law of equal innervation" (and its references: Yarbus 1967; Bahill et al. 1976; Enright 1984); Iowa EyeRounds atlas, "Hering's law" |
| Even a shut (or covered) eye moves with the open one ("still walking... blindfolded") | established: the covered eye follows the fixing eye's command; it is the basis of the cover tests eye doctors use | EyeWiki (AAO), "Cover Tests"; Vision Sciences Society abstract "Monocular Drift in the Cover Test Is Driven by Binocular Commands Consistent with Hering's Law" |
| You can feel it: a fingertip resting lightly on the shut lid feels the eyeball turn under it | established (the cornea bulges the lid and moves with the eye) | the cover-test sources above; Hering himself listened to eye movements through a tube on the closed lid (Wikipedia references) |
| A chameleon's eyes can look two ways at once, one forward and one back | established (independent scanning, "voluntary strabismus") | Katz et al. 2015, *J Exp Biol* 218, doi:10.1242/jeb.113084; Technion / ScienceDaily, 20 Aug 2015 |
| ...until it spots a fly: then both eyes lock on before the tongue fires | established in that study (the second eye swings to the first eye's target just before the strike) | Katz et al. 2015; ScienceDaily, 20 Aug 2015 |
| (not said) Crossing your eyes is still a both-eyes command (vergence) | established | Wikipedia, "Hering's law of equal innervation"; in the description |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set. The cover: `src/cover.sh`.
