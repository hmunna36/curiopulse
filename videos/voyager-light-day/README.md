# Your Text to VOYAGER 1 Would Take a Whole DAY 🛰️ — Short (28.5 s)

**Final file:** [`voyager-light-day-short.mp4`](voyager-light-day-short.mp4)
MP4 · H.264 High · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 28.5 s · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`voyager-light-day.srt`](voyager-light-day.srt). Cover: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg).

The hiker on his porch at night texts "u ok?" to Voyager 1 (last seen 1977), grabs his own message as it leaves at
the speed of light, surfs it past the Moon and Pluto, dozes off, BONKS into Voyager's dish a day later, rides the
reply home for another day, and gets "beep." Same narrator, captions, bloom and the cinematic look (`"look": "cine"`):
the porch is lit by a wall lantern on the left with the cool sky as his rim; space by a far Sun with his glowing
green text as the rim (`setLights` in `src/web/scenes.js`; the worlds in `src/web/vg_world.js`).

## The plan (written before any code; story.md §5 and §6)

1. **Kind and length:** a CURIOUS Short (the rules of 5 Nov 2026: a mind-bending comparison of time and size) for the
   23:30 IST slot of 18 Nov 2026, the day NASA's Voyager 1 becomes the first human-made object one light-day from
   Earth (10:16 UTC, 15:46 IST). Tight arm: 25–30 s, 62 words.
2. **Hook (The Impossible Claim; it confirms the title word for word):** "Your text to Voyager 1 would take a whole DAY
   to arrive." Frame 0: CU, him on his porch, his thumb on SEND, "u ok?" popping into a chat with "Voyager 1 · last
   seen 1977". The last three Shorts used The Comparison, The Direct Address and The Question.
   **Answer (by 5 s):** "It's not ignoring you... even LIGHT takes that long." (the left-on-read joke, then the
   plain claim). **Button:** "Better hit send now." runs straight into the hook when the Short loops.
3. **Claims:** below (Science notes).
4. **Beats** (≈ 2.15 words a second with the gaps):
   - hook 0–3.3 s: the claim, the ETA pill ARRIVES IN 24 HOURS on DAY, the bubble lifts off the screen;
   - answer ≈ 3.8–6.2 s: the bubble hovers (SENDING...), crickets for the left-on-read pause, he grabs it on LIGHT and
     is yanked into the sky (ONE picture: he rides his own text at the speed of light, through the next two beats);
   - race ≈ 6.5–9.8 s: the Moon whooshes by in one second (the clock 00:00:01), Pluto glides by at five hours
     (05:00:00; he waves);
   - tomorrow ≈ 9.9–11.4 s: he dozes; the clock spins to 24:00:00; BONK into the dish: DELIVERED;
   - reply ≈ 11.8–13.8 s: the grey reply (typing dots) carries him home, arms folded, stubble growing (48:00:00);
   - beep ≈ 14.0–15.0 s: the porch two days later, stubble, the tea gone green: "beep." (the payoff's laugh);
   - weird ≈ 15.4–20.5 s: the scale: 49 years for one light-day (the trail since 1977), then the zoom out to the
     nearest star: ×1,500 (about 75,000 years at Voyager's speed);
   - button ≈ 20.9–22.4 s: back on the porch, typing "u ok?" again, fast; his thumb lands on SEND: frame 0.
5. **Framings and light** (never two alike in a row): CU eye level him left (dusk) → WHIP → MEDIUM low angle him centre
   (dusk) → WHIP → WIDE eye level him left surfing (cold) → MEDIUM high angle (night, then warm on the BONK; punch-in,
   tilt, hitstop) → WHIP → MEDIUM him right (cold) → CU him right, phone left (dusk; punch-in on "Beep.") → WHIP → EXTREME
   WIDE scale (spot) → CU him left (dusk: frame 1's mood for the loop). Particles in every shot (sparkle in space, dust on

6. **Subscribe arm:** none (no pill); the Short loops.
7. **The loop:** the button shot runs on the loop's clock (t − duration); the last 0.43 s are the hook's picture with

8. **Worlds:** new: the porch at night, the chat phone, the text bubble, Voyager 1, the trip clock, the scale. Borrowed

9. **Cover:** the hook's CU on DAY + 0.4 s (the ETA pill up, his shocked face) with "24 HOURS" big.
10. **Caption question:** "Would you wait for the reply? Yes or no?"

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the porch's low night breeze; space's low hum with a faint shimmer above 7 kHz.
- **Hits:** a send "fwip" on frame 1, the ETA's thump on DAY, a riser as the bubble lifts off; crickets for the
  left-on-read pause; the launch whoosh and a rocket roar under 260 Hz; the Moon's and Pluto's low passes; a ding after
  "one second"; a clock ratchet above 5 kHz under the counting; a snore and three music-box notes in the pause before
  TOMORROW; BONK + the layered hitstop hit + the stamp; typing dots; the phone buzzing and its message tone before
  "Beep.", a cricket after; the years ticking, the zoom's riser and a sprinkle of glints for the star; keys tapping.
- **Score:** a light 112 BPM porch groove (F Dm Bb C), out for "a whole DAY" and the launch; a 128 BPM race groove; the
  long way back at half time; a wonder drone that opens up on the zoom; the porch groove again for the loop, easing
  down over the last 0.6 s. It drops out for every punchline.
- **Mix:** voice first, −14 LUFS, true peak −1.9 dBTP in the mix; speech band voice −24.1 dB against −42.7 dB
  everything else; one word under 10 dB ("is", 9.0 dB).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
