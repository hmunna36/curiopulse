**Title:** Why Does the Sun Make You SNEEZE? 🤧

**Description:**
You step out of a dark cinema into bright sunshine and... ACHOO. That's the photic sneeze reflex, and roughly one in
four people have it. It isn't a cold: it's the sudden jump from dark to bright light that sets it off. Subscribe for a
new strange question every day.

Why? Nobody has proved it yet. The leading idea is crossed wires: the nerve from your eyes runs close to the trigeminal
nerve, which feels irritation in your nose, and a sudden flood of light may spill from one into the other, so your
brain thinks something is up your nose. It runs in families, and in 1978 four doctors (sun sneezers themselves) gave
it a real medical name: ACHOO syndrome, for Autosomal dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst. Sunglasses help.
Aristotle was already asking about it 2,300 years ago.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges, and the
explanation carries a BEST GUESS stamp on screen from start to finish.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stepping from the dark into bright sunlight makes some people sneeze (the photic sneeze reflex) | established | Langer, Beeli & Jäncke 2010, PLoS ONE 5(2): e9208; Wikipedia, "Photic sneeze reflex" |
| "About one in four people" | established as a range: 18–35 % in the reviews (24 % of Swedish blood donors; "almost a fourth of people" in The Scientist) | Breitenbach et al. 1993, Mil Med 158: 806–9 (18–35 %); Langer et al. 2010 (24 %); The Scientist, "ACHOO syndrome: a strange reflex that causes sneezing in the sun" |
| "It's not a cold": it is a reflex, set off by the sudden change from dark to bright, not by any one colour of light | established | Breitenbach et al. 1993 ("not... mediated by specific wavelengths of light, but rather by the change in light intensity"); The Scientist (light about 10 times brighter at a sneeze, from one person's light logger) |
| "Scientists think it's crossed wires": the eye's nerve runs close to the trigeminal nerve (which feels irritation in the nose), and a sudden flood of light spills from one into the other, so the brain reads it as something in the nose | leading idea, unproven (said as "Scientists think", stamped BEST GUESS). A rival idea: the body's rest-and-digest wiring over-reacts as a whole. A 2010 EEG study of 10 sneezers found a more excitable visual cortex, so the brain itself may be involved, not just a brainstem reflex | Scientific American, "Looking at the sun can trigger a sneeze"; Langer et al. 2010; Everett 1964, Neurology 14: 483–90 (the "photic sneeze effect") |
| "The nerve that guards your nose": the trigeminal nerve senses irritants in the nasal lining and triggers the sneeze | established | Scientific American (as above); Langer et al. 2010 |
| "It runs in families" | established (family studies describe it as dominant; a 2010 genome study of 5,390 people found two linked DNA variants, so it is probably not one gene) | Collie et al. 1978; Peroutka & Peroutka 1984, N Engl J Med 310: 599–600; Eriksson et al. 2010, PLoS Genet 6(6): e1000993 |
| "Its real medical name: ACHOO syndrome... doctors did that on purpose" (Autosomal dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst), and the stamp "REAL. SINCE 1978." | established: a deliberately contrived acronym from a 1978 paper by four doctors who were sun sneezers themselves | Collie WR, Pagon RA, Hall JG, Shokeir MHK 1978, "ACHOO syndrome", Birth Defects Orig Artic Ser 14(6B): 361–3; NIH Medical Genetics Summaries, "ACHOO syndrome" (NBK109193) |
| "What helps? Sunglasses." | common advice (they soften the jump in light, which is the trigger); not tested in a trial, so the line says "helps", not "cures" | Wikipedia, "Photic sneeze reflex" (management); Breitenbach et al. 1993 (the trigger is the change in intensity) |
| On the chart: "photic sneeze reflex" is the plain name | established | as above |

Not said, on purpose: that Aristotle blamed the sun's heat and Francis Bacon tested it with his eyes shut (true, and in
the description, but a second story); the fighter-pilot risk; "up to 43 sneezes in a row" (one case report).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). This Short uses no recorded one-shots: every sound but the
narration is synthesized.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
World files: `src/web/street.js` (the street outside the cinema, the sun, the hero lit for daylight, the sneeze as
acting, popcorn, sunglasses, the other cinema-goers, the cold germ) and `src/web/sneezehead.js` (his head in section:
eye, the two nerves, brainstem, the brain with a face and its big red button).
