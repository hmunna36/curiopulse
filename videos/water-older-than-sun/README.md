## Sound design

- **Beds:** a mountain breeze under 420 Hz on the trail; in the cloud a deep cold hum under 200 Hz with a faint glassy shimmer.
- **Hits:** glugs under the first words (all under 300 Hz); two glints on "the Sun"; a two-note ding in the pause after "The
  WATER."; a glass tink on the freeze; the spray (low burst + top hiss) under "leftovers" and the note's slap; a tape
  whirr for the rewind (motor + top chatter), the Sun going out (a low power-down), the suit's pop; a whoosh into the cloud;
  four clinks (top) as the grains drop in, an ice crack in the pause after "ice...", chattering teeth (top) and a brrr after
  "cloud."; a low boing as the molecule hops, a thud as it lands, a ratchet as the needle creeps, BOING + a sagging "uh-oh" in
  the pause after "HEAVY...", a sniff as it sucks its belly in, glints for the baby Sun, the dummy's squeak-squeak after "make.";
  a low gurgle as he fills, glints for the stars, a poof for the beard, a cork pop as it leaves, a glug at the loop.
- **Score:** a bouncy C–F–G groove on the trail (108 BPM), out for "The WATER." and from the freeze to the rewind; a held drone

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Up to half of Earth's (ocean) water formed as ice in the cold cloud before the Sun, so it is older than the Sun | leading idea (a model of the young Sun's disk; 30–50 %); said as "up to half" | Cleeves, Bergin, Alexander et al., "The ancient heritage of water ice in the solar system", *Science* 345 (2014); Scientific American, "Earth Has Water Older Than the Sun"; Nature News 2014 |
| The clue is heavy water: Earth's water holds more of the heavy kind of hydrogen than the young Sun's disk could make; only very cold chemistry (interstellar ice) makes that much | established measurement + the study's argument | the same paper; Carnegie Science release (EurekAlert, 25 Sep 2014: interstellar ice is rich in deuterium "because of the very low temperatures at which it forms") |
| The cloud was around −260 °C (on screen) | established (dense cold cores of molecular clouds are about 10 K) | standard astronomy (molecular cloud cores ~10–20 K) |
| The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old (the counter) | established | NASA solar system facts |
| You're mostly water (≈60 % on screen) | established (adults about 50–65 %, varying with body build) | USGS "The Water in You"; standard physiology texts |
| Bonus (comment, description): about 1 in 6,400 hydrogen atoms in seawater is the heavy kind | established (VSMOW D/H ≈ 155.76 ppm) | IAEA VSMOW reference water |

The words keep to the honest version: "up to half" (the model's range is 30–50 %, of the oceans), "too heavy for the baby Sun
to make" (the disk's chemistry could not produce Earth's deuterium share), and the button "bits of YOU" (not all of you).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
