## The plan (written before the script)

- **Spine:** you think each hand can keep its own beat, but two against three glues into one, because your hands share
  one timer that only does easy counts; so drummers cheat and turn both beats into ONE rhythm: "nice cup of tea".
- **Button (written first):** "Now you. Nice... cup of tea." It hands the viewer the trick and sends them back to the
  test, running into the hook when the Short loops.
- **Running device:** the kettle (set up: watches, its lid hopping the NICE-CUP-OF-TEA rhythm all along; escalate:
  boils over in the panic; reverse: whistles and pours on TEA).
- **Flash-forward:** at 4.0 s, in the gap after GO!, 0.5 s of the payoff (the kettle whistling WHEEE) in a round window.
- **Length:** tight arm, 25–30 s, 55–64 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: TIGHT, the 28 Nov 2026 11:30 IST slot). 58 words, 27.0 s.
- **Topic kind:** TEST · HAND (the 17 Nov mix: the first `[ ]` line of `topics.md`'s Queue, two-hand coordination;
  limp-fingertip's description and "Next up" promised it). Opening arm: none (hand tests are not in the opening test).
- **Hook formula:** The Direct Address, as the test ("Tap two with your left hand, three with your right. GO!"). The
  other two candidates: The Question ("Can your hands tap two different beats?": weaker, it asks before it shows), The
  Impossible Claim ("Your hands can't count to three. Try it.": a claim the viewer can't check before the instruction).
  The first sentence is the title's own test; the last three Shorts used The Direct Address twice and The Question once.
- **The viewer's test:** tap two even beats with the left hand while the right taps three in the same stretch, both
  starting together (3 against 2). Most people's hands slide into one shared beat.
- **Answer line (starts 4.48 s):** "Same beat? Your hands only have ONE drummer."
- **The 8–13 s rule:** 8.0 s "Left once, right twice? Easy." (the viewer taps 1 against 2: it works); 10.7 s "Two and
  three? He PANICS." (they try 2 against 3 again); 13.1 s "Speed up..." (they go faster and it collapses). The mechanism
  is one picture on his own head: one drummer, two strings, one drum.
- **Cover:** a dedicated `SC.cover`: his two hands on the pots (2, 3) from his eyes, the two beat rows, TAP 2 / AND 3?.
- **Caption question:** "Did your beats merge?"

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the campfire (a low rumble and crackle above 4.8 kHz), crickets far and high, a soft breeze.
- **The pots:** every hit of both hands from the timeline's hit lists: the big blue pot a low BONG (G3 partials), the
  small orange one a higher TONK (G4); on a word only the pot's low body and the slap's top are kept, so the narrator
  stays clear; in a pause the whole pot rings. The viewer hears the two beats glue into one on GO.
- **Hits:** the window's pop, the slam's low BOOM with a layered hitstop hit, the drummer's snare ruff, a frantic snare
  roll under "He PANICS" with a cymbal tumble and a bonk after it, steam and lid rattles while the kettle boils, a riser
  into the speed-up, a low bwomp as the drummer flops and a sad slide whistle after "beat.", the wink's ting, the card's
  flick, the glue's splorch and squeak, the rows' thump, a shimmer on ONE row, the kettle's whistle with a chord stab and
  the biggest layered hit on TEA, the pour.
- **Score:** the loop score, 12 bars at 106.8 BPM: a soft night pad under the test (no beat: the pots are the rhythm),
  a march in G with his pizzicato hook and the drummer's snare ruff, an E-minor drive for two-and-three, a deflated
  half-time section, a build into the glue, silence for the trick, a bright G resolution with steel pan for the payoff,
  and the night pad again at the end (as at the start, for the loop). Six dead stops (NOTES.md, rule 16).
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `qc_audio.py` before the render: 0 words under 10 dB.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7). Hedged lines are said as hedges; a myth is said as a myth.

| Claim | Status | Sources (two, independent) |
|---|---|---|
| Tapping two different rhythms at once, one per hand, is easy when they divide simply (2:1, 3:1) and hard when they don't (3:2) ("Left once, right twice? Easy." / "Two and three?") | established | Klapp, Hill, Tyler, Martin, Jagacinski & Jones, "On marching to two different drummers", J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 11, 814-827 (1985) · Deutsch, "The generation of two isochronous sequences in parallel", Perception & Psychophysics 34, 331-337 (1983) |
| Most people's hands slide into one shared beat on a first try | established as a tendency (no share of people measured for a first try: none said) | Klapp et al. 1985 · Summers, Todd & Kim 1993, Psychological Research (integrated, slow hand subordinate to the fast hand) |
| Speed up, and the hands fall back to an easier ratio ("Speed up, and he falls back to an EASY beat") | established | Peper, Beek & van Wieringen, "Frequency-induced phase transitions in bimanual tapping", Biological Cybernetics 73, 301-309 (1995) · Peper, Beek & van Wieringen, J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 21, 1117-1138 (1995); reviews of bimanual polyrhythms (transitions to 1:1 or lower-order ratios) |
| Skilled players manage it by folding both hands into one combined pattern ("So drummers cheat. They glue both into ONE rhythm.") | established as the common strategy (some experts may time the hands in parallel at fast tempos: Krampe et al. 2000) | Summers, Rosenbaum, Burns & Ford, "Production of polyrhythms", J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 19, 416-428 (1993) · Baylor University open textbook on rhythm, chapter 12, "Polyrhythms: two against three and three against two" |
| "Nice cup of tea" is the teaching phrase for 3 against 2: NICE on both hands, CUP and TEA on the three-beat hand, OF on the two-beat hand | established (music teaching) | Baylor open textbook, chapter 12 · Soundbrenner, "The 2:3 polyrhythm" / Musical U, "All about polyrhythm" |
| "Your hands only have ONE drummer" | a picture, not an anatomical claim; whether the brain times both hands with one clock or two is debated (said so in the description) | Summers et al. 1993 (one timer, a hierarchical model) · Krampe, Kliegl, Mayr, Engbert & Vorberg, "The fast and the slow of skilled bimanual rhythm production", J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 26, 206-233 (2000) (parallel timing in experts at speed) |

The topic line also suggested a bonus fact (tapping two rhythms to two beeps got easier when the beeps were closer in
pitch, Klapp et al. 1985). It was cut from the 25–30 s script (the tight rules drop the bonus fact); it is true per the
paper's abstract.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
World files borrowed: thumb-pinky-swap's `tp_world.js`, fist-edge-palm's `fp_world.js`, one-finger's `fist3d.js`,
bent-wrist-fist's `bwhand.js`, sign-seven's `s7_world.js`; new here: `tb_world.js` (the dusk camp, the table, the
pots, the kettle, the lantern, the mug, the fire, the tiny drummer, the window into his head, the beat lanes).
