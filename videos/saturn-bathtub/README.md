## Sound design

- **Beds:** water lapping in the tub (under 480 Hz, on a whole number of cycles so the loop holds) and a deep space hum (under
  170 Hz) for the front shots; under water for the split-level shots (a muffled rumble and bubble clicks above 5 kHz).
- **Hits:** each gag in the pause after its word: SPLASH after "Sink...", the rings' slap after "FLOATS!", the duck's squeak
  after "duck.", the cups' clink, POOF after "rock.", Earth's slide whistle and CLUNK, Jupiter's splash and sad bloop, the
  record scratch before "Catch?", the core's CLUNK (low + top only, where it meets "the gas"), the squeak after "rings?", and
  ba-dum tss after "RING.". What lands on a word is only low (under 300 Hz) or only top (above 5 kHz): the pop-up on FLOATS,
  the balance's boing on LIGHTER, the scan, the fizz, the five plinks, the foam's rush.
- **Score:** a bouncy bath-time groove (F Dm Bb C, 108 BPM); a suspense drone while Saturn is under; a hit with a cymbal on
  FLOATS; a wonder chord on the pull back; an underwater groove (Am F C G, 96 BPM) for the planets; a tension drone for the
  catch; the groove again into the loop. It drops out for every punchline (Sink, rubber duck, LIGHTER, rock, GAS, Catch, the
  CLUNK and the eruption, "it leaves a RING").
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.
  The gags measured in their gaps at −17…−23 dB against the voice's −16.5.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Saturn's average density is about 0.687 g/cm³; water's is 1 g/cm³ (so cup for cup Saturn is about 30% lighter) | established | NASA NSSDC Planetary Fact Sheet (687 kg/m³); BBC Sky at Night Magazine, "Saturn could float on water" |
| Saturn "could float in a bathtub if such a colossal thing existed" | established (NASA's own thought experiment, a density comparison) | NASA Science, Saturn facts |
| Saturn is the only planet in our solar system whose average density is less than water's (Earth 5.51, Jupiter 1.33 sink) | established | NASA Science, Saturn facts; NSSDC fact sheet; Caltech IPAC Cool Cosmos, "Can Saturn really float on water?" |
| Saturn is mostly hydrogen and helium, "the two lightest elements in the Universe" (not rock) | established | NASA Science, Saturn facts; BBC Sky at Night |
| A bath for it would be about 120,000 km wide (Saturn is 120,536 km across, about 9 Earths) | established | NSSDC fact sheet; NASA ("9 times wider than Earth") |
| In a real ocean it would probably fall apart: the gas would rise into a new atmosphere on the water, the dense core of metals and rock would sink | leading idea (a thought experiment; said with "probably", PROBABLY on screen) | IFLScience, "Is NASA's claim that Saturn could float on water really true?"; EarthSky ("it's also been argued that Saturn would break apart and its core would sink") |
| Its rings are made of chunks of ice (with some rock and dust); ice floats on water | established | NASA Science, Saturn facts ("billions of small chunks of ice and rock") |
| Bonus (comment): a day is about 10.7 hours; it is about 10% wider at the equator than pole to pole | established | NSSDC fact sheet (10.7 h; equatorial 120,536 km, polar 108,728 km) |

The cartoon liberties: the rings ride on the water's surface (they would orbit at Saturn's equator, a quarter of its radius
under the surface at 69% submerged); the sizes of the planets against each other are true (Earth a marble, Jupiter 1.19
Saturns across), their size against the hero is not.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
World files: `src/web/sb_space.js` (space, the Sun, Saturn and its rings, Jupiter, the small worlds, all painted once) and
`src/web/sb_bath.js` (the tub in space, the water as an image over him, foam, the duck, Saturn afloat, the cups, the brush,
the split-level set).
