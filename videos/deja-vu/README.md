**Title:** Why Do We Get DÉJÀ VU? 🌀

**Description:**
You open a strange door... and you've BEEN here before.

Déjà vu is not a memory, and it is not a glimpse of the future. The leading idea: a quick "that's familiar!" signal in
your brain goes off by mistake (one trigger shown in the lab: a new room laid out just like one you have forgotten),
your memory finds no record of the place, and the front of your brain spots that the two can't both be true. That clash
is the eerie feeling: your brain catching its own mistake. Subscribe for a new strange question every day.

It can even feel like you know what happens next. When researchers tested that in virtual reality, people felt sure...
and did no better than chance. About two in three people get déjà vu now and then, most often between about 15 and 25.
(If it happens very often, or with blackouts or strange smells, tell a doctor.)

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the empty café (a hush and the machine's hum), the inside of his head (a muffled slow pulse and tiny high
  blips), the lab (mains hum), the tent (heavy cloth and wind chimes).
- **The feeling** is two glassy notes a semitone apart that beat against each other. They are in the speech band, so
  they only ever play in a pause: after "before." (3.5 s), after "clash?" with a heartbeat, and after the last
  "before?", on the way into the ball.
- **Under the words:** what happens ON a word is a low thud on the bed bus, under the speech band: the drop on "BEEN",
  both hands on the bell on "SEEN", the stamps on "Never" and inside "IMPOSSIBLE", the ball's verdict on "not".
- **In the pauses:** the door's bell on frame 1 (high-passed over the first words), his swallow after "Relax.", the torch's
  click, BUSTED, the breath in and the wind-up before SEEN IT, the bell's DING after it, the "?" after "forgot.", the
  empty folder's pop and the moth, the womp after "nope.", NO RECORD after "here.", a ding for the green slip and a
  bzzt for the red one, the alarm after "IMPOSSIBLE.", phew, the subscribe click, the cat's meow after the click and
  the very same meow after "weirder.", the ting after "NEXT.", the buzzer after "SURE...", the coin's ching after
  "flip.", his "ommm" after "So no...", a bzzt and the turban's slide after "psychic.", the picture coming through
  after "Wait...".
- **Score:** a new-café strut that stops when he stops walking, so the feeling arrives in a hush; a bouncy groove for
  the eager one, dropped for the breath in, the shout and the DING; a slow curious groove under the outlines and one

  tent; nothing under the last line.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The hook: déjà vu is the feeling that something is familiar together with knowing that it is new | established (that is its definition; about two thirds of people report it, most often between about 15 and 25, less with age: used in the description only) | Brown, "A review of the déjà vu experience", Psychological Bulletin 2003;129(3):394–413; Teale & O'Connor, "What is déjà vu?", Frontiers for Young Minds 2015;3:1 |
| "Your brain just caught itself lying" / "one eager part of your brain yells: SEEN IT!" / "your memory checks the files... never been here" / "the front of your brain steps in: familiar... but IMPOSSIBLE" / "That creepy clash? THAT'S déjà vu" | leading idea, said as "Scientists think". Feeling that something is familiar and recalling where you met it are separate memory processes; in déjà vu a false signal of familiarity meets the knowledge that the place is new, and the brain's conflict-checking regions at the front flag it. In a scanner, a lab version of déjà vu (21 volunteers) lit up conflict-monitoring regions (anterior cingulate, medial prefrontal and parietal cortex) rather than the memory regions. In epilepsy patients, stimulating the rhinal cortex (the familiarity system, next to the hippocampus) produces déjà vu | Urquhart, Sivakumaran, Macfarlane & O'Connor, "fMRI evidence supporting the role of memory conflict in the déjà vu experience", Memory 2021;29(7):921–932 (first online 2018; accepted manuscript at research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/18521); Bartolomei et al., "Cortical stimulation study of the role of rhinal cortex in déjà vu and reminiscence of memories", Neurology 2004;63(5):858–864 |
| "Maybe because this room is laid out just like one you forgot" | one supported trigger, said as "Maybe". In virtual reality (rooms built in The Sims 2), people reported déjà vu most often in a new scene whose objects were arranged like an earlier scene that they could not call to mind. Other triggers are possible; the theories do not exclude each other | Cleary et al., "Familiarity from the configuration of objects in 3-dimensional space and its relation to déjà vu: a virtual reality investigation", Consciousness and Cognition 2012;21(2):969–975; Scientific American, "Been There, Done That—or Did I? Déjà Vu Found to Originate in Similar Scenes" (2012) |
| "It even feels like you know what happens NEXT. In the lab, people felt SURE... and guessed like a coin flip" | established in the lab. During déjà vu people felt more strongly that they knew which way the next turn would go, and chose it no better than chance (two ways to turn: a coin flip) | Cleary & Claxton, "Déjà Vu: An Illusion of Prediction", Psychological Science 2018;29(4):635–644 (doi 10.1177/0956797617743018); Colorado State University, "Déjà vu and feelings of prediction: They're just feelings" (1 Mar 2018) |
| "So no... you're not psychic" | follows from the line above | — |
| The grandma's kitchen, the three workers, the cat that walks past twice | pictures and gags, not claims. The workers stand for the familiarity signal, recollection and conflict monitoring | — |
| Description only: very frequent déjà vu can be a symptom (it is a classic aura of temporal lobe seizures) | established | Bartolomei et al. 2004; Brown 2003 |

Not used: jamais vu (a familiar word turning strange when you repeat it: an Ig Nobel prize in 2023), why déjà vu fades
with age (one idea: the checking gets lazier), and the people who have it all day: each is another day's fact.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). No recorded one-shots are used in this Short.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
The cover is `SC.cover` in `src/web/scenes_end.js`, rendered from a one-shot timeline (see `src/cover.sh`).
