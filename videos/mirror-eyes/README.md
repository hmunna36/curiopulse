# Why Can't You See Your Own EYES Move in a Mirror? 👀 — Short (31 s)

**Final file:** [`mirror-eyes-short.mp4`](mirror-eyes-short.mp4)
MP4 · H.264 High · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 31.0 s · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`mirror-eyes.srt`](mirror-eyes.srt). Cover: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg).

The hiker in blue pyjamas at his bathroom mirror (the camera is the mirror: tongue-nose's `mirror.js`, borrowed from
blocked-nostril), his eyes drawn again, bigger, over the rig's (`eyes.js`): in the mirror they never travel, they are just
somewhere else from one frame to the next. Inside his head, an editing room (`edRoom`): a film strip of his own eyes on a
light table and his brain (blind-spot's `erBrain`, in `brain.js`) in a green editor's visor with a pair of scissors in each
hand. Then his own view of his phone's selfie camera, the strip again with the hole patched, a wall clock that pops up in
the bathroom, and the window on his forehead once more. Jessica narrates, word-pop captions, the house bloom.
Cinematic lighting (`publish.json` "look": "cine"): the morning bathroom lit from its window on the left, the editing room
from a desk lamp up left with the light table's cool rim, the phone in daylight (`setLights` in `src/web/scenes.js`).

## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** short arm, 30–35 s, 66–76 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: the 25 Oct 2026 11:30 IST slot). 74 words.
2. **The viewer's test:** in a mirror, look from your left eye to your right eye: you never see them move (anyone
   who watches you can). The second, on-screen test: snap your eyes to a ticking clock that pops up on the screen and
   the first second seems to hang. Hook formula: **The Direct Address** ("Look at your eyes in a mirror. Left, right.
   See them move?": an instruction to the viewer, then the question); candidates: The Impossible Claim ("You've never
   seen your own eyes move.": strong, but the instruction comes later and the answer can't be "Nope"), The Question
   ("Can you watch your own eyes move?": the second Question in a row after tongue-nose, and vaguer). Last three:
   voice-deeper, circle-line (Direct Address), tongue-nose (Question): no formula three times in a row.
3. **Why this topic tonight:** the first line of the Queue, and circle-line's Reel caption (27 Oct 18:30 IST) already
   promises it as "the next one". No new hit in the last 3 days (wrist tendon 11.2K is under five times the middle).
4. **Hook sentence:** "Look at your eyes in a mirror. Left, right. See them move?" (0.11–4.25 s; on frame 0 he is
   leaning into the bathroom mirror, close, his eyes on his own left eye; on "Left," and "right." his pupils JUMP: from
   one frame to the next, with nothing in between, the way he sees them).
   **Answer (starts 4.83 s):** "Nope. Your brain cuts out every jump, like a sneaky film editor." (a window opens on
   his forehead: his brain in an editor's visor at a film strip of his own eyes, scissors in hand).
   **Button:** "It does this three times a second. Not convinced?" → the loop: "Look at your eyes in a mirror."
5. **Beats:** the test (0–4.3) → nope; the editor (4.8–9.4) → mid-jump it's a blurry smear, snip (9.7–14.1, the film
   strip: smeared frames cut out) → your selfie camera runs late, so it shows the jump after the snip (14.5–18.7, the

6. **Every mechanism line points back to the test:** the film strip is HIS face in the mirror; the selfie camera is

7. **The caption's one-word question:** **"Did you catch them move? Yes or no?"**
8. **Ending:** no pill; on "Not convinced?" he squints at us, then leans back into the mirror; the last 0.45 s is the

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the bathroom's room tone (under 230 Hz); in the editing room an old projector's motor under 380 Hz, and its
  clatter above 5.2 kHz only while the film runs; the same motor in the button's window.
- **Hits:** a tiny dry tick above 5 kHz on every jump of his eyes in the mirror (no swish: they don't travel); "move?"
  hm?; "Nope." nuh-uh; the window opening (top-only swish and a glint); a top-only snip at each jump inside the window;
  the film winding to a stop on "Mid-jump"; "smear..." a wobbly woo; snip after "so,"; SNIP (soft-clipped so it has body)
  and a second little bite after "SNIP.", then the frames drop; the stopwatch ticking above 5.2 kHz; the late slide as a
  run of glints; "snip." ting!; the patch: two low stamps; "hole." ting; the clock popping up; the three wall-clock ticks
  (the viewer's test); "right?" a nervous squeak; three snips a second under the button; "convinced?" hmm?; a swish
  into the loop.
- **Score:** a bouncy pizzicato groove at 112 BPM (F, Dm) under the hook, Bb–C under the window, a sneaky drive under
  the blur, a lighter groove for the phone, a held curious chord for the patch, and the hook's groove again from "It
  does this" into the loop. It drops out for "Nope.", for "so, SNIP." and the falling frames, for the ting after "snip.",
  and for the whole clock test: the ticks play alone over the room tone.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. The joke sounds measure −19 to −24 dB in their gaps (`src/qc_gags.py`), the narrator about −16.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You can't see your own eyes move in a mirror; someone watching you can | established (first described by Erdmann and Dodge, 1898) | Wikipedia, "Saccadic masking"; michaelbach.de/ot/lum-saccadicSuppression |
| Vision is suppressed around each quick eye jump (saccade), so the smear isn't seen; it starts just before the jump | established (how complete it is is debated: some vision continues, Mathôt et al. 2015, PeerJ 3:e1150) | Vallines & Greenlee 2006 (via Wikipedia); Idrees et al. 2020, Nature Communications 11:1977 |
| A phone's selfie camera shows the picture a little late, so you see the jump after the suppression is over | established for most phones (a very fast camera can be too quick to show it) | michaelbach.de (saccadic suppression page); cogsci.nl, "Can you see while your eyes move?" |
| The brain stretches the new picture back over the gap: the first second on a clock you glance at seems to last too long (stopped-clock illusion) | the illusion is established; the "backdating" explanation is the leading idea (said as "probably") | Yarrow, Haggard, Heal, Brown & Rothwell 2001, Nature 414:302–305; Melcher et al. 2020, Scientific Reports |
| The eyes jump about three times a second | established (3–4 a second while looking around) | Rayner 1998, Psychological Bulletin 124:372 (fixations ~200–300 ms) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
