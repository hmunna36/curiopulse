**Title:** Why Does Your Finger JUMP When You Close One Eye? 👁️

**Description:**
Point at something across the room, then shut one eye, then the other. With one of them, your finger jumps. Each eye
sees your finger from a slightly different spot, so when the two pictures disagree your brain aims with just one of
them: your dominant eye, the boss. Shut the boss and you get the other eye's picture, and the finger jumps. Which eye is
your boss? Left or right?

About two in three people have a right-eye boss, and it doesn't always match the hand you write with (roughly a third
of right-handers are left-eyed). The other eye isn't idle: you need both for depth.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Point at a distant object with both eyes open, close each eye in turn: with one eye the finger stays on the target, with the other it jumps sideways; the eye that keeps it there is the dominant (sighting) eye | established (the Porta test, described by Giambattista della Porta in 1593) | Wikipedia, "Ocular dominance" (the Porta test, and the studies it cites for it: Roth, Lora & Heilman 2002); Porta, *De refractione*, 1593 |
| Each eye sees a near finger in a different spot against the far target (the eyes are about 6 cm apart) | established (binocular disparity, parallax) | any vision text; Wikipedia, "Binocular disparity" |
| When the two pictures disagree, you aim with one eye's picture: the dominant one | established for sighting tasks (simplified: the brain sees two fingers and lines up the dominant eye's) | Mapp, Ono & Barbeito 2003, "What does the dominant eye dominate? A brief and somewhat contentious review", Perception & Psychophysics 65:310–317 |
| Shut the dominant eye and you get the other eye's picture, so the finger jumps | established | as above |
| About two in three people are right-eye dominant | established (about 63–70 % depending on the study and the test) | Bourassa, McManus & Bryden 1996, Laterality 1:5–34 (36.5 % left-eyed across 54,087 people); Ehrenstein et al. 2005, Graefe's Arch Clin Exp Ophthalmol 243:926–932 |
| It doesn't always match the hand you write with | established (in the meta-analysis about 34 % of right-handers and 57 % of left-handers are left-eyed) | Bourassa, McManus & Bryden 1996 |
| The other eye still works on depth | established (stereopsis needs both eyes' pictures; the dominant eye's lead is mostly about aiming) | Mapp, Ono & Barbeito 2003; Wikipedia, "Stereopsis" |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). `src/cover.sh` renders `SC.cover` into `cover.jpg`.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.

World files: `src/web/hill.js` (the hill at night, the moon, the hero from the front with the crown and the INTERN
badge), `src/web/pov.js` (his own view: the sky, the moon, his hand from behind, the eye icons, the blink, JUMP!),
`src/web/brainroom.js` (the control room, the two screens with a roller shutter and a reticle, the brain; from
pins-needles' `brainy.js`). The wink (`shutL` / `shutR` on any face) and the pointing hand (`'point'`) are in the
template's `character.js`.
