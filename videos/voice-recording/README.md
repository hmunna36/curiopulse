**Title:** Why Does Your Voice Sound WEIRD on Recordings? 🎙️

**Description:**
You hit play on your own voice message and a stranger comes out. Bad news: that's you.

When you talk, you hear yourself two ways at once: through the air, and through your own head. The sound that travels
through your skull and tissues comes across deeper and fuller, so the voice in your head has more body than the one in
the room. A microphone only picks up the part that travels through the air, and that is the voice everyone else has
always heard. Try it: plug your ears and hum. The boom you hear is the part that travels through your head. Subscribe
for a new strange question every day.

The twist: in a 2013 study, 80 people rated recorded voices without knowing their own was in the mix, and they rated
theirs higher than other listeners did. So maybe you don't hate your voice. You just hate knowing it's you.

## Sound design

Everything is synthesized and cue-locked to `timeline.json`; the only recording is the narration.
- **The demonstrations:**
  - the voice out of his phone is `gibber(thin=True)`: formant gibberish with no body (what a microphone keeps), on
    the same syllable clock as the picture; quiet under her words, loud in the pauses;
  - from "deeper" to "trailer." the narrator's own take goes through `deep_voice` (mixlib's new `voice_fx` hook):
    more low-mid body (140–460 Hz), less top (above 2.3 kHz), a close little room; it snaps back on "But a microphone".
    It is an illustration, not a measurement;
  - his hum (`hum_voice`) is thin while we hear it across the room, and turns into a throbbing boom on "That boom?".
- **Beds:** the living room's hush; a muffled slow pulse inside the head; wind on the trailer's ridge; the bar's murmur
  under karaoke (it stops with the record); almost nothing in the listening booth.
- **Hits (in the pauses):** the tap on PLAY; a sting on "WHO"; a sad blat after "That's you."; two ticks after "TWICE";
  a knock on the skull after "SKULL."; a low swell after "deeper."; the braam and a DUN for the trailer; mic taps, a
  buzzer for SKULL ✗; the record scratch and a cricket; plup-plup for the fingers; a WHUMP as we go inside his head;
  star ticks, a ta-da, a music box for the hearts; "mm-hm"; a heart pop; a small hic, the click and the bell, the big
  HIC, a slide whistle up and a whoosh down for the phone.
- **Score:** a sneaky walk for the hook (it stops for the phone's solo), the bouncy mechanism groove in D minor (it
  stops for the knock), a low held note under her deep voice, a choir for the trailer, a light walk for the
  microphone, a karaoke backing track (an original little tune in C; the scratch kills it), nothing for the hum, a
  curious walk for the study (the music box takes over), a warm chord for the button, a strut under the tease (the
  hiccup cuts it off).
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.
  `src/qc_gags.py` prints every gag's level in its own window.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| When you talk you hear yourself by two routes at once: through the air (out of the mouth, into the ear canals) and through your head (vibrations from the voice box and mouth travel through bone and tissue to the inner ear) | established | Hullar 2009; Reinfeldt et al. 2010; Muceli et al. 2025 |
| "The skull version sounds deeper" | the standard explanation, simplified | Hullar: the head's mechanics "enhance its deeper, lower-frequency vibrations"; Muceli et al. describe an isolated bone-conducted voice as somewhat deeper and less clear. The measurements are messier: the bone-conducted share is largest in the middle of the range (Pörschmann 2000: about 700–1,200 Hz; Reinfeldt et al. 2010: 1–2 kHz for most speech sounds), and Kimura & Yotsumoto 2018 found no single filter that turns a recording into "your" voice for everyone. So the Short says "deeper", shows a slower, fatter wave, and gives no numbers |
| "A microphone only gets the air part ... what everyone else has always heard" | established | Hullar 2009; Muceli et al. 2025 |
| "Plug your ears and hum. That boom? Your skull." | established: the occlusion effect. With the canal blocked, the low-frequency bone-conducted sound that normally leaks out of the open ear is trapped, so your own voice gets louder and boomier | Stenfelt & Reinfeldt 2007; audiology texts |
| "In one study, people rated recorded voices without knowing one was their OWN. They liked theirs MORE." | one study, said as one study (on screen: 1 STUDY · 80 PEOPLE) | Hughes & Harrison 2013: 80 people rated recordings of voices counting from one to ten; three samples of each rater's own voice were mixed in, and most did not recognise them. They rated their own voice as more attractive than other raters did, and higher than they rated other people's. The Short stages it as three voices; the study used many |
| "Maybe you don't hate your voice. You just hate knowing it's you." | a joke on that result, hedged with "Maybe" | The usual explanations of why a recording makes people cringe ("voice confrontation") are the mismatch with the voice you expect, because the head route is missing, and hearing cues you didn't know you were giving (Holzman & Rousey 1966) |

Left out on purpose: the often-quoted "only 38 % of people recognise their own recorded voice" (a 1967 study, as cited
on Wikipedia); I could not check the paper.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
