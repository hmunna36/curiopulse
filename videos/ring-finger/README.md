**Title:** Why Can't You Lift Your RING FINGER? 💍

**Description:**
Hand flat, fold your middle finger under, now lift your ring finger. Stuck? Your fingers are lifted by cords on the
back of your hand. Your pointer and your pinky each get a spare lifting muscle of their own; your ring finger only has
the shared one, and a little strap ties its cord to the middle finger's. Fold the middle finger down and the strap
holds the ring finger down with it. Could you lift it?

In the 1880s a surgeon in Philadelphia cut those straps for pianists, on purpose, to free the ring finger (the
operation was controversial, and it fell out of favour).

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fingers are straightened (lifted) by tendons that run over the back of the hand from muscles in the forearm | established | Extensor digitorum muscle, standard anatomy (Wikipedia: "Extensor digitorum muscle", and its references) |
| The index finger and the little finger each have an extra extensor of their own (extensor indicis, extensor digiti minimi); the middle and ring fingers have only the shared extensor digitorum | established | Wikipedia: "Extensor indicis muscle", "Extensor digiti minimi muscle"; scienceinsights.org, "Why can't I move my ring finger independently?" |
| Little bands (juncturae tendinum) tie the extensor tendons together on the back of the hand; a band between the middle and ring finger tendons was found in every hand examined | established | Folia Morphologica 72(3), 2013 (juncturae tendinum, cadaver study): journals.viamedica.pl/folia_morphologica/article/view/FM.2013.0040 |
| With the middle finger folded, the ring finger cannot be fully straightened: the band and the shared muscle hold it down | established (the posture was described by Forbes in 1884; how much each part contributes varies, and some people can lift it a little) | W. S. Forbes, 1884 (below); scienceinsights.org |
| In the 1880s a Philadelphia surgeon, William S. Forbes, divided those "accessory tendons" of the ring finger for musicians, on purpose | established (a primary source); the operation was controversial and fell out of favour | Forbes, "The liberating of the ring finger, in musicians, by dividing the accessory tendons of the extensor communis digitorum muscle", read 12 Nov 1884 (NLM: collections.nlm.nih.gov/catalog/nlm:nlmuid-101722021-bk); paskpiano.org, "Stretching exercises: benefits, limitations and dangers" |

Left out on purpose: the story that Robert Schumann ruined his hand with a gadget to free this finger (the finger is
disputed, and the cause is now usually thought to be focal dystonia), and the "2,500 operations" figure (second-hand).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set. `src/cover.sh` renders the cover.
