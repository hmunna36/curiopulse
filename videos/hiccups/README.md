**Title:** Why Do We Get HICCUPS? 🫢

**Description:**
You chug a soda on a first date... and HIC. Smooth.

A hiccup is your breathing muscle, the diaphragm, jerking all of a sudden. Air rushes in, and a split second later your
vocal cords snap shut on it: that is the "hic". A stomach swollen with fizz, a big meal or swallowed air is the usual
trigger, because it sits right under that muscle. Subscribe for a new strange question every day.

Why do we have the reflex at all? Nobody knows for sure. One idea, still unproven: it is left over from our amphibian
ancestors. Tadpoles pump water over their gills with the door to their lungs shut, a move that looks a lot like a
hiccup. Bonus fact: you were already hiccuping before you were born.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the restaurant (a hush and other tables far off, under the speech band), the inside of his body (a muffled
  slow pulse), the pond (a deep swell and bubbles).
- **The hiccups** are the star and each one has a pause of the narration to itself, with the score dead silent round
  it: after "and—" (with the glasses, the cutlery and the candle's pff), after "door.", after "tadpoles." (his and the
  tadpole's together, a fifth apart), after "shut." (the tadpole's) and after "tadpole." (a croak first, then a small one).
- **Under the words:** what happens ON a word is a low thud on the bed bus, under the speech band: the doors on
  "slammed" and "SHUT", the muscle on "JERKS". The air rushing in after the jerk is a hiss above the speech band that
  stops dead when the doors shut.
- **In the pauses:** the swallows of the first seconds (one per bob of his head; the first two pitched low so "You chug"
  stays clear), aah, the drop off his chin, the sign's ding, the breath's bonk, the last poke and the muscle's "hm!",
  the subscribe pop and click, the squelch, phew, poof.
- **Score:** a date-night strut that the first hiccup stops dead; one low chord in his throat, cut by the slam; a bouncy
  groove for the stomach; a rise into SHUT, cut by the doors; a light strut under the aside; a slow curious groove in
  the pond; the date-night strut again until "You're..."; silence for the punchline.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qa.py`: see the ship review.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A hiccup is a sudden, involuntary jerk of the diaphragm (and the muscles between the ribs) that pulls air in | established | Seifi & Fox, "What puts the 'Hic' into Hiccups?", BMJ Open Gastroenterology 2022;9:e000918 (PMC9237865); Cleveland Clinic, "Hiccups" |
| Within milliseconds (about 35 ms) the glottis, the gap between the vocal cords, snaps shut on the incoming air; that closure makes the "hic" | established. Seifi & Fox filmed it with X-ray video in 2022: it is the glottis that shuts, not the epiglottis | Seifi & Fox 2022; Cleveland Clinic; Wikipedia, "Hiccup" (the 35 ms figure, with its references) |
| "Fizz swells your stomach, poking your breathing muscle": fizzy drinks, eating fast and swallowed air are common triggers; a distended stomach sits right under the diaphragm and irritates it (and the nerves of the reflex) | established as triggers; the "poking" is the standard explanation, simplified. In Seifi & Fox's volunteer, hiccups were started on demand by drinking carbonated soda | Cleveland Clinic; Seifi & Fox 2022 |
| "Why?": what the reflex is for is not known | open question | Straus et al. 2003 ("their neuronal origin and physiological significance are still unresolved") |
| "One idea: we got it from tadpoles": the hiccup may be an old motor pattern from our amphibian ancestors, the gill ventilation of tadpoles | hypothesis. Said as "One idea", and stamped UNPROVEN on screen through both pond shots | Straus, Vasilakos, Wilson, Oshima, Zelter, Derenne, Similowski & Whitelaw, "A phylogenetic hypothesis for the origin of hiccough", BioEssays 2003;25:182–188 |
| "They gulp water for their gills... with that same door shut": to breathe water a tadpole fills its mouth, closes the glottis (so no water gets into the lungs) and forces the water out through the gills | established (it is the observation the hypothesis rests on) | Straus et al. 2003; summarized in Howes, "Hiccups: a new explanation for the mysterious reflex", BioEssays 2012;34:451–453 (PMC3504071), which also proposes a rival idea: a reflex that burps swallowed air out of suckling babies |
| "You're... part tadpole" | a joke on the hypothesis above (the button) | — |
| Description only: "you were already hiccuping before you were born" (from about nine weeks) | established | UCL News, 12 Nov 2019, on Whitehead et al., Clinical Neurophysiology 2019 |

Not used: Charles Osborne's 68 years of hiccups (Guinness World Records), the cures, the newborn brain-wave study, and
the burping-reflex hypothesis: each is another day's fact.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). No recorded one-shots are used in this Short.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
The cover is `SC.cover` in `src/web/scenes.js`, rendered from a one-shot timeline (see `src/cover.sh`).
