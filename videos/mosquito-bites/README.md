**Title:** Why Do Mosquitoes Bite YOU More? 🦟

**Description:**
It's probably not your "sweet blood". Mosquitoes follow the carbon dioxide in your breath from about 30 feet away,
then choose by smell. In a 2022 Rockefeller University study, the people mosquitoes loved most had far more oily
carboxylic acids on their skin. One volunteer was 100 times more attractive than another, and stayed that way for
years. Subscribe for a new strange question every day.

Scientists think those acids are the main lure, but the study shows a link, not final proof, and it tested one species
(Aedes aegypti). The cheese part is real too: in a 1990s experiment that later won an Ig Nobel Prize, malaria
mosquitoes went for Limburger cheese as much as for human feet. Garlic and vitamin B have been tested and do not keep
them away; a registered insect repellent does. Next up: why time flies as you get older.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the night air and the fire (a low roll under 230 Hz, crackle above 5 kHz), a few crickets; a hush for the
  skin close-ups; a hum and a little air for the lab.
- **The whine:** a mosquito's whine sits in the speech band, so the full sound (`mosquito_whine`, new in `sfxkit.py`)
  is only ever heard in a pause of the narration: frame 1 before "You", the cloud after "bites!", the guest flying in
  before "dinner", the take-off after "away.", the swarm after "more." Under words only its top (above 5.2 kHz) plays.
- **Hits:** the slap (`slap_hit`, new), short and on its frame; a buzzer for each crossed-out word; a service bell
  after "dinner."; a sonar ping after "breath"; two low notes after "suspect?"; a drum roll in the pause before "a
  HUNDRED"; a sad trombone after "years."; kisses after "cheese"; a boxing bell after "feet."; springs, knives and
  forks and a low blat on the button; one cricket in the stare.
- **Score:** a nervous groove for the first seconds, a held chord for the friend's peace, a sneaky line under "smells
  like", a hunt under the breath trail, a lab groove, a weigh-in groove for the scales. It drops out for every
  punchline: "It's not sweet blood", "dinner", the suspect, "WAY", "a HUNDRED", the stamp, "stink", the tie, the button.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qa.py`: see the ship review.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "It's not sweet blood." | The "sweet blood" idea has no support; the evidence for a blood-type effect is weak (one 2004 landing study, type O against type A) | Scientific American, "Some People Really Are Mosquito Magnets, and They're Stuck That Way" (Oct 2022); Shirai et al., *J. Med. Entomol.* 2004 |
| "Your skin just smells like... dinner." (the smell of the skin decides who is chosen) | Leading idea, strongly supported for one species | De Obaldia et al., "Differential mosquito attraction to humans is associated with skin-derived carboxylic acid levels", *Cell* 185 (2022) |
| "Mosquitoes track your breath from thirty feet away." | Established: carbon dioxide is the long-range cue. The distance is an estimate: up to about 30 feet (Riffell, University of Washington), 10–50 m in the model of the 2015 wind-tunnel study | van Breugel, Riffell, Fairhall & Dickinson, *Current Biology* 25 (2015); UW News, 16 July 2015; Gillies, *Bull. Entomol. Res.* 1980 (review) |
| "Then they sniff your skin." | Established: skin odour (with heat and moisture) guides the last part of the approach and the landing | Takken's and Gillies' reviews; McMeniman et al., *Cell* 2014 |
| "The leading suspect? Oily acids... and some people make WAY more." | Leading idea (said as "the leading suspect"): the people mosquitoes preferred had much higher levels of carboxylic acids in their skin oil. An association, in *Aedes aegypti* | De Obaldia et al. 2022; NIH Research Matters, 8 Nov 2022 |
| "In one study, one volunteer was a HUNDRED times more attractive than another... and stayed that way for years." | As reported: Subject 33 was "100 times more appealing than the least attractive, Subject 19"; the paper's abstract: the differences "were stable over several years" (a three-year study) | The Rockefeller University news release, "Why some people are mosquito magnets" (18 Oct 2022); De Obaldia et al. 2022 |
| "The same kind of acids make cheese stink." | Established chemistry: carboxylic (fatty) acids give smelly cheese and feet their odour, and they are what the attractive fraction of Limburger cheese is made of. "Same kind", not the same molecules: the ones raised on mosquito magnets are longer-chained members of the family | Knols et al., *Bull. Entomol. Res.* 87 (1997), 151–159 |
| "In one experiment, malaria mosquitoes loved stinky cheese as much as human feet." | As published, for *Anopheles gambiae*: "attracted equally to the smell of limburger cheese and to the smell of human feet" (the Ig Nobel Prize in biology, 2006) | Knols, "On human odour, malaria mosquitoes, and Limburger cheese", *The Lancet* 348 (1996), 1322; Improbable Research |
| Description only: garlic and vitamin B do not keep mosquitoes away | Tested and not supported | Rajan et al. 2005 (garlic); Ives & Paskewitz 2005 (vitamin B); C. Webb, University of Sydney (2018) |

On screen: the friend's zero bites, the counter and the 10 : 10 on the scales illustrate the lines; they are not data.
The mosquitoes of the two studies are different species (*Aedes aegypti* in 2022, *Anopheles gambiae* in 1996); the
script says "malaria mosquitoes" for the second, and the description names the first.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
