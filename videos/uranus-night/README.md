# One Night on URANUS Lasts 42 Years — Short (28 s)

**Final file:** [`uranus-night-short.mp4`](uranus-night-short.mp4)
MP4 · H.264 High · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 28.2 s · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`uranus-night.srt`](uranus-night.srt). Cover: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg).

The hiker, in purple pyjamas and a golden nightcap, floats in a hot-air balloon over Uranus's south pole at sunset and sets his twin-bell alarm clock (its split-flap face reads 8 HOURS); the planet itself lies asleep on its side on a pillow; an orbit diagram shows his pole turning away from the Sun for half of an 84-year lap; he falls asleep in 2007, the stars wheel and the years race, and the alarm finally rings at the sunrise of 2049: he is an old man with a beard down past the basket. Same narrator (Jessica), captions and bloom look as the channel's other Shorts.
Cinematic look (`"look": "cine"`): the pole at sunset is lit from the low Sun on the left (`flipLight(LIGHTS.sunset)`), the 42-year night by starlight with the lantern's warm rim (`LIGHTS.night`), the dawn of 2049 from the left again; space from the Sun on the left; the orbit shots use the diagram light (`setLights` in `src/web/scenes.js`).

**Direction record:** [`NOTES.md`](NOTES.md): the spine, the last line, and how this Short meets each of the 21 rules
of the direction sheet (the skill's `reference/direction.md`), written as it is built.

## Narration (57 words, performed)

Directed in [`src/script.txt`](src/script.txt), one take per block. The joke timing sits in the gaps between blocks.

> Your night lasts eight hours. On Uranus? Forty-two years.
> The whole planet is lying on its SIDE.
> So as it rolls around the Sun, your pole turns away for half the trip.
> One trip takes eighty-four years. Half of that... in the DARK.
> It's been night there since two thousand seven...
> and sunrise? Twenty forty-nine.
> *(the bell)* Morning... Grandpa.

| It goes round the Sun once every 84 Earth years | established | [NASA, Uranus: Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/facts/) · [Natural History Museum, Uranus fact file](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/factfile-uranus.html) |
| So each pole gets about 42 years of sunlight, then about 42 years of darkness (said for "your pole": NASA's own page speaks of a 21-year dark winter for half the planet, which is about the hemisphere, not the pole) | established | [Natural History Museum, Uranus fact file](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/factfile-uranus.html) · [StarDate (McDonald Observatory), Uranus](https://stardate.org/astro-guide/ssguide/uranus) · [The Planetary Society, 7 Dec 2007: the north pole sees the Sun for the first time in 42 years](https://www.planetary.org/articles/1253) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
