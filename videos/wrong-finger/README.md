## The plan (written before the code)

- **Spine:** you think you'll wiggle the finger you're looking at, but its twin on your other hand moves, because your
  brain picks a hand by the side it sees it on, and crossing your wrists swapped the sides.
- **Button (written first):** "Now try it on a friend." It sends the viewer back into the test as the one who points, and
  runs straight into the first line ("Cross your wrists, clasp, roll them in.").
- **Running device:** the raccoon across the table. Set up (3-5 s): it points, the twin pops, it smirks. Escalate
  (10-12 s): it points twice, fast, and falls back laughing. Reverse (18-22 s): it TOUCHES the finger, the right one pops
  and its jaw drops; in the button its own knotted paws fail the same way. Back as on frame 0 for the loop.
- **Hook formula:** The Direct Address, as the test itself (the last three Shorts: The Question, The Direct Address, The
  Direct Address): "Cross your wrists, clasp, roll them in. Now wiggle THIS finger!" It confirms the title (the finger
  that moves is the wrong one) and the artifact (the twin popping) is on screen at 4.5 s.
- **Length arm:** tight (25-30 s, 55-64 words): 63 words, 28.97 s. Topic kind TEST · HAND (the Queue's first line,
  promised by smooth-eyes' Next-up), opening arm NONE (a hand test), subscribe arm NONE.
- **The test:** cross the wrists, clasp, roll the hands in toward the chest and up; then wiggle the finger the raccoon
  points at. **Answer (starts at 4.93 s):** "Wrong one? You moved its twin... on the OTHER hand."
- **8-13 s:** the viewer keeps trying ("Try this one. And THIS one!" to 11.9 s), and the mechanism is one picture on
  their own hands: the hands light up orange (his right, on the LEFT) and blue (his left, on the right), a call runs up
  his left arm from the bottom left and crosses over to the right.
- **Flash-forward:** 6.93 s for 0.6 s, in a window over his face: the payoff's touched finger up with its green tick.
- **Cover:** the POV at the first pop: the raccoon pointing at a ringed finger, its twin up with a face; WIGGLE / THIS ONE?
- **Caption question:** "Did the right finger move?"

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a campfire rumble under 240 Hz with its crackle kept above 5.2 kHz, crickets now and then.
- **Hits:** the fingers lock (a low knock + a high tick on "clasp"), the roll (a top whoosh, a low whum), the paw's
  glints, the twins' cork pops and a boing in the pauses, the raccoon's snickers (after "one?", after "one!"), the
  flash-forward (whoosh in, thud out), OTHER HAND's layered hit, the x-ray hum, an old phone bell for the call (its edges
  under the line, a real ring in the pause), the X (a low thud), the paw's creeping steps (under 300 Hz), the touch (a
  low boop), the RIGHT finger (the biggest hit: layered hit, pop, chime, cymbal), the jaw drop (a low clunk), the skin's
  glints, his eyes' ticks, a deflating whistle after "guess.", the raccoon's own pop.
- **Score:** a sneaky night groove in D minor (Dm-Bb-Gm-A) on the loop grid: 14 bars at 116 BPM = 28.97 s, so the
  bar line falls on the loop; his test motif on the marimba from the dare to the mechanism, the raccoon's motif a low
  tiptoe pizzicato on its reaches; five dead stops (the twin, OTHER HAND, the laugh, full silence before the RIGHT
  finger, the guess); two marimba pickup notes into bar 1.
- **Mix:** voice first, effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from (direction sheet, rule 7).

| Claim | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| With the wrists crossed, the fingers clasped and the hands rolled in, people asked to move a finger someone points at often move the wrong one | established (a classic demonstration, studied since 1904) | Hong, Xu, Kang & Tong, "The hand-reversal illusion revisited", Frontiers in Integrative Neuroscience 6:83 (2012) · BrainFacts.org (Society for Neuroscience), "Point to Finger Illusion" (2021) |
| The finger that moves is often the matching finger of the OTHER hand (its "twin") | established in the literature as reported | Hong et al. 2012 ("likely to erroneously lift the matched finger of the other hand"), reporting Van Riper, "An experimental study of the Japanese illusion", Am. J. Psychol. 47:252-263 (1935); BrainFacts says only "the wrong finger". The originals were not read directly: noted in NOTES.md |
| When the finger is touched instead of pointed at, mistakes almost never happen | established | Hong et al. 2012 (replicated: one or two errors at most per person when the finger was tapped) · BrainFacts.org 2021 (the "touch it" version of the trick) |
| Why: the brain picks the hand by the side it sees the finger on, and the crossed wrists swapped the sides | the long-standing explanation (a conflict between what the eyes see and where the body feels the hands); debated: Hong et al. found the twisted hands stay slower even with eyes closed, so the posture itself adds to it. Said as the simple picture in the script, the nuance in the description | Hong et al. 2012 (reviewing Burnett 1904, Klein & Schilder 1929, Van Riper 1935) · BrainFacts.org 2021 ("your brain can get confused and sometimes mix up these signals") |
| It used to be called the "Japanese illusion" | established (paper titles of 1929 and 1935) | Klein & Schilder, "The Japanese illusion and the postural model of the body", J. Nerv. Ment. Dis. 70 (1929) · Van Riper 1935 (both cited in Hong et al. 2012). Description only, not spoken |

The orange and blue hands, the "call" up the arm and the skin's glow are pictures of the idea (the description says so).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
