## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** tight arm, 25–30 s, 55–64 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: the 15 Nov 2026 23:30 IST slot, a TEST slot, no
   pill). 61 words → 25.8 s.
2. **The viewer's test:** roll your tongue, then flip the tip up into a three-leaf clover. Most people can roll (83.7 %)
   and almost nobody gets the clover on the first try (14.7 %): the rules of 5 Nov (a face test most people can NOT do).
   It is the kin of the tongue roll and tongue-to-nose (24K views, 97 % viewed), promised by finger-tip-bend's "Next up".
3. **Hook formula: The Question, as the viewer's test** (after The Comparison, The Comparison and The Direct Address in
   the last three Shorts): "Can you make a tongue clover? Roll it, then flip the tip UP." It is the title, word for word.
   Candidates: The Direct Address ("Make a clover with your tongue. Roll it, flip the tip UP.": no "you" in the first
   eight words), The Statistic ("Only one in seven can do this with their tongue.": the number before the test leaks the
   answer). Three wordings auditioned with Jessica (hook A 4.4 s, B 5.0 s, C 6.6 s): A kept, re-taken at 3.4 s.
4. **Answer (starts at 4.50 s):** "Taco? Join the club. Only one in seven can." (the first laugh: the taco toppings land
   in his rolled tongue and a TACO CLUB card pops).
5. **Beats:** the test (0–3.4 s; the tip flips, the sides let go) → taco? join the club (4.5) → only one in seven can
   (6.3; a line-up of seven, one clover with a crown) → try again: tip up... sides flop? (8.0–11.4; the viewer is doing
   it again: the 8–13 s rule) → every tongue has the same muscles, the WIRING's different (11.8–15.0; YOU and the CLOVER
   CHAMP side by side in x-ray: the same three ways of muscle, then a tangle against three neat wires) → scientists
   suspect that's what makes a tongue a gymnast (15.3–18.4; a tongue in a leotard flips off the beam and lands a clover,
   10 10 10) → clover people can do most other tongue tricks too (18.8–21.2; roll, fold, twist, twist, clover) →
   SHOW-offs. (21.8, the sting) → Now flip the tip FIRST. (23.0–25.1, the button: back to the test a new way, and the
   loop). No science word spoken before 15 s ("muscles" at 13 s is everyday; "Scientists" at 15.3 s).
6. **The caption's one-word question:** "Taco, clover or flat?"
7. **Ending:** no pill; "Now flip the tip FIRST." and he flips the tip, tries the sides, and the camera eases back into
   frame 0's framing and state (the hook's state is valid at negative time).

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a bathroom room tone under 230 Hz and a little air above 6 kHz; the hall's murmur (low); the x-ray drone.
- **Hits:** the strain (a low wet creak under the words), BOING when the tip flips (in the pause after "UP."), the roll
  squelching back, the toppings sprinkling in (after "Taco?"), the card flap and the membership DING (after "club."),

  low squelch as the sides flop, a short "wah" (after "flop?"), the wiring's low buzz and high fizz, a drumroll under the

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Only about 1 in 7 people can make the cloverleaf tongue; 83.7 % can roll | established for that sample (374 Dutch adults, three tries each, a mirror allowed); the hardest of the five movements tested | Kappert KDR et al., "Five Specific Tongue Movements in a Healthy Population", *Dysphagia* 2021 (online 2020): rolling 83.7 %, cloverleaf 14.7 %, folding 27.5 %, twisting 36.1 % / 35.6 % (PMC8289788) |
| "Join the club": rolling without the clover is the most common result | established for that sample | Kappert 2020: "The group that can roll, but not a cloverleaf is by far the largest." |
| Every tongue has the same muscles, running three ways, and no bone | established | Kappert 2020: "The muscle topology of the tongue is overall the same among humans"; the intrinsic muscles run lengthways (superior and inferior longitudinal), across (transverse) and up and down (vertical): Sanders & Mu, *J Speech Lang Hear Res* 2013 (PMC3687025) |
| The wiring (the nerves) differs from person to person | established (innervation differs between individuals) | Kappert 2020, citing refs 19–20: "the innervation is known to differ between individuals" |
| Scientists suspect that's what makes a tongue a gymnast | hedged: a suggestion, not tested | Kappert 2020: the different innervation "might contribute to the individual versatility of this organ" (said "Scientists suspect") |
| Clover people can do most other tongue tricks too | established for that sample | Kappert 2020: those who can do the cloverleaf "are the best all-round", able to do on average more than 60 % of the other movements (97 % roll, 62 % fold, 65–66 % twist) |
| Tongue rolling is not a one-gene trait | context (not spoken: roll-tongue's Short said it) | McDonald, *Myths of Human Genetics* (udel.edu/~mcdonald/mythtongueroll.html): identical twins differ, children learn it |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.

World files: `src/web/mirror.js` (the mirror Shorts' bathroom rig, from mirror-eyes), `src/web/tongue.js` (roll-tongue's
tongue from the front, its slice and little faces) and the new `src/web/tc_world.js` (the apricot bathroom with the clover
pot; the tongue with the tip flipped up `tcFlap`, taco toppings `tcToppings`, the nerves `tcWires` (tangle / neat), the
three-leaf clover `tcClover` with an x-ray, a twist `tcTwistEdge`; `tcMini`; the tongue gymnast `tcGym`; the TONGUE CUP
hall `tcArena`; the TACO CLUB card `tcCard`; score cards `tcScore`; `tcLeafPath`).
