## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** short arm, 30–35 s, 66–76 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: the 24 Oct 2026 11:30 IST slot). 76 words → 31.8 s.
2. **The viewer's test:** with a finger of each hand, draw a circle in the air with the left and a straight up-and-down
   line with the right, at the same time: the line bends into an oval. Anyone can try it in three seconds (the phone
   goes in the lap). Hook formula: **The Direct Address, as a test** (the instruction is the first line; "your" is word
   five). Candidates: The Question ("Can you draw a circle and a line at the same time?": the question costs a second
   before the test), The Impossible Claim ("Your hands can't draw two shapes at once. Try a circle and a line.": two
   promises before the test), The Direct Address (kept). The last three Shorts used The Direct Address (voice-deeper),
   The Statistic (wiggle-ears) and The Direct Address (one-eyebrow): not three in a row.
3. **Why this topic tonight:** the first topic of the Queue, put there by the 00:00 run as the kin of the foot-switch
   hit (its Reel had 115K views on Instagram at the 23 Oct 00:10 reading, five times the middle and more): two body
   parts the brain won't run separately. The Vulcan-salute Short (68,858 views on YouTube at this run's reading, 20 Oct)
   is the same kind of test.
4. **Hook sentence:** "Draw a circle with your left hand and a straight line with your right." (0.10–3.77 s; the line
   starts to go round at 2.6 s and is an oval by 3.9 s, while the viewer draws their own).
   **Answer (starts 4.29 s):** "Bent, right? Your hands are copying each other's homework."
   **Button:** "Your hands aren't clumsy. They're cheating." → the loop: "Draw a circle with your left hand..."
5. **Beats:** the test (0–3.8) → copying each other's homework (4.3–7.7) → each hand has its own half of the brain
   (8.1–10.6) → the halves chat over a bridge and the circle leaks across (10.9–15.2) → proof: two patients with that
   bridge cut drew both shapes better than you (15.5–21.5) → weirder: people missing an arm still bend the line,
   circling a ghost hand (21.9–27.5) → not clumsy, cheating (28.1–30.8). No science word spoken ("halves", "bridge").
6. **The picture:** the night office as a mirror: his left hand on the screen's left, so "your left hand" reads on
   screen the way the viewer does it. Cyan = the circle and the half of the brain that runs it, orange = the line and
   its half, in the trails, the cords and the captions.
7. **The caption's one-word question:** **"Did your line bend? Yes or no?"**
8. **Ending:** no pill; on "cheating." the circle hand whispers PSST! to the line hand, the line eases back to straight
   and the last 0.4 s is the hook at negative time: both fingers drawing, the line straight, the tongue out.
9. **Cover:** `SC.cover`: him mid-test, the cyan circle and the bent orange line with the dashed straight one beside it,
   the words DID IT / BEND?

## Narration (76 words, performed)

Directed in [`src/script.txt`](src/script.txt), one take per block. The joke timing sits in the gaps between blocks.

> Draw a circle with your left hand and a straight line with your right.
> Bent, right? Your hands are copying each other's homework.
> Each hand gets its own half of your brain.
> But the halves chat over a bridge... and your circle leaks across.
> Proof? Two patients with that bridge cut drew both shapes... better than YOU.
> Weirder? People missing an arm still bend the line... circling a GHOST hand.
> Your hands aren't clumsy. They're cheating.

The hook went through three wordings: "...with one finger and a line with the other" (whisper heard "the line", and no
"your"), "...a straight line with the other" (no "your"), and the kept one, three takes of it (the liveliest pitch,
7.9 semitones, and 3.7 s). The payoff and the weird line were taken three times each, picked by their last words in
the speech band ("YOU.", "hand.").

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the office's room tone with a light's faint hum (low only); inside his head a warm, slow throb.
- **The test:** a soft swish above 5 kHz on every stroke of each hand (the circle on the left, the line on the right;
  it runs under the words) and a faint fizz for the glowing trails; the bend is a low thud under "line" and a wobbly
  brass dip in the pause before "Bent,"; a quick "huh?" slide after "right?"; the copy flies across as a fizz above
  5 kHz, COPIED! lands low on "copying", and a copier goes **beep beep** after "homework.".
- **Hits:** glints as the hands light up and as his head turns to glass, a low thud as the halves light, the cords'
  fizz, a whoosh into his head after "brain."; the chat bubbles pop (top only), a tiny voice after "bridge...", the
  bridge's planks land low, the circle's roll is a ratchet above 5 kHz, a low boing when it lands after "across.";
  a ding after "Proof?", a snip above 5 kHz on "cut", the bridge drops (low), two ticks for both shapes; the red X
  lands low on "YOU" and a wrong-answer **buzzer** follows; a spooky slide after "Weirder?", a puff (low and airy) as
  the arm goes, sparkles and a breathy whoosh on each of the ghost's circles, a wobble after "line...", a ghostly
  **oooo** after "hand."; a tick after "clumsy." and a **psst** after "cheating.". Every speech-band sound sits in a
  pause of its own (`src/qc_gags.py`: −19 to −24 dB in their gaps).
- **Score:** a bouncy pizzicato/marimba groove at 112 BPM (F, Bb) for the test, a sneaky Dm groove for the copying,
  a low held chord for the halves, a chatty groove and a sneaking groove for the bridge, a bright groove for the
  clean shapes, a high thin pad for the ghost, and the hook's groove again into the loop; it drops out for the bend
  and every punchline.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC (`levels`: music −14, sfx −13; music duck 0.74).

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing a circle with one hand and a straight line with the other at the same time is hard: the line bends toward an oval (spatial interference between the hands) | established | Franz, Zelaznik & McCabe, *Acta Psychologica* 77 (1991) 137–151 ("Spatial topological constraints in a bimanual task"); Franz et al. 1996 (below) describe the circle-and-line pairing as the standard example |
| "Each hand gets its own half of your brain": each hand's movements are run mainly by the opposite half (the picture crosses the cords in his neck) | established | any neuroscience textbook; NINDS "Brain basics" |
| "The halves chat over a bridge... and your circle leaks across": the two halves are joined by the corpus callosum, and the spatial interference between the hands depends on it | established for the split-brain result below; how the plans mix (cross-talk between the halves' motor plans, or the brain planning both hands as one shape) is still argued, and the Short shows only the cross-talk picture | Franz, Eliassen, Ivry & Gazzaniga, *Psychological Science* 7 (1996) 306–310: "spatial interference in movements results from callosal connections"; Diedrichsen et al., *J Neurophysiol* (2003); Mechsner et al., *Nature* 414 (2001) (the perceptual account) |
| "Proof? Two patients with that bridge cut drew both shapes... better than YOU." | established (two patients, one study): two callosotomy patients (J.W., V.P.; the bridge cut to treat epilepsy) drew pairs of different figures (lines, curves, circles, squares) at once; four independent judges rated the patients' drawings more accurate than the healthy controls' on 63 % of the trials (worse on 17 %). "Better than YOU" stands for "better than the healthy volunteers" | Franz et al. 1996 (above), Experiment 2 and "Generalizability of results" (full text: ivrylab.berkeley.edu/files/organized_pubs_pdfs/1996_franz_et_al_psychological.pdf) |
| "People missing an arm still bend the line... circling a GHOST hand": amputees who can move their phantom arm show the same coupling when the phantom draws one figure and the real hand another; patients asked to draw circles with a phantom limb while drawing vertical lines with the intact hand drew the lines oval | established in small studies; only people who feel they can MOVE the phantom show it (it is absent with a "paralysed" phantom): the line says "circling a GHOST hand", i.e. those who do | Franz & Ramachandran, *Nature Neuroscience* 1 (1998) 443–444; Ramachandran & Hirstein, *Brain* 121 (1998) 1603–1630 (describing the experiment); Osumi et al., *Neuroscience Letters* (2015), nine patients with a phantom limb after brachial plexus avulsion: "Drawing of circles by the phantom limb resulted in an oval transfiguration of the vertical lines" |
| Not in the script: the same pull shows up when people only IMAGINE drawing circles with one hand (Garbarini et al., *NeuroImage* 2014), against the 1998 report that imagining did not do it | debated (left out: the foot-switch Short used "just imagining it") | Garbarini et al., *NeuroImage* 88 (2014); Ramachandran & Hirstein 1998 |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The
JavaScript libraries the scenes use are vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
