**Title:** Can You SWAP Your Thumb and Pinky? 👍

**Description:**
Make two fists: your right thumb out, your left pinky out. Now swap them, and go FASTER. Ended up with two thumbs, or two pinkies? Your hands are copycats. Every finger has a twin on your other hand, and when one moves, its twin wants to move too. Kids' hands copy each other way more; it fades as you grow. And in some families, one faulty gene keeps it for life. Now try the swap slowly.

Did they swap?

The science: when two hands speed up an alternating move, they tend to snap into the mirror pattern, the same move on both sides, and not back (Kelso, American Journal of Physiology, 1984). Scientists think part of each hand's command spills over to the other side ("crosstalk"; Swinnen, Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 2002); another camp says the pull is toward what looks symmetrical (Mechsner et al., Nature, 2001). For this trick both predict the same result: matching fingers. Working one hand makes the same muscle in the other hand fire a little: clearly in young children, faintly in adults (Mayston et al., Annals of Neurology, 1999; Koerte et al., 2010: 236 people aged 3 to 96). These mirror movements mostly fade by about age 7 to 10 (GeneReviews; Beaulé et al., 2012). In congenital mirror movement disorder, one faulty copy of a gene such as DCC makes one hand involuntarily mirror the other hand's movements, from infancy and for life (Srour et al., Science, 2010; MedlinePlus Genetics); it is very rare. Nobody has measured how many people fail the swap on the first try.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
