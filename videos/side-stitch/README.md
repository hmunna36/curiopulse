**Title:** Why Do You Get a STITCH When You Run? 🏃

**Description:**
That stab under your ribs when you run is not a muscle cramp: when scientists wired up runners mid-stitch, the
muscles were calm. The best guess today is friction. Your belly has a slippery two-layer lining, one layer on the
wall and one around your organs; running bounces the organs, a full stomach presses the layers together, and the
outer layer is the one that feels sharp, pinpoint pain. Nobody has proven it yet. It is probably not your breathing
muscle either: people get stitches riding horses and camels. Subscribe for a new strange question every day.

What tends to help: slow down, bend forward, and go easy on big meals and sugary drinks before a run. Next up: what
really happens when you crack your knuckles.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

Main source: Morton D, Callister R. "Exercise-Related Transient Abdominal Pain (ETAP)". Sports Medicine 2015;45(1):
23–35 (open access, PMC4281377), a review of the studies named below.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A stitch is a sharp pain, usually at the side under the ribs | established | Morton & Callister 2000 (965 athletes); the 2015 review |
| It is not a muscle cramp | established (one study, clear result) | Morton & Callister 2008: muscle electrical activity at the sore spot was not raised during a stitch |
| "Best guess": the belly's lining is being irritated by friction between its two layers | leading idea, unproven (said as "best guess"; the review calls it speculative but the best fit) | Morton & Callister 2000; the 2015 review |
| The lining has two layers, one on the wall and one over the organs, with lubricating fluid between | established anatomy (parietal and visceral peritoneum, serous fluid) | the 2015 review; any anatomy text |
| Running bounces the organs; a full stomach presses the layers together | part of the leading idea; that food and drink before exercise provoke stitches is established | Morton et al. 2005 (848 fun-run participants); Plunkett & Hopkins 1999; Morton et al. 2004 |
| The outer layer feels sharp, well-localised pain | established | the 2015 review (the parietal layer has the body wall's pain nerves) |
| The pain eases quickly when you stop | established | Morton & Callister 2006; the 2015 review |
| It is not from breathing hard: people get stitches riding horses and camels | established observation; the "starved breathing muscle" idea is judged unlikely | the 2015 review: horse, camel and motorbike riding; lung function was normal during a stitch (Morton & Callister 2006) |
| Shakespeare wrote about it | established | The Tempest, Act 1, Scene 2: "thou shalt have cramps, / Side-stitches that shall pen thy breath up"; the review also cites Pliny the Elder |
| Science still isn't sure | established | the 2015 review: "the etiology of ETAP remains speculative" |
| Slow down, lean forward | commonly reported relief, not proven (the Short offers it as a tip, not a cure) | Capps 1941; Sinclair 1951; Plunkett & Hopkins 1999 |

Left out on purpose: the roughly 70 % of runners a year who get one (a number that only labels the thing), the
shoulder-tip pain, the other theories, and the advice about sugary drinks (it is in the description).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
