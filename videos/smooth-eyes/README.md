he tries a slow "film-star pan" with his eyes and they JUMP; a butterfly, then his own finger, give them something to

## Narration (60 words, performed)

Directed in [`src/script.txt`](src/script.txt), one take per block. The joke timing sits in the gaps between blocks.

> Can you move your eyes smoothly? Slide them across the room.
> Jumpy, right? Your eyes can't glide... without something to CHASE.
> Hold up a finger. Move it slowly... and chase it.
> Smooth! Your eyes hitch a ride on it.
> Even in pitch dark, they'll still ride your finger...
> because your brain FEELS it move.
> Now lose the finger. Try again.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a breezy meadow (wind under 340 Hz, a far cicada above 6 kHz), two far birds in pauses; crickets above 5 kHz
  in the dark; all of it out for the full silence before "FEELS".
- **Hits:** every eye jump is a tick (a glass tick above 5 kHz over a soft low knock, so it can sit on a word); every
  viewfinder jump a slide-projector clack; the glide is a smooth low hum under the words and a WHEEE slide whistle in the
  pause after "CHASE"; the hitstop's layered hit on the butterfly's pass; a pop as the finger comes up; the butterfly
  is a flutter (top only) and a music-box twinkle when it lands; a bright three-note chord after "Smooth!"; the tow lines
  zip out and clip on; a light switch into the dark and out of it; a shimmer for the ghost finger; a low thud for SORT OF;
  a deep BOOM under "FEELS" with a rising zing and sparkles as the brain lights; a BOOP and a bonk for the nose.
- **Score:** the loop score, 12 bars at 108.3 BPM (bar 1 comes round on frame 1): a jumpy pizzicato groove with snaps for
  the test, a legato groove from CHASE (the glide), a low drone and one music-box twinkle in the dark, a warm C chord
  under the payoff, the sunny groove back for the button, its last bar's G leading into bar 1's C. Dead stops at 4.07,
  9.09, 16.77, 20.95 (full silence) and 22.95 s.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. Each gag measured in its own gap after the mix: −19 to −23 dB against a narrator at about −15.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7). Hedged lines are said as hedges; a myth is said as a myth.

| Claim | Status | Sources (two, independent) |
|---|---|---|
| Without a moving target most people can't move their eyes smoothly: they make a series of quick jumps (saccades) instead | established | Purves et al., *Neuroscience*, 2nd ed. (2001), "Types of eye movements": only highly trained observers can make a smooth pursuit without a moving target, most people who try make a saccade (NCBI Bookshelf NBK10991) · Krauzlis, *The Neuroscientist* 11:124 (2005), via the Wikipedia article "Smooth pursuit": "Most people are unable to initiate pursuit without a moving visual signal"; Berryhill, Chiu & Hughes, *J Neurophysiol* 96:461 (2006): smooth pursuit's "initiation generally requires a moving visual target" |
| Following a moving finger, the eyes glide (smooth pursuit) | established (the standard bedside test) | Purves et al. 2001 · the clinical pursuit test with a moving finger (Oxford Neuroexam video 32, "Smooth pursuit"); Wikipedia "Smooth pursuit" |
| In total darkness the eyes can still glide after your own moving finger, because the brain senses the hand's movement (limited, weaker than with a visible target: SORT OF on screen) | established, hedged | Gauthier & Hofferer, *Exp Brain Res* 26:121 (1976): in total darkness smooth pursuit appears only when the imagined target is moved by the subject's hand, actively or passively · Berryhill et al. 2006: "limited smooth pursuit of the motion of a subject's own finger in total darkness", proprioceptive pursuit gains intermediate between visual and auditory |
| The tow lines, the brain lighting up and the ghost finger | pictures of the idea, not anatomy | (said so in the description) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
World files borrowed from one-eye (`src/web/trail.js`, `eyes.js`, `oneeye.js`; `oneeye.js`'s `oeIrisAt` takes `F.wide`
here so an iris can slide into the corner); this Short's own: `src/web/se_world.js`, `src/web/scenes.js`.
