## The plan (written before the build)

- **Why this topic:** the Queue's first line was SAD eyebrows, but the finger-W Reel became a hit on Instagram (153,926
  views by 16 Nov 2026, about 60 times the Reels around it), so its closest kin went first (rule 7 of 15 Oct, `story.md`
  §4): another "make your fingers do it" test that most people can't hold on the first try. SAD eyebrows stays next
  (the fold-arms Short's "Next up" already promises it, and so does this one).
- **Length:** tight arm (25–30 s, 55–64 words) from `yt.mjs next-slot`: 55 words, 26.7 s. No pill (none arm).
- **The test:** wag both index fingers side to side like windscreen wipers, the same way, then faster: at speed they
  snap into a mirror (both swinging in and out together). Two hands, two seconds, most people can't stop it.
- **Hook formula:** The Direct Address, the instruction itself: "Wag your fingers like wipers. Same way... now FASTER!"
  (confirms the title in its first four words). Candidates rejected: The Question ("Can you wag your fingers like
  wipers... fast?": slower to the flip) and The Impossible Claim ("Your fingers will flip on their own...": a claim
  before the test, 1.5 s later to the strange thing).
- **Answer (starts at 4.68 s):** "Flipped into a mirror? Your hands are clingy TWINS." (the metaphor; faces pop on the
  two fingertips and fall for each other).
- **Mechanism, one picture, the viewer's hands moving through 13 s:** "Slow down. One swings IN, the other OUT..." (the
  viewer re-does the test slowly; IN / OUT tags ride the fingertips) "...your brain's refereeing." (a window opens over
  his head: the brain in a striped shirt with a whistle, pointing the way each finger goes).
- **Payoff:** "Faster? The ref gives up... and the twins COPY." (the referee's arms flail, it goes dizzy and raises a
  white flag; the fingers snap into the mirror and high-five).
- **Weirdest true fact:** "Two PEOPLE swinging their legs side by side? They flip too!" (he and a gran on the van's back
  step under the awning swing their legs in turn, then fall into step, and look at each other).
- **Button (the loop):** "Now try to flip it BACK." (back in his view: the twins cheer in the mirror, he strains, and the
  fingers slip back into frame 1's slow wipers). It sends the viewer back into the test; the first line runs on from it.
- **Caption question:** "Did yours flip? Yes or no?"
- **Cover:** a frame of its own (`SC.cover`): his face between the two clingy twins and a heart, "LIKE WIPERS?".

## Sound design

- **Beds:** rain on the van's roof (a low drum under 380 Hz and its patter above 5.2 kHz), heavier outside with drips
  off the awning; the wiper motor (low).
- **Hits:** the wipers' thwump (low) and rubber chirp (high) at every turn, a tiny air swish each time a fingertip passes
  the middle (high); the flip's fwip and a small boing in the pause after "FASTER!"; pops as the twins' faces appear;
  a ding, sparkle and the hitstop's hit after "TWINS."; a slow-down slide; glints for the IN / OUT tags; the window's
  clunk; the referee's whistle (whole in the pauses, only its air under the words); a bwomp and a flag flap when it
  gives up; the snap into the mirror and the high-five clap with the layered hit after "COPY."; boot swishes, the

- **Score:** a bouncy marimba groove for the test that gives way to a fast one on "FASTER!", a sweet one for the twins,
  a sneaky half-time for "Slow down", the referee's march that speeds up on "Faster?", a bright groove after COPY, a

## Science notes

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wagging both index fingers side by side in the parallel ("wiper") pattern and speeding up, people switch by themselves into the mirror pattern at a critical speed | established | Kelso, "Phase transitions and critical behavior in human bimanual coordination", Am J Physiol 1984; Haken, Kelso & Bunz, Biol Cybern 1985 |
| The switch goes one way: the mirror pattern stays stable at speed, the parallel one is the one that breaks (hysteresis) | established | Kelso 1984; Kelso, Scholz & Schöner 1988; HKB model |
| One finger swings in while the other swings out in the wiper pattern; in the mirror pattern both move in and out together | established (geometry) | the patterns as defined in the studies above |
| "Your brain's refereeing": keeping the hands on opposite jobs takes more control (attention, effort) than mirroring | simplified (it is the less stable pattern and needs more control; the referee is a metaphor) | Carson & Kelso 2004 (review); Spencer & Ivry 2007 (PMC1904833) |
| Two people swinging their lower legs while watching each other show the same switch from alternating to in step as the pace rises | established | Schmidt, Carello & Turvey, J Exp Psychol HPP 1990 |
| Why the mirror wins (matching muscles, or how the move looks) is still argued | debated (in the description and the comment only) | Mechsner, Kerzel, Knoblich & Prinz, Nature 2001 |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set. `src/cover.sh` renders the cover.
