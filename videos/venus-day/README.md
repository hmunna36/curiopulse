**The second CURIOUS Short** (the topic test, 25 Oct–8 Nov 2026; `reference/story.md` §6; promised by lake-natron's "Next up"
comment). The hero goes there: it is his first birthday on Venus. He stands on the orange plain in a silver heat suit and a
glass helmet with a party hat on top, holding a cake whose "1" candle is a bulb (there is no oxygen to burn), next to a desk
calendar on a rock that still says MONDAY. Then he stands on top of the planet itself, Little Prince style, while it goes round
the Sun. Same narrator, captions and bloom look. The look is cinematic (`publish.json` "look": "cine"): an orange overcast
from above on the surface (`SURF_LIGHT`), one light in space, the Sun, whose direction is worked out per frame from where
the planet is (`sunLight`), and a dawn that rises behind him in the weird-fact shot (its rim light grows with it).

**Two worlds, built for the topic** (`src/web/venus.js`, `space.js`): Venus's surface as a far plate painted once (a sky
under a lid of cloud, thick cloud rolls, a soft brightening where the Sun is, broad shield volcanoes in the haze, one with a
vent that breathes red and a plume) over a 2.5D ground plane of basalt slabs (Venera 13 saw flat, layered plates), soil,
drifting dust, heat shimmer at the horizon and warm motes; and space: stars, a faint galaxy band, the Sun with a corona,
the dashed orbit, and Venus seen from above its pole (butterscotch clouds in a spiral round the polar vortex, a night side
away from the Sun, a lit limb), with him standing on it.

## Narration (68 words, performed)

Directed in [`src/script.txt`](src/script.txt), one take per block. The joke timing sits in the gaps between blocks.

> On Venus, one day lasts longer than your whole YEAR. / Venus is the laziest planet... it barely spins. / It laps the whole
> Sun in two hundred and twenty-five days... / but takes two hundred and forty-three to turn around ONCE. / So you'd be a
> year older... before you're a day older. / Oh, and it spins BACKWARDS... so the Sun comes up in the WEST. / Happy
> birthday. It's still... Monday.

"Day" here is one turn of the planet (the sidereal day), and the script says so ("to turn around ONCE"), so the claim is exact.
Sunrise to sunrise is shorter (about 117 Earth days, because the spin is backwards): that is the pinned comment's bonus, not a
line, so the Short keeps one idea.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** Venus: a slow, heavy wind and a far rumble, all under 300 Hz; space: a low hum of the Sun.
- **Hits:** a muffled breath against the glass (low + top only, under "On Venus"), the calendar's flaps (top only), a boing for
  the hat, a whoosh up through the cloud and a shimmer into space, a snore from the planet, his steps on it, a creak, a sweat
  drip; an odometer tick for every Earth day of the time-lapse (top only); a ding for 1 YEAR, his foot taps, a ding and a
  feeble party-horn toot for 1 DAY; the popper's pop, the page's rip and a bwomp; a CLINK on the glass, a record scratch for
  BACKWARDS, a soft "aah" for the dawn in the west; a music box playing the start of "Happy Birthday" (public domain) and a
  wah-wah after "Monday"; a breath in, into the loop.
- **Score (104 BPM):** a birthday bounce (C-F-G-C) for the hook, a riser through the cloud, a wonder drone in space, a walk for
  the log-roll, a faster drive for the time-lapse, a low waiting drone with a riser for the turn, the bounce again for the
  payoff, a lazy lounge for the deck chair, a dawn drone; it drops out for every punchline.

## Science notes

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A Venus day (one turn on its axis, the sidereal day) is 243 Earth days (243.0226 ± 0.0013, radar 2006–2020), longer than its year | established | NASA Science, "Venus Facts"; Margot et al., Nature Astronomy 2021 (UCLA release, 29 Apr 2021); Wikipedia "Venus" |
| A Venus year (one lap of the Sun) is 224.7 Earth days ("225") | established | NASA Science, "Venus Facts"; NASA NSSDC planetary fact sheet |
| Venus spins more slowly than any other planet (the next slowest, Mercury, turns in 58.6 days) | established | NASA NSSDC planetary fact sheet (rotation periods); Universe Today, "How fast does Venus rotate?" |
| It spins backwards (retrograde), so the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east | established | NASA Science, "Venus Facts"; Wikipedia "Venus" |
| Sunrise to sunrise (the solar day) is about 116.75 Earth days | established | Wikipedia "Venus"; NASA NSSDC fact sheet (length of day 2802 h) |
| The sky at the surface is orange-yellow; only about a tenth of the sunlight gets through; the clouds hide the Sun's disc | established | Wikipedia "Venus" |
| Its upper clouds circle the planet in about four Earth days | established (picture only: the cloud deck creeps) | Wikipedia "Venus"; NASA Science |
| Venera 13 photographed flat, layered plates of rock on the surface | established (picture only) | Venera 13 panoramas (1982) |
| Volcanoes on Venus may still be active (a vent that glows faintly) | leading idea (picture only, faint) | Herrick & Hensley, Science 2023 (Maat Mons vent changes) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
