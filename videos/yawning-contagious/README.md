## Narration (105 words, performed)

Directed in [`src/script.txt`](src/script.txt), one take per block. The joke timing sits in the gaps between blocks.
The "you" block is the hook's payoff: Jessica's "yoooou" turns into a real yawn (take C1 of five tried, a falling
pitch glide 276 → 152 Hz over 1.6 s, and whisper still hears "you").

> *[curious]* One person on the bus yawns... then the next... then the next...
> and then... *[yawning]* yoooou.
> *[deadpan]* Thanks, stranger.
> That's contagious yawning. About half of adults catch it.
> *[sighs]* Why? Honestly... nobody's sure.
> One idea: yawns cool your brain. A big gulp of air... a rush of fresh blood.
> *[excited]* People with a cold pack on their forehead caught WAY fewer yawns.
> *[curious]* Another idea: it's social. We copy the faces around us...
> *[chuckles]* some dogs even catch OURS.
> *[whispers]* And the weirdest part? Just READING about yawns... can start one.
> So if you yawned during this... it's not boredom. *[chuckles]* It's science.
> *[mischievously]* Tomorrow: why your stomach growls. Subscribe... if you're still awake.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Seeing (or hearing, reading, thinking about) a yawn can trigger one: contagious yawning | established | Provine, "Yawning", *American Scientist* 93:532 (2005); Provine, *Ethology* 72:109 (1986) |
| About half of adults catch yawns (≈40–60 % yawn within minutes of watching others yawn) | established (the figure varies by study) | Provine 1986; Platek et al., *Cognitive Brain Research* 17:223 (2003) |
| "Nobody's sure" why yawns are contagious | accurate: open question | Bartholomew & Cirulli, *PLOS ONE* 9:e91773 (2014, Duke): age explained only 8 % of the variation; most of it is unexplained |
| One idea: yawning cools the brain (deep inhalation of cooler air + increased blood flow) | leading idea, debated (said as "One idea") | Gallup & Gallup, *Evolutionary Psychology* 5:92 (2007); Gallup & Eldakar, *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 6:188 (2013) |
| People with a cold pack on the forehead caught far fewer yawns (≈9 % vs ≈41 % with a warm or room-temperature pack) | one study (shown with its citation on screen) | Gallup & Gallup 2007 |
| Another idea: it's social, we copy the faces around us (mimicry / empathy) | debated (said as "Another idea") | Platek et al. 2003; Norscia & Palagi, *PLOS ONE* (2011); the empathy link is contested by Bartholomew & Cirulli 2014 |
| Some dogs catch human yawns | one study, replications mixed (said as "some dogs") | Joly-Mascheroni, Senju & Shepherd, *Biology Letters* 4:446 (2008): 21 of 29 dogs; Harr et al. 2009 found no effect; Silva et al. 2012 found an auditory effect |
| Just reading about yawning can start one | established (Provine's reading experiments) | Provine 2005 (*American Scientist*); Provine 1986 |
| Not used: "yawning brings in more oxygen" | myth (dropped from the script for length) | Provine, Tate & Geldmacher, *Behavioral and Neural Biology* 48:382 (1987): breathing 100 % O₂ or extra CO₂ didn't change yawning |

  got it to 49 s with no retake. Five takes of the "you" line were tried, and the one where Jessica yawns the word won.

## Rebuild

pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi), node with playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac.
