## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7). Hedged lines are said as hedges; a myth is said as a myth.

| Claim | Status | Sources (two, independent) |
|---|---|---|
| The thumb's top joint (the interphalangeal joint) can bend backward a little; how far varies a lot from person to person | established | Cleveland Clinic, "Hitchhiker's thumb (distal hyperextensibility)" · Harris and Joseph 1949, J Bone Joint Surg 31: 547-559 (X-rays of 294 people), summarised in McDonald, *Myths of Human Genetics* (Univ. of Delaware) |
| A tough strap on the palm side of the joint (the palmar, or volar, plate) stops it bending backward (the "seatbelt" is a metaphor) | established | "Interphalangeal joints of the hand", Physiopedia (the volar plate resists hyperextension) · "Interphalangeal joints of the hand", Wikipedia and its anatomy references (the palmar plate prevents hyperextension) |
| Looser ligaments let joints bend further than usual ("more bungee cord") | established | NHS, "Joint hypermobility syndrome" (the ligaments that support the joints are weak) · Cleveland Clinic, hitchhiker's thumb (hypermobile joints; joint hypermobility syndrome: ligaments loose) |
| "Straight or hitchhiker's", two kinds of thumbs, is a myth: thumb angles run smoothly from straight to sharply bent | established (the two-kinds idea is the myth) | McDonald, *Myths of Human Genetics*, "Genetics of hitchhiker's thumb" · Cleveland Clinic (thumb bendability varies greatly, from none to extreme; more than two kinds) |
| X-rays found every angle in between | established | Harris and Joseph 1949 (294 people X-rayed: a continuous distribution, most in the middle) · McDonald (who reports their data) |
| The usual dividing line, 50 degrees, was picked in a 1953 study | established | Cleveland Clinic ("In 1953, two researchers … greater than or equal to 50 degrees") · McDonald (Glass and Kistler 1953, Acta Genet Stat Med 4: 192-206: no reason given for 50°) |
| Your two thumbs don't always match (description, comment; the button sends the viewer to try) | established | Cleveland Clinic ("one may be a hitchhiker's thumb, and one may be straight") · McDonald (Glass and Kistler found many people with one of each) |

Not said, on purpose: any share of people who "can" do it (the 24.7 % and 35.6 % of the 1953 study rest on the
arbitrary 50° line), and the one-gene story (the study's recessive model is contradicted by its own family data and by
Beckman et al. 1960).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
