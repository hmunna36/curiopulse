**Title:** The Planet Where It Rains GLASS Sideways ☔

**Description:**
There's a planet where it rains glass, sideways: HD 189733b, a gas giant 64 light-years away. It hugs its star (about 13
times closer than Mercury is to our Sun, so a year there lasts about two days), and its sky is around 1,000 °C. That's hot
enough that rock goes up as vapour and comes back down as tiny bits of glass: scientists think its clouds are made of
silica, the stuff of sand and glass, and in 2024 the Webb telescope picked up their signal. Then winds measured at over
8,700 km/h (faster than a bullet) blow the glass sideways. All that glass may be why the planet looks deep blue. And Webb
also found hydrogen sulfide in its air: the gas that makes rotten eggs stink. Umbrella or helmet?

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the planet's wind (a roar under 420 Hz and a thin whistle above 5 kHz, louder from the gust), a constant fall
  of tiny glass glints above 5 kHz (they sit under every word), the burner's low roar now and then; in space a hush.
- **Hits:** glass tinks on the canopy in the hook's pauses; the cloth rip after "Shredded."; ting-ting-ting after
  "factory."; the whip and a low WHOOM on the star, the fire's rumble; a rising hiss for the steam; tinks after "glass.";
  the gust; the bullet's whizz (top only) and the bead's ZING after "bullet."; three pings after "SIDEWAYS."; a shimmer
  before "BLUE"; sniffs, a low gurgle, the CLONK and a bonk after "EGGS."; a ding for the thumbs-up; the umbrella's FWUMP.
- **Score:** a light D-minor groove for the glass rain, a drone and riser into the star with a soft crash, an A-minor
  awe groove over the furnace, a 120 BPM drive for the wind, a wide F-major wonder pad in space, the storm groove again
  into the loop. It drops out for every punchline.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| HD 189733b, a gas giant 64 light-years away, "rains glass, sideways, in howling 7,000 km/h winds" | leading idea (NASA's 2013 release said it "possibly" rains glass; the press releases of 2013 and 2024 state it) | ESA/Hubble heic1312 (2013); Johns Hopkins, 8 Jul 2024 |
| Its clouds hold tiny grains of silica (SiO₂, the stuff of sand and glass) high on the day side | strong result (one study; the exact mineral form is debated) | Inglis et al. 2024, ApJL, "Quartz Clouds in the Dayside Atmosphere of the Quintessential Hot Jupiter HD 189733 b" (JWST MIRI, 8.7 μm) |
| "Rock steams up and comes down as glass" | simplification of silicate vapour condensing into grains that fall | ESA/Hubble heic1312: silicate condensation "could form very small grains of glass" |
| About 13 times closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun; a year of about two days (2.2) | established | Johns Hopkins 2024 |
| A sky of about 1,000 °C (1,700 °F) | established (measured, day side) | ESA/Hubble 2013 ("over 1000 degrees Celsius"); Johns Hopkins 2024 |
| Winds faster than a bullet: over 2 km/s (≈8,700 km/h, 5,400 mph), day side to night side | measured (Doppler, sodium); other estimates 7,000–8,000 km/h, all faster than a rifle bullet (≈1 km/s) | Louden & Wheatley 2015, ApJL (University of Warwick release); ESA/Hubble 2013 |
| It looks deep azure blue | measured (Hubble STIS, the light dimming in blue as it passed behind its star) | Evans et al. 2013, ApJL; ESA/Hubble heic1312 |
| The glass may be why it looks blue | hedged in the words ("scientists think") | ESA/Hubble heic1312: an atmosphere "thought to be laced with silicate particles, which scatter blue light" |
| It smells of rotten eggs (hydrogen sulfide, trace amounts) | strong result (first H₂S on an exoplanet, Nature 2024) | Fu et al. 2024, Nature; Johns Hopkins hub: it "reeks of rotten eggs" |
| (comment) a year of 2.2 days; one side probably always faces its star | established / expected for a planet this close | Johns Hopkins 2024; Louden & Wheatley 2015 (rotation consistent with tidal locking) |

The hot spot east of noon on the painted planet follows Spitzer's 2007 map (Knutson et al., Nature).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
