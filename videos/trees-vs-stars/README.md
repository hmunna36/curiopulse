# Earth Has More TREES Than Our Galaxy Has STARS 🌲 — Short (34 s)

**Final file:** [`trees-vs-stars-short.mp4`](trees-vs-stars-short.mp4)
MP4 · H.264 High · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 34.4 s · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`trees-vs-stars.srt`](trees-vs-stars.srt). Cover: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg).

A CURIOUS Short of the topic test (25 Oct–8 Nov 2026; `reference/story.md` §6), in the pill test's no-pill arm (the 7 Nov
11:30 IST slot). The hiker sits on a bald hilltop at night clicking a tally counter at the Milky Way while the forest stands
all round him and a little sapling with a face sits beside him. The whole place is ONE 3D world seen through a pinhole camera
(`src/web/tv_world.js`): terrain drawn as silhouette slices back to front (the far ones carry a saw-tooth of moonlit pine tops,
so the forest runs to the horizon), real pines near the hill that can wear a star and do the wave, and a painted night sky
(Milky Way, crescent moon) whose bright stars twinkle and fall. Space (Earth painted from 3D noise, with a second "before
farming" copy whose fields are forest), a squinting satellite and a balance are in `tv_space.js`.
The cinematic look (`publish.json` "look": "cine"): the hill is moonlit from the upper left with a cool rim (`L_HILL`), the
starred forest gets a warm rim (`L_GOLD`), orbit a soft diagram light, the plot daylight with sun shafts (`L_PLOT`).

## The plan (written before the build)

- **Kind / length / arms:** CURIOUS (`yt.mjs next-slot`: "topic kind: CURIOUS"), short (30–35 s, 66–76 words), no pill.
- **Hook formula: The Impossible Claim** (after The Comparison on scale-trees, The Direct Address on toes): "Your planet has
  more trees than your galaxy has STARS." It is the title in other words; frame 0 already shows it (him counting the stars,
  the forest behind him). The proof starts at once: the scoreboard (TREES 7 : STARS 1) at 6 s.
- **Answer line (starts 4.64 s):** "And it's not close. Trees win... at least SEVEN to one."
- **The one picture (the 8–13 s rule, adapted):** every star of the sky falls onto a treetop, and the crane up shows the lit
  trees as a small island in a dark sea of them: 2 TRILLION+ STILL WAITING.
- **Escalation:** scientists used to think it was about a tie (400 billion trees vs 100–400 billion stars) → then they counted
  from the ground (430,000 plots): 3 TRILLION → before farming, almost double that.
- **Button (runs into the hook):** "So, stargazers... you've been looking the wrong way." → "Your planet has more trees..."
  The sapling taps his shoulder, he looks round at it... and goes straight back to counting stars; the last frame is frame 0.
- **Jokes (frame-timed):** his counter clicking at the sky; the forest doing the wave under the scoreboard; the counter
  slipping out of his hand after "one." (clunk); the last star bonking onto HIS head after "tree..." while the sapling
  beside him droops; the satellite squinting; the balance wobbling level ("TIE?"); his slap on a trunk after "GROUND.";
  3 TRILLION slamming the old guess off the screen; the sapling's tap and his cold return to the stars.
- **Caption question:** "Trees or stars: what did you guess?"

## Sound design

- **Beds:** the hill: soft wind under 260 Hz, leaves and crickets above 5 kHz; orbit: a low hum; the plot: leaves and birds in
  the pauses.
- **Hits:** the counter's click (a steel tick above 4.6 kHz, under the words) on every click; a run of glints for the flaring
  stars; the scoreboard's rise, a far crowd's cheer after "win...", a rustle sweeping left to right for the wave, flap-flaps
  for 7 and 1; a clunk and a tink for the dropped counter; seventy glints for the landing stars and a BONK + ding on his head;
  a little sad slide for the sapling; the crane; the satellite's beeps and servo; the balance's creak and a ding when it
  levels; the dive; ticks for every trunk; the slap; the slam with a low boom; a ratchet running back for "before farming";
  the tap-tap on his shoulder.
- **Score:** a soft pizzicato groove for the starry hook, a drone for the Milky Way, a stadium groove with a crash for the
  scoreboard, a bright drone as the stars come down, a darker one over the dark sea of trees, a sneaky minor groove in orbit,
  a counting groove in the plot, a deep drone into the slam, a warm one for "before farming", and the hook's groove again on
  the button. It drops out for every punchline ("not close.", "one.", "tree...", "TIE.", "GROUND.", "that.", "the wrong way.").
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| There are about 3.04 trillion trees on Earth ("Three TRILLION") | established estimate (the best one we have) | Crowther et al. 2015, "Mapping tree density at a global scale", *Nature* 525, 201–205 (abstract: boris.unibe.ch/72539); Yale News, 2 Sep 2015 |
| The Milky Way has about 100–400 billion stars | estimate (dim red dwarfs are hard to count) | NASA Goddard, "How Many Stars in the Milky Way?" (Blueshift); ESA (Gaia): "over 100 billion" |
| Trees win "at least seven to one" | arithmetic: 3.04 trillion ÷ 400 billion = 7.6 (≈ 30 to one with 100 billion stars) | the two rows above |
| One star per tree leaves "over two trillion" trees without one | arithmetic: 3.04 trillion − 0.4 trillion = 2.64 trillion (or more) | the two rows above |
| Scientists used to think it was "about a tie" | the previous estimate was about 400 billion trees (from satellite images), about the stars' upper estimate | Yale News 2015 ("about seven and a half times more than some previous estimates"); press coverage of Crowther 2015 citing ~400 billion |
| "Then someone counted... from the ground" (430,000 plots) | method: tree densities measured in more than 400,000 forest plots (429,775) on the ground, combined with satellite maps and models | Crowther et al. 2015 |
| "Before farming? Almost double that" (~5.6 trillion) | estimate: the number of trees has fallen by about 46 % since the start of human civilization, so about 5.6 trillion before (1.8×: said as "almost double", shown as "~") | Crowther et al. 2015 (abstract); press coverage of the 5.6 trillion figure |
| (description) about 15 billion trees are cut down every year | estimate | Crowther et al. 2015 (abstract: "over 15 billion"); Mongabay 2015 (15.3 billion) |

## Ship review

Two QA rounds (`.work/qa/ship-review.md`, not committed; the gist here).

- **Round 1** (qa.py 18/18): during "Three TRILLION." the screen still showed the old 400 BILLION card until the word ended (the
  picture lagged the line by 1.4 s: show-don't-tell 7), and "Almost" sat at 5.9 dB under the "before farming" drone (sound 7).
- **Fixes:** the card now counts up while she says it (400 -> 3,040 BILLION, TREES (COUNTING...), a ratchet above 5 kHz) and slams
  into 3 TRILLION in the pause after the word; three drones softened (before farming, the stars coming down, the dark sea of trees).
- **Round 2 (shipped):** qa.py 18/18: 34.43 s, -14.03 LUFS, -1.68 dBTP, voice mean 24.3 dB (min 11.0, none under 6), WER 0 %, the
  answer at 4.64 s, no pill. Scores: hook 8.5, retention 9, story 9, show 9, narration 8, comedy 8.5, look 8.5, safe area 8.5,
  sound 8.5, science 9, packaging 9, loop 9. Gags in their pauses at -16...-21 dB (narrator -17.5). The last frame matches frame 0
  (mean difference 2.4/255).
- Earlier, while building: the stars' glow stacked into a white-out (hundreds of toppers: glow cut to a third), the sapling was too
  dark to read (lit greens), the forest from 300 m had no texture (moonlit strokes on every slice's pine tops), the island was a
  rectangle (now an ellipse), the scoreboard was too far, the bonk's star blended into the starred trees behind him (bigger,
  outlined, and the camera pushes in for it), and the gags got their own pauses (a longer gap before "and over two", the slap,
  the slam and the 2x stamp moved after their words).
- **Rebuild:** `WORK=../.work-build bash src/build.sh` gave the same timeline and mix and pixel-identical frames; qa.py 18/18.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set. `src/cover.sh` renders the cover.
