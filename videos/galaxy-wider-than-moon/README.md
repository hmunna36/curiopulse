## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7).

| Claim | Status | Sources (two, independent) |
|---|---|---|
| In long-exposure photos the Andromeda galaxy spans about six full Moons ("six times wider than the Moon") | established (how far it reaches depends on the exposure: said "nearly six" in NASA's words; on screen "*in long-exposure photos") | NASA APOD 2022-10-21 "Andromeda in Southern Skies" ("spanning nearly 6 full moons"), APOD 2025-02-21 Hubble mosaic ("almost six full moons") · Wikipedia, Andromeda Galaxy: apparent size 3.167 x 1 degrees; the full Moon is about 0.52 degrees |
| With your eyes alone, even on a dark night, you see only a faint fuzzy patch about a Moon wide: its brighter middle (drawn as the smudge, ALL YOU SEE) | established | Space.com, "You can see another galaxy with the naked eye" (a fuzzy patch, much fainter than the Moon) · Sky & Telescope, "How to see the farthest thing you can see" (an elongated haze; dark skies needed); EarthSky, "Andromeda galaxy: closest spiral to Milky Way" (binoculars show the bright central region) |
| Your eye grabs light for about a blink (a tenth of a second); a camera can collect it for minutes | established (the eye's own "exposure" is debated in detail; the comparison holds) | CU Boulder ASTR 1120 notes, "Sensitivity of optical telescopes" (the eye takes a new exposure about 10 times a second) · Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, MicroObservatory "eye and telescope" activity (eyeScopeT.pdf: about 1/15 s for the eye, 60 s for the telescope on faint galaxies); NASA Hubble, "How Hubble captures an image" (long exposures of faint objects) |
| Its light left about two and a half million years ago | established | Wikipedia, Andromeda Galaxy (2.5 Mly; eclipsing binary 2.52 +/- 0.14 Mly) · Universe Today, "Distance to Andromeda" (about 2.5 million light-years) |
| Before people like us existed (our species is about 300,000 years old) | established | Hublin et al., Nature 546 (2017), Jebel Irhoud · Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, Human Origins: Homo sapiens (about 300,000 years) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
