## Sound design

- **Beds:** the lake (low wind, water lapping), a far-off colony honking only in the pauses (`pause_mask`), the canoe's
  creak on the picture's own roll clock.
- **Hits:** fingertip ticks on stone (top only), two stone knocks after "STONE", a yank + squelch + a cloth wipe after
  "MUMMY", soda fizz, the paddle's splash and a steam hiss (top under "BURNS", whole after it), the swallow's swish and
  PLIP (low + spray), drops rising, crystals growing (glass glints faster and higher), a ting when it is a statue, a poof
  for the mummy, a ting for the thumbs-up, a flamingo honk, a gurgle for the dunk, the flock's wingbeats, a chick's peep,
  the hyena's steps, sniff, paw sizzle and yip, a slide whistle "come in" and a "nuh-uh".
- **Score (100 BPM):** a curious Dm-Bb groove (the hook and the button: the same groove into the loop), a drone under
  "Not stone, actually", a bubbly Gm-Eb groove for the soda, a suspense drone with a riser under the crust, an
  "Egyptian" line (D phrygian dominant on pizzicato with a frame drum) for the mummy, a happy F-C groove for the
  flamingos, a big half-time groove and a riser for the crane shot, a tiptoe for the hyena; it drops out for every
  punchline.

## Science notes

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Natron (northern Tanzania, the Gregory Rift) is a highly alkaline salt lake; natron = sodium carbonate decahydrate (washing soda) + sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) | established | Wikipedia "Lake Natron" and "Natron"; Smithsonian (2013) |
| Its pH has been measured as high as 10.5 ("nearly as high as ammonia"); some sources say over 12; water up to 60 °C | established (the figures vary) | Smithsonian (2013); Wikipedia "Lake Natron" |
| The caustic water burns: "even if you've got the tiniest cut, it's very painful" | established (photographer's report; caustic brine) | Nick Brandt in Smithsonian (2013) |
| Animals found on its shore are preserved: dried ("desiccated") and coated in sodium carbonate, not truly turned to stone ("they probably aren't truly calcified") | established | National Geographic (2013, geologist Thure Cerling); Smithsonian (2013) |
| How they die is not known: Cerling thinks natural causes; Brandt thinks the mirror-like lake fools birds | debated: not in the script ("Whatever falls in" holds either way) | National Geographic (2013) |
| Ancient Egyptians mummified with natron: it absorbs water and raises pH, hostile to bacteria | established | Wikipedia "Natron" (with its sources) |
| The lake is the only regular breeding area of East Africa's lesser flamingos; up to about 75 % of the world's lesser flamingos are born there; the caustic water keeps predators from the nests | established (the share varies: 65-75 %) | Wikipedia "Lake Natron"; RSPB quoted there; Mongabay (2011); Guinness World Records |
| Flamingos feed with the head upside down (the "dunk") | established | general flamingo biology |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
