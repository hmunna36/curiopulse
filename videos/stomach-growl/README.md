**Title:** Why Does Your Stomach GROWL? 🤫

**Description:**
Your stomach picks the quietest room to growl... and it isn't just complaining. A couple of hours after a meal, your
gut starts cleaning: a wave of muscle squeezes all the way through, sweeping out leftovers and bacteria. With no food
inside to muffle it, it's just gas and juice squeezed through a tube. It even has a name: borborygmi. Subscribe for a
new strange question every day.

Full stomach? The same squeezes happen; the food just muffles them. And in 1912 a student swallowed a balloon so his
professor could record his stomach: his hunger pangs lined up with the squeezes.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a quiet hall (low air) with the wall clock ticking; a wet gut churn under 400 Hz inside the body; a
  projector clatter with dust crackle for 1912; a low rumble under the title.
- **Hits:** pencils scribbling in the hook's pauses, a warning gurgle, then the GROWL (synthesized 70→45 Hz buzz with
  gurgles, a sub thump, heads swishing, a chair creak, a dropped pencil); a cricket between the two "cool"s; the
  spinning clock; a sparkle after "cleaning"; a wet swell for the squeeze wave; bacteria "eeps"; bubbles and drips;

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The rumble is called borborygmi; the Greek word (borborygmos, from "to rumble") imitates the sound | established | [Scientific American: Why does your stomach growl when you are hungry?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-your-stomach-gro/); [Wordsmith: borborygmus](https://wordsmith.org/words/borborygmus.html) |
| The noise comes from the muscular walls of the stomach and small intestine squeezing their contents (food, gas, fluid) along | established | Scientific American (above) |
| Between meals the gut runs the migrating motor complex: a cycle whose phase III is a burst of strong contractions that starts in the stomach and sweeps down the small intestine, about every 90–120 min | established | [Takahashi 2014, J Smooth Muscle Res (PMC5137267)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5137267/); [Takahashi 2012, J Neurogastroenterol Motil (PMC3400812)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3400812/) |
| "Hours after a meal": eating switches the cycle off (a 450 kcal breakfast stopped it for ~3.5 h); hunger contractions pick up after ~2 h of emptiness | established | [Wikipedia: Migrating motor complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrating_motor_complex) (Vantrappen 1977); Scientific American |
| Phase III is the gut's "housekeeper": it clears undigested leftovers and keeps bacteria from building up in the small intestine | established (the bacterial role is inferred from overgrowth when it fails) | Takahashi 2012 and 2014 ("cleansing of the empty stomach"); [Wikipedia: Migrating motor complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrating_motor_complex) (bacterial balance) |
| It's louder when empty because there is nothing to muffle it; it also happens on a full stomach | established | Scientific American |
| 1912: Walter Cannon's student A. L. Washburn trained himself to swallow a rubber tube with a balloon; the balloon recorded his stomach's contractions on a kymograph drum, and the key he pressed for each hunger pang lined up with the contractions | established (historic experiment) | Cannon & Washburn, "An explanation of hunger", Am J Physiol 29 (1912); [Wikipedia: Cannon–Washburn hunger experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon-Washburn_Hunger_Experiment); [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/jf59cmsa) |
| Modern support: hunger peaks line up with phase III contractions of the stomach (motilin-driven) | established | [Tack et al. 2016, Gut 65:214](https://gut.bmj.com/content/65/2/214) |

Not claimed: that the squeezes *cause* hunger (people without a stomach still get hungry), or that ghrelin drives the
human cycle (in humans it is mostly motilin; ghrelin does it in rodents, per Takahashi 2014). "Vacuuming" and
"bagpipes" are jokes, shown as jokes.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`); this Short uses none.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
