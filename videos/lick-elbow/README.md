## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** the tight arm, 25–30 s, 55–64 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: the 2026-11-11 23:30 IST slot; no pill: the
   pill test ended 8 Nov). 64 words → 29.0 s.
2. **The viewer's test:** try to lick your own elbow, right now, gently. Almost nobody can, it fails in a funny way
   on the first try, and everyone has a "made it / didn't" to say in the comments (the rules of 5 Nov: a test with the
   face and the hands that most people can't do; the kin of tongue-to-nose, 24K views, 97 % viewed). Hook formula:
   **The Question** ("Can you lick your elbow? Go on... gently."), the exact question people search. Candidates: The
   Direct Address ("Try to lick your elbow. Gently."; finger-w used it three Shorts back), Contrarian Flip ("Everyone
   thinks your tongue's too short. Try licking your elbow." — 4+ s before the test starts). The Question won: it
   confirms the title in its first four words and the test starts at once.
3. **Answer (starts 3.47 s):** "Can't? Your tongue's fine... your ARM's too long." — the surprise flip (everyone
   blames the tongue). **Button:** "Now try the OTHER elbow." → the loop: "Can you lick your elbow?" (it sends the
   viewer back into the test a new way, the finger-w/word-weird button that the hits share).
4. **Beats:** the test (0–3.0) → can't; your tongue's fine, your arm's too long (3.5–7.1, a tape measure along the
   upper arm and a TOO LONG stamp) → feel your upper arm: one stiff bone, pinned at your shoulder (7.4–11.9; the
   viewer squeezes their own arm; the arm goes see-through, a pushpin THUNKS in) → now swing your elbow to your mouth:
   it sails right past (12.2–15.7; the arm goes round the pin like a compass, a gold trail; CHOMP, missed) → your mouth
   sits INSIDE its circle; the elbow can only go round (16.0–21.2; the whole circle, MOUTH as a target inside it, the
   elbow windmills round) → the rare few who can: long tongues, short arms (21.7–25.6; his tongue stretches, his arm
   shrinks, the smaller circle reaches his mouth: SLURP) → now try the other elbow (26.1–28.3; he tosses the cone to the
   other hand and tries the other arm: frame 1). No science word is spoken (bone, pinned, circle; the olecranon is in
   the pinned comment only).
5. **The 8–13 s rule:** 7.4–8.5 the viewer squeezes their own upper arm ("Feel your upper arm?"); 9.2–11.9 they feel
   it is one stiff piece while the bone shows on his; 12.2–15.7 they swing their own elbow toward their mouth and miss.
   The mechanism is ONE picture: a compass pinned at the shoulder.
6. **The caption's one-word question:** **"Could you reach it? Yes or no?"**
7. **Ending:** no pill, nobody says "subscribe"; on "OTHER" the cone flies over his head into the other fist, the left
   arm drops and the right comes up; the last 0.4 s is the hook at negative time, so the last frame is frame 0's
   picture (0.69 s from the last word to the loop).
8. **World files:** `trail.js` borrowed whole from water-older-than-sun (sky-blue's); `elbow.js` new (above).
9. **Cover:** `SC.cover`: the hook at its strain, the yellow gap marker between his tongue and the drip, CAN YOU?

## Narration (64 words, performed)

> Can you lick your elbow? Go on... gently.
> Can't? Your tongue's fine... your ARM's too long.
> Feel your upper arm? One stiff bone... pinned at your shoulder.
> Now swing your elbow to your mouth... it sails right PAST.
> Your mouth sits INSIDE its circle. The elbow can only go round.

> Now try the OTHER elbow.

One take per block, no retakes (347 characters). Whisper heard every word.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a still summer wind under 340 Hz, a far cicada above 6.4 kHz, a far bird now and then.
- **Hits:** the strain creaks and a tongue ratchet above 5.6 kHz in the hook, plips of the drop; the cut whoosh and a
  low tongue boing on "Can't?", ticks on the tongue, a tape zip and a low stamp on "long"; squeaks in the pause after
  "arm?", the x-ray buzz above 5.2 kHz, tok-tok, a low THUNK for the pin; a pencil scribble for the compass trail, a
  whoosh and a CHOMP on "PAST"; a ding after "INSIDE", three whooshes for the windmill and a high slide whistle for the
  dizzy; a cork POOF, a low rising wooomp under "Long tongues...", a low shrink boing and a SLURP in the pause after
  "arms."; a POOF back, the cone's whoosh and its catch, and the strain creak into the loop.
- **Score:** a light marimba/pizzicato groove at 116 BPM from "Your tongue's fine" (none under the test), out for
  TOO LONG, for a breath before "Now swing", for CHOMP and the miss, for SLURP and into the loop.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qc_audio.py`: no word under 10 dB of the background.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Almost nobody can lick their own elbow (the bony point); a few can | popular observation; no study counts it (said only as "Can't?" and "The rare few") | I'm a Scientist (UK), researcher's answer "is it possible to lick your elbow": possible but very difficult |
| The upper arm is one bone (the humerus), joined to the body at the shoulder's ball-and-socket joint | established | standard anatomy (Wikipedia "Humerus", "Shoulder joint") |
| So the elbow can only move on a circle (a sphere) round the shoulder, at a fixed distance: the length of the upper arm | established (geometry of a rigid bone on a ball joint) | as above |
| Your mouth is closer to your shoulder joint than your elbow is: it sits inside that circle | a rough estimate from average body measurements: shoulder–elbow length ~365 mm (men) / ~330 mm (women), 50th percentile (Pheasant & Haslegrave, *Bodyspace*); shoulder joint to mouth roughly 25–30 cm (half the shoulder breadth across, ~15–20 cm up, a few cm forward) | Pheasant & Haslegrave, *Bodyspace* (anthropometric tables); our geometry |
| The tongue sticks out only a few centimetres past the lips; average tongue length 8.5 cm (men), 7.9 cm (women) | established (lengths); protrusion varies by method | Healthline "How long is the human tongue?" (the Edinburgh dental school study) |
| The rare few who can: long tongues, short (upper) arms | inference from the geometry above (also very bendy shoulders: said in the description) | as above |
| The pointy bit of the elbow is the top of the forearm bone (the ulna's olecranon), not the upper arm | established | Wikipedia "Olecranon"; Radiopaedia "Olecranon" (pinned comment and description only) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
