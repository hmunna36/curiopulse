**Title:** Why Does Time FLY as You Get Older? ⏳

**Description:**
You blow out your candles... and somehow it's already your birthday again.

The leading idea: looking back, your brain judges how long a stretch of time was by how many new memories it left. As a
kid almost everything is a first, so one summer leaves a mountain of them. As an adult the days repeat, far less gets
saved, and the whole year looks short in hindsight. It is a leading explanation, not a settled one: in surveys it is
mostly the last ten years that feel faster with age, and feeling rushed plays a part too. Subscribe for a new strange
question every day.

The tower is real. In 2007, scientists dropped volunteers about 30 metres into a net. Asked afterwards, they judged
their own fall to have lasted 36% longer than the same fall watched from the ground... even though a flickering display
strapped to their wrists showed they were not actually seeing in slow motion. A small study, but a neat one: richer
memories make time look longer. So do something new this week.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** a warm quiet room for the party, a muffled slow pulse inside his head, a breeze in the meadow, strip lights
  and air conditioning in the office, night air over the fair.
- **Hits:** every loud sound sits in a pause of the narration: the party horn (after "again!"), the brain's shutter
  (after "memories.", "kid," and twice after "new..."), the bike bell and the frog, a record scratch (after "forever."),
  two pages torn off the calendar, the bite (after "lunch..."), the snore (after "bother."), the tape being tugged and
  its plink with a cricket (round "tiny."), the helmet's strap, the net (after "tower..."), two dings for +36 %, the
  last shutter, and the candles' two notes (after "Or..."). What happens ON a word is on the bed bus and is either
  under 250 Hz (cakes landing, the lever, thuds) or above 5 kHz (his breath on the candles, the tape's ratchet, the
  camera on burst, the air past him on the way down). New atoms in `sfxkit.py`: `party_horn`, `page_rip`, `ratchet`,
  `boing`.
- **Score:** a party groove that stops dead when cake 31 lands and stays out for the horn and "Relax."; a light curious
  figure for the brain that stops for "in new memories"; the sunniest groove of the Short for the summer, doubling for
  "lasts forever" and cut by the record scratch; nothing at all in the office (a clock, keys, a hum: that is the joke);

  only air on the way down; a light figure for the proof; a bright bar for "something new". The button has a music

- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "It's already your birthday again": the years seem to go faster as you get older | a common report; in surveys the age effect is real but modest, and it shows for long spans (how fast the last ten years went, rising to about the fifties), not for the last week, month or year. People of every age say time passes quickly | Wittmann & Lehnhoff 2005 (499 people aged 14–94); Friedman & Janssen 2010; Janssen, Naka & Friedman 2013 |
| "Your brain measures time in new memories": looking back, a stretch of time is judged longer the more events and changes are remembered from it | well supported in experiments on remembered duration (it is about hindsight, not about how time feels while it passes) | Avni-Babad & Ritov 2003; Friedman & Janssen 2010 (review of the laboratory work); Scientific American 2016 |
| "Now? Same desk, same lunch ... your brain doesn't bother": time spent in routine is remembered as shorter | supported: four experiments and two field studies ("doesn't bother" is the joke; the picture shows one photo) | Avni-Babad & Ritov 2003 |
| "As a kid, everything is new ... Scientists think that's why a year looks tiny": fewer firsts with age, so fewer distinct memories and shorter-looking years | a leading idea since William James (1890), still debated: said as "Scientists think", and tagged LEADING IDEA on screen. The surveys found it explains little of how fast time seems to pass now, and point to time pressure as well; a 2026 study links the faster-seeming decade to how well new memories are formed, not to how many are recalled | James 1890; Friedman & Janssen 2010; Janssen, Naka & Friedman 2013; Teghil et al. 2026 |
| "One lab dropped people off a tower ... they remembered the fall a third longer": volunteers fell backward into a net from a tower (2.49 s of free fall, about 30 m). Afterwards they judged their own fall, with a stopwatch, to have lasted 36 % longer than the same fall watched from the ground (2.96 s against 2.17 s, the two numbers on screen) | one small study (seven volunteers gave the duration estimates; twenty took the perception test): tagged ONE SMALL STUDY, 2007 on screen. A flickering wrist display showed no faster perception during the fall, so the authors put the stretch down to memory: "a richer encoding of memory" | Stetson, Fiesta & Eagleman 2007 |
| "So try something new": new and varied experiences make a period look longer in hindsight | follows from the routine studies; said as advice, lightly | Avni-Babad & Ritov 2003 |
| Not in the script: the proportional idea (a year is a smaller share of a longer life), a slowing internal clock, and the "mental images" idea are other explanations; none is settled | hypotheses | Janssen, Naka & Friedman 2013 (overview) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 6 minutes; `src/cover.sh` then renders the cover. It needs
python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile, pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic
chain runs), node with playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting
runs on the graphics chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so
(`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
