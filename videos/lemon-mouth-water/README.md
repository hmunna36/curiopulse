## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Watching, or even thinking of, someone biting into a juicy lemon makes your saliva flow ("Imagine biting this juicy lemon... Your mouth's watering, right?") | established (several labs) | Hagenmuller F, Rössler W, Wittwer A, Haker H (2014) "Juicy lemons for measuring basic empathic resonance", *Psychiatry Research* 219(2):391-396, abstract: "Watch or even think of someone biting into a juicy lemon and your saliva will flow." (49 adults, saliva caught in cotton rolls, a video of a man cutting and eating a lemon against a control video); Wang QJ, Knoeferle K, Spence C (2017) "Music to make your mouth water?", *Frontiers in Psychology* 8:638 (a video of a man eating a lemon raised salivation; a "sour" soundtrack did not) |
| Imagining eating it is what does it: picturing the taste of a lemon slice in front of you raised saliva more than just looking at it ("your brain just took a PRACTICE bite") | leading idea, with experiments behind it | Keesman M, Aarts H, Vermeent S, Häfner M, Papies EK (2016) "Consumption simulations induce salivation to food cues", *PLoS ONE* 11(11):e0165449 (a fresh lemon slice; the instruction "How would the object taste?" raised salivation for the sour food, d = 0.76); the metaphor "practice bite" stands for this "simulation" of eating |
| A caveat (not said): looking at a lemon alone does not raise saliva in every study, and imagining a sour taste alone was not significant in one (24 people, parotid flow) | debated | Wang et al. 2017 citing Kerr 1961 and Shannon et al. 1974 (looking does not), Pangborn 1968 (sniffing or slicing does); Drummond PD (1995) *Physiology & Behavior* 57(2):373-376. Hence the test is "imagine biting THIS lemon" while he bites it on screen (watching + imagining), not imagining in the dark |
| The flood comes from glands whose ducts open under the tongue and inside the cheek ("Feel that flood under your tongue?") | established anatomy | the submandibular and sublingual ducts open under the tongue, the parotid duct inside the cheek by the upper molars (any anatomy text; e.g. Cleveland Clinic, "Salivary glands") |
| Sour = acid; saliva dilutes and buffers acid (bicarbonate) and protects the teeth; acid is the strongest taste trigger for saliva ("Lemon means acid... so your brain hoses your teeth down early") | established | Pedersen AM, Bardow A, Jensen SB, Nauntofte B (2002) "Saliva and gastrointestinal functions of taste, mastication, swallowing and digestion", *Oral Diseases* 8:117-129; Dawes C (2008) "Salivary flow patterns and the health of hard and soft oral tissues", *JADA* 139:18S-24S; the bicarbonate buffer: Bardow et al., *Archives of Oral Biology* (2000) |
| The mouth gets ready BEFORE the food arrives ("early") | established (Pavlov's "psychic secretion", the cephalic phase) | Pavlov's dogs salivated at the sight or footsteps of the person who fed them before any food (Indiana University library, "The work of the digestive glands", 1902; Comptes Rendus Biologies 2006, 329:358-365) |
| "In one lab, people drooled just WATCHING a man eat a lemon." | established | Hagenmuller et al. 2014 (the video: a man cutting and eating a lemon); replicated by Wang et al. 2017 |
| "The more they drooled... the more they cared about others." | one study (a correlation, said of "one lab") | Hagenmuller et al. 2014: the salivation response "correlated positively with self-reported empathic concern" (the Interpersonal Reactivity Index's caring-for-others scale); the button's "Congrats, you're nice" is the joke on it, not a claim |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
