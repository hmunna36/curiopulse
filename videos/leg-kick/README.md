## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tapping the tendon just under the kneecap stretches the thigh muscle (quadriceps); its stretch sensors (muscle spindles) send a signal to the spinal cord, which fires the same muscle back: the leg kicks | established (the patellar or knee-jerk reflex, a monosynaptic stretch reflex at the L2–L4 segments) | Wikipedia "Patellar reflex" and its references; any physiology text (e.g. Purves, *Neuroscience*) |
| "That was your spine": the loop runs through the spinal cord, not the brain ("Your brain? Wasn't even asked.") | established (the reflex arc is independent of higher centres; the brain can only damp or boost it) | Wikipedia "Patellar reflex" ("completely independent of higher centres"; it "may be influenced by the patient consciously inhibiting or exaggerating the response") |
| About a fiftieth of a second (on screen: REAL SPEED 1/50 s) | established: about 18 ms from the stretch to the start of the muscle's contraction | Wikipedia "Patellar reflex", citing Brown, *Electromyography and Disorders of the Central Nervous System* (1984) |
| "No kick? Clench your teeth and tap again": clenching the teeth (part of the Jendrassik manoeuvre) makes leg reflexes bigger | established for the manoeuvre (used in clinics since 1883); teeth clenching alone measurably boosts the leg's H-reflex | Miyahara et al., J Neurophysiol 76:2033 (1996); Boroojerdi et al., Clin Neurophysiol (2000); Zehr & Stein, Exp Brain Res (1999); Wikipedia "Jendrassik maneuver" |
| "In 1902 a scientist rang a bell before each tap. Then the bell alone made legs kick." | established as history: Edwin B. Twitmyer's Penn dissertation (1902): a bell half a second before each hammer blow; after 150–238 paired trials the bell alone produced the kick in all six subjects. (Whether this counts as discovering the conditioned reflex is debated: Coon, Psychol Rep 1983; the Short only says what happened) | F. W. Irwin, *Am J Psychol* 56:451 (1943), on psych.upenn.edu/history/twittext.htm; Wikipedia "Edwin B. Twitmyer" |
| "He told the experts... crickets." | established as his recollection: he reported it at the American Psychological Association's meeting in Philadelphia, December 1904 (William James presiding), and recalled no discussion whatever | Irwin 1943 (above); the abstract is in *Psychological Bulletin* 2:43 (1905) |
| "Pavlov's dog got famous instead." | established: Pavlov's conditioning work with dogs (announced 1903, Nobel Prize 1904 for digestion) became the famous one; the Short does not claim Pavlov copied anyone | Irwin 1943; Wikipedia "Classical conditioning" |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
