## Sound design

- **Beds:** the club (room tone and a crowd's murmur, all under 400 Hz), inside the head (a soft low hum), the booth (an air
  conditioner's hum).
- **Hits:** frame 1: a soft whoosh and the mic coming up (edges only, under "Pad"); his rattle as tiny lip ticks (edges); a rubber
  twang in the gap after "fast." (the knot), a slurp before "Your mouth"; rubbery squeaks for the jam; the PK! as edges on "once";
  a bloop and the dive. Inside: the slow pops land whole in the gaps after "pops", "LIPS", "K," and "BACK" (a round low "puh" for
  P, a dry click for K); speeding up, the pops run under the words as their edges only (a low knock, a high tick) and get faster:
  they are the drums; the big PK! whole in the gap after "BOTH.". The payoff: ticks on each try, a ding after "easy...", a slide
  whistle and a crowd's "ooh" after "apart.", letters clattering. The lab: a soft beep, then nothing but the hum, and two crickets
  after "talking.". The button: a short cheer after "talking?", a drum roll under "Then go".
- **Score:** a bouncy 132 BPM groove from frame 1 (a crash on it), cut at the knot; a sneaky pizzicato walk under the answer; a
  low pad inside, then a swelling chord as it speeds up, a crash on the big PK!; the groove again under the tries (no marimba, so
  the payoff line is clear), cut on "falls"; a calm pad in the lab, then silence from "just" to "Still"; the groove back for the
  last bar, landing on a bar line at the end so the loop rolls into frame 1's bar.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC. The pill
  is silent.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Pad kid poured curd pulled cod" was so hard that the volunteers could not get through it; some simply stopped talking altogether (an MIT team led by Stefanie Shattuck-Hufnagel, with Haskins Labs, LMU Munich, Wellesley and USC, at the 166th Acoustical Society of America meeting, San Francisco, Dec 2013) | reported by the researcher | ASA press release via ScienceDaily, "Tripped tongues teach speech secrets", 5 Dec 2013 (sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/12/131204130916.htm); The Register, 6 Dec 2013 ("some people simply gave up halfway through"); HuffPost misprints the last word as "cold" |
| Tongue-twister slips are often not swaps but two sounds at once (the "t" and "c" of "top cop" arriving together: "tkop") | established | ASA release (above); Goldstein, Pouplier, Chen, Saltzman & Byrd, "Dynamic action units slip in speech production errors", Cognition 103:386–412 (2007): "an error usually involves the concurrent production of more than one gesture"; intrusions in 28.2 % of tokens, substitutions 4.3 % (electromagnetic sensors on the tongue and lips) |
| Faster speech and more repetitions mean more slips (fast rate: 9 % on the first repetition, 32 % on the seventh) | established (one well-cited study) | Goldstein et al. 2007 |
| "Your mouth slips into an easier rhythm": alternating onsets over a repeated sound make a 2:1 rhythm between the articulators, and the slips move them toward the steadier 1:1 rhythm (everything together) | leading account | Goldstein et al. 2007 (frequency locking, the 1:1 mode); said in everyday words, the description says "one idea" for the D's |
| P is a closure and burst at the lips, K at the back of the tongue against the soft palate: the same kind of sound (voiceless stops) at opposite ends | established | standard phonetics (IPA: bilabial and velar plosives) |
| The brain plans speech ahead: sounds of later words are already active (anticipation slips); tongue twisters are a window on that planning | established | ASA release; Fromkin 1971 (Language 47:27–52); Dell 1986 (Psych Review 93:283–321) |
| Guinness once named "The sixth sick sheik's sixth sheep's sick" the hardest tongue twister in English (pinned-comment bonus) | reported | NPR, 8 Nov 2021, "Happy International Tongue Twister Day"; Christian Science Monitor, 2021 (Guinness stopped tracking the category in 1974) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
