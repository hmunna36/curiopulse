# Why Do You See a GREEN Dot That Isn't There? 👁️ — Short (34 s)

**Final file:** [`green-dot-short.mp4`](green-dot-short.mp4)
MP4 · H.264 High · 1080×1920 (9:16) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 34.3 s · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`green-dot.srt`](green-dot.srt). Cover: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg).

The test is on the screen itself: the lilac chaser (Jeremy Hinton, 2005), twelve blurry pink dots round a black cross on
plain grey, one dot off at a time, the gap stepping round clockwise (a lap in 1.6 s). The hiker stands close below it in
grey morning fog, head and shoulders, his eyes locked up on the cross: he does the test with the viewer. Then his eye cut
in half with a team of little colour-seers on its back wall, the brain painting the boring dots over, and a 1950s
suction cup on his eyeball. Same narrator, captions and bloom look as every CurioPulse Short.
The look is the cinematic one (`publish.json` "look": "cine"), but the test is drawn after the light and the lens
(`post.overlay`, `src/web/fog.js`), so its grey (196, 196, 196) and its pink are exact on every frame and nothing on
screen is green until the payoff word: the answer line says "There's no green on your screen", and it is true. The
fog's light has no pool, halo, split-tone or fringe, so the fog round his head meets the exact field without a seam.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** fog wind under 280 Hz; a warm throb inside the eye; the brain's office hum; a big quiet room and an old
  projector for the 1950s.
- **Hits:** a shimmer on frame 1; bwip? after "round?"; dun-dun after "screen."; waah after "faking it."; two lid
  squeaks after "blink..."; falling glints on "fade away"; a whoosh into his eye; snores from the pink team; a pip as the
  dot goes off; a drumroll, then a party horn after "GREEN."; the brain's yawn and the BORING stamp; a roller's swish
  on each of the twelve dots; THWOP (low and top only, so it can sit on the word) and the cup's wobble; pew as the
  picture fades; stopwatch ticks and a ding; the blink, a ping as the eyes open, a happy arpeggio after "back."
- **Score:** the test's own theme: a soft A-minor pad and a music box going round four notes a lap (one lap of the dots
  per bar); a low tension drone and a quickening top-only clock while he must not blink; a bouncy marimba groove for
  the seers; a bored half-time groove, then a brisk painting groove; an old upright rag for the 1950s; the test's theme
  again after "Now...", counted back from the end so the loop goes round. The score drops out for every punchline.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after
  AAC. `qc_audio.py`: only four short function words under 10 dB (on, The, a, in); every gag measured −17 to −23 dB in
  its own gap with the narrator at about −16.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The test: 12 blurry lilac/magenta dots round a fixation cross on grey, one gap stepping round; staring shows a green dot running round, and with steady fixation the magenta dots vanish | established (the lilac chaser) | Michael Bach, "Lilac Chaser", michaelbach.de/ot/col-lilacChaser (stimulus defaults read from its source: 12 blobs, 1 gap, ring radius 0.7 of the half-height, blob size 35 with a solid core to 0.3, magenta at 80 %, a black cross); Wikipedia "Lilac chaser" |
| There is no green on the screen: the green is made by your eye | established | Bach: a negative afterimage of the magenta patch on a neutral background; "a strong greenish colour" when the violet patch is gone. Checked on our frames: the test's grey is exactly R = G = B |
| Staring at pink tires out the eye's response to pink in that spot, so grey there looks like the opposite, green | established (negative afterimage; adaptation) | Bach (adaptation of the retinal spot; complementary colour); Zaidi et al. 2012, Current Biology 22:220, "Neural locus of color afterimages" (retinal ganglion cells); Wikipedia "Lilac chaser" (opponent process: lilac adapts red and blue, the afterimage is green) |
| One moving green dot (not twelve flashes) | established (phi phenomenon) | Bach; Wikipedia "Lilac chaser" (Bertamini 2018) |
| Blurry things that never change fade away while you stare; the brain fills the place with what is around it | established (Troxler fading, 1804); "paints over them" is the metaphor for filling-in, partly in the brain | Martinez-Conde & Macknik 2017, Phil Trans R Soc B, "Unchanging visions" (PMC5332862): fading under fixation, rediscovered by Troxler in 1804; Hsieh & Tse 2006 (part of the fading is in the brain); Wikipedia "Troxler's fading" (stronger for small, low-contrast, blurred, peripheral stimuli) |
| A scientist stuck pictures onto people's eyeballs with a suction cup; they faded in seconds | established | Martinez-Conde & Macknik 2017: "Yarbus's ... suction cup technique, where a suction device served to attach a tightly fitting contact lens assembly to the eyeball. The contact lens assembly displayed an image ... led to rapid visual fading, usually after a few seconds" |
| Blinking (or moving your eyes) brings the faded dots back | established | Bach: shifting the gaze brings the magenta circle back; fading needs unchanging stimulation |
| Jeremy Hinton made the illusion in 2005 (description and comment) | established | Bach: invented in 2005, sent to him on 22 May 2005, copied widely within days |

What changed along the way:
- Stills: the eye shot was too small and its nerve read as a lollipop stick: the eye now fills the frame with its front
  up under the test card (no nerve), the seers stand big on its floor; the brain's screen moved up and the roller grew;
  the suction cup became a pink rubber dome with a lip and a stalk (it read as a black eye); his veins thinned.
- Safe-area mask: his chin crossed y 1640: his head moved up to y 1462 at a slightly smaller size; WHAT YOU SEE moved
  to y 400.
- Sound: the music box first sat on "Keep" and under the whispered lines (staring, Don't, The under 6 dB): it starts
  after the first word, stops before "faking it", the clock ticks moved above 5.2 kHz, the wind below 280 Hz.
- qa.py round 1: "See" at -19 dB was the alignment (its window was the "s" and the silence before it): moved onto its
  vowel. Round 2: a skeptical squint on "faking it".
- `src/build.sh` from the cached takes made the final MP4; its mix.wav is byte-identical to round 1's.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
World files borrowed: `src/web/eyeball.js` (eye-rub-stars), `src/web/brainpaint.js` (blind-spot: `erBrain`). New:
`fog.js` (the test, the fog, the hiker under it), `seers.js` (the eye's colour-seers), `paint.js` (the brain's
roller), `lab.js` (the 1950s suction cup), `cover.sh` renders `SC.cover`.
