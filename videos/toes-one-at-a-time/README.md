## The plan (written before the build)

1. **Length:** the short arm, 30–35 s, 66–76 words (`yt.mjs next-slot`: 5 Nov 2026 23:30 IST, a TEST slot, the pill arm).
2. **The test:** sitting, foot flat (socks fine): lift only your big toe (easy: it has its own lifting muscle), then only
   the next toe: the other three come with it. Then curl them: the same again underneath.
   - Hook (The Direct Address, as the test): **"Lift your big toe. Easy. Now lift JUST the next one."** Other candidates:
     The Question ("Can you move just ONE of your toes? Try the second one.") and The Impossible Claim ("Four of your
     toes can't move alone. Lift just the second one."); the instruction won: it starts the viewer's foot moving on
     word one, and the title's question is the strange result itself. Last three formulas: The Question, The Direct
     Address, Contrarian Flip, so no formula runs four in a row.
   - Answer (starts 4.55 s): **"The whole gang came, right? They share ONE string."**
   - Button (runs into the hook): **"So... practise. Big toe first."** → "Lift your big toe..."
3. **The 8–13 s rule:** 5–8 s the four are up (the viewer's are too) and a string ties them; 8.4–10.5 s "Your big toe has
   its own muscle": the viewer lifts the big toe again, the glass leg shows its own orange muscle pulling it; 10.7–13.8 s
   "The other four? ONE muscle... four strings.": the viewer lifts the four again, the cyan muscle's one string splits
   in four; 14.2 s "Now curl them": the viewer curls. No science word spoken (tendon is in the description only).
4. **Subscribe arm:** the pill (23:30 slot): the short silent pill over the last 2.4 s; his face and foot stay above y 1050.
5. **Loop:** the button's big toe goes up on "Big toe first", comes down in the tail, and the last 0.4 s plays the hook
   at negative time (the big toe starting to lift), so frame 1 continues the move.
6. **Caption question:** "Did the whole gang come? Yes or no?"

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The big toe has its own long lifting muscle (extensor hallucis longus). | established | Kenhub, "Extensor hallucis longus muscle"; QUT podiatry anatomy app |
| The four small toes are lifted mostly by ONE long muscle (extensor digitorum longus) whose tendon splits into four slips, one to each of toes 2–5 ("ONE muscle... four strings"). A short muscle on top of the foot (extensor digitorum brevis) helps toes 2–4, so "mostly" in the description. | established | QUT podiatry anatomy app, "Extensor digitorum longus"; WikiMSK, "Extensor Digitorum Longus" |
| Pull that one muscle and the four rise together, which is why lifting only the second toe drags the others along (the test). | established mechanism; how far each person can separate them varies with practice | the anatomy sources above; toe-control exercises ("toe yoga") are taught because the skill improves with practice (sportsedtv, Active.com) |
| Curling the four is the same: one long curling muscle (flexor digitorum longus) with four tendons under the sole. | established | standard anatomy (FDL: one belly, four tendons to toes 2–5) |
| In brain scans of nine typical people, touching each toe gave no neat map of separate toes ("no neat spot for each toe"). | established for that study (small sample) | Dempsey-Jones, Wesselink, Friedman, Makin, "Organized Toe Maps in Extreme Foot Users", *Cell Reports* 28: 2748–2756 (2019); Neuroscience News, "The toes of artists who paint with their feet can be mapped in their brains" (2019) |
| Two artists born without arms, who paint with their feet, had a separate, finger-like map for each toe ("had one for EVERY toe"). | established for that study (two people) | the same paper and coverage (AFP via Malay Mail, 11 Sep 2019) |
| Practice is part of it ("So... practise."): either we lose toe maps we are born with, or we build them with use; the authors give both. | debated: said as the button's joke and hedged in the comment | the same paper's discussion, as reported by Neuroscience News |
| Safety: stop if your foot cramps. | advice | on screen (CRAMP? STOP.) and in the caption and description |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
