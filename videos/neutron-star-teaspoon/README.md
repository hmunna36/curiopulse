- **Beds:** camp: a breeze under 260 Hz and crickets; space: a low cosmic hum; the Sun: a roar under 200 Hz while it is the Sun;

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A teaspoon (or a sugar cube) of neutron star would weigh about a billion tonnes on Earth, "about as much as a mountain" | established (an order of magnitude; pages give 1 to a few billion tonnes) | NASA Science, "Neutron Stars Are Weird!" (sugar cube: about 1 trillion kg, 1 billion tons, "about as much as a mountain"); NASA Goddard (NICER, Swift): a teaspoon about a billion tons; Astronomy magazine 2018 |
| A neutron star holds more than our Sun's mass (typically about 1.4 Suns, up to about 2) in a ball about 20 km across, the size of a city | established | NASA Science, "Neutron Stars Are Weird!" ("up to two solar masses into a city-size volume"); NASA Goddard Imagine the Universe |
| Atoms are mostly empty space; in a neutron star that space is crushed out, its matter as dense as an atom's nucleus | established (the picture: if an atom were a stadium, its nucleus would be a pea or a marble on the centre spot: an illustration, the ratio varies by source) | NASA (neutron star density); university and outreach analogies (the stadium and the pea) |
| Off its star a spoonful would not stay in the spoon: without the star's gravity it would spring apart with an enormous release of energy | leading answer (popular-science physics, no detailed model) | Astronomy magazine, Ask Astro (Aug and Sep 2018): "only their immense gravity keeps the matter inside from exploding"; Andrew Pontzen (UCL) on The Naked Scientists: "this thing is just going to spring apart really fast... release a huge amount of energy" |
| (Pinned comment) The fastest known one, PSR J1748-2446ad, spins about 716 times a second, twice as fast as a household blender | established | NASA Science ("spins 43,000 times every minute... twice as fast as the typical household blender"); Hessels et al., Science 2006 |

The picture of a neutron star hovering over a city is a size comparison, not an event; the campsite spoon is the textbook
"if you brought it to Earth" thought experiment, and the explosion line says why it could never happen.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
