**Title:** Can You Make a 7 in SIGN Language? 🤟

**Description:**
Can you sign a seven? Touch your thumb to your ring finger and keep the other three fingers straight up. Did your pinky sink? With the pinky folded down, your seven looks like the sign for TWO. When your ring finger bends, your pinky (and a bit of your middle finger) comes along, because the cords that bend those fingers are tied together inside your hand, like the reins of a chariot. One violinist had a surgeon cut every one of those ties, in both hands, and ended up with fingers that move on their own. Now try your seven fast.

Could you make a 7?

The science: in a lab study of single-finger movements, the ring finger was the least independent finger in 9 of 10 people's right hands: when they moved it, the middle and little fingers moved too (Häger-Ross & Schieber, Journal of Neuroscience, 2000; Lang & Schieber, Journal of Neurophysiology, 2004). The deep tendons that bend the middle, ring and little fingers share muscle fibres and are cross-linked at the wrist; the hand surgeon Claude Verdan named this the "quadriga" effect in 1960, after the four reins of a Roman chariot (Schreuders, Journal of Hand Surgery European Volume, 2012). The violinist is Joris Leijnse, who had the connections between his finger tendons removed in both hands between 1979 and 1988 and described it in his 1995 PhD thesis (Erasmus University Rotterdam): much better dexterity, and he could type at great speed. Fast finger movements are even less independent (Häger-Ross & Schieber 2000). The reins, the knot and the little charioteer are pictures of the idea, not real parts of your hand.

## Rebuild

pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
