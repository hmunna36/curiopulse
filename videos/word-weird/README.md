## Sound design

- **The chant's clock:** a soft tok on every rep (a knock under 250 Hz and a tick above 5.2 kHz, so it can sit on any word);
  the groove runs at the chant's tempo (167.6 BPM, half time), its marimba eighths landing on the reps. In the button the
  toks run on the loop's clock, into frame 1's.
- **Beds:** the campfire (a low rumble, crackle above 5 kHz, far crickets above 5 kHz) in the camp shots; a low hum inside his
  head; the hall's room tone and pencils on paper above 5.2 kHz.
- **Gags, each in a pause of its own (level in its gap, the narrator's median word at −15.8 dB):** the crack after "word."
  (−20.6), tiny steps and the bubble's pop after "out." (−22.7), the little spoon's "WHAT?!" after "answers." (−20.6),
  DING-DING-DING-DING after "in?" (−18.0), one cricket after "home." (−24.3), the wire's ZAP after "worn out." (−21.9), the
  pencils' clatter after "quit..." (−24.8), the whip and the jelly door's boing after "WRONG." (−22.4), a wobbly whistle after
  "NAME." (−21.2).
- **Score:** a bouncy F-C-Dm-Bb groove from "Keep going" (drops out for "walks out" and comes back under the doorbell), a
  tension drone under "Thirty rings in?", silence for nobody home, a pizzicato lab groove in A minor, a drone down the wire,
  a marimba ostinato for "over and over", a drone under "Seven in ten", silence for "it looked WRONG", a light groove under
  the button that stops before "NAME".
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC.

## Science notes

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Saying (or seeing) a word over and over makes it lose its meaning for a while: "semantic satiation" | established (the name: Leon Jakobovits James, McGill, 1962; reports since Severance & Washburn 1907) | Tian & Huber 2010, Cognitive Psychology 60(4):267-290 (PMC2882703), introduction |
| The sound stays, the meaning drops away ("The sound stays... but the MEANING walks out.") | established: participants describe a string of letters or sounds that no longer means anything | Moulin et al. 2021 (quotes: "They lose their meaning the more you look at them. They just seem like a string of letters"); Titchener 1916, quoted by Tian & Huber |
| It is the link between the word and its meaning that tires, not the word or the meaning ("Lab results: sound, fine. Meaning, fine. The WIRE between them... worn out.") | leading idea, said as a lab result (the cutaway is labelled Tian & Huber, Cognitive Psychology 2010) | Tian & Huber 2010: repeating the same category label slowed meaning matches; repeating the meaning with other words, or the word in a word-matching task, did not; "satiation reflects the association between the lexical entry and its meaning" |
| "Thirty rings in? Nobody's home." (about thirty repetitions) | established for the lab task (about 30 copies, about a minute); spoken repetition varies person to person, so the Short says "thirty rings in?" as the picture's count | Moulin et al. 2021, Table 1: jamais vu stoppages after 33.6 copies (68.6 s) on average; St Andrews news release ("about 30 repetitions, or one minute") |
| "Scientists had students copy DOOR over and over. Seven in ten quit... it looked WRONG." | established | Moulin, Bell, Turunen, Baharin & O'Connor, "The the the the induction of jamais vu in the laboratory", Memory 29(7):933-942 (2021; online 2020): 94 Leeds undergraduates copied 12 words (door, money, room, drink among them); 66 of 93 (70.9 %) stopped at least once because the word felt peculiar; the top reason was "I knew it was spelt right, but it seemed wrong" (43 %) |
| IG NOBEL PRIZE 2023 (on screen) | established | 2023 Ig Nobel Prize for Literature to Moulin, Bell, Turunen, Baharin and O'Connor (University of St Andrews news, 2023) |
| It wears off (description) | established: satiation is temporary | Tian & Huber 2010; Moulin et al. 2021 |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
