## Sound design

- **Beds:** bathroom room tone (under 230 Hz), a quiet room for the book, an old film projector for the photo, a laptop hum for the email; gated per shot.
- **Hits:** a tiny wet "blep" on every try of his tongue's edges, on the picture's own clock (the full blep in pauses, only its low and top under words); Can't? a sagging slide + a thud; the parents' eyes: two fwips; no bone: bonk; three glints for the three ways of muscle; the octopus: a low thud and a bloop; the slice curls on a ratchet (top only); ONCE: a low thud, TUBE! a ding, his flop a wah; the book slams (low only) with dust; ONE GENE: a stamp thud + ding; twins: boing-boing on the same note; one could: fwip + sparkle; one couldn't: wah-wah; the photo: a shutter; steam (top only); typing (top only); SEND, whoosh, ding; NOT ADOPTED: a warm ding.
- **Score:** a 112 BPM pizzicato/marimba groove that drops out for every punchline; a held curious chord inside the tongue; a riser into ONCE; a silent-film stride piano for 1940; a warm chord for "They're not."; the hook's own groove under "Try again", into the loop.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP after AAC. Gags measured in their gaps at −17…−25 dB with the narration at −16 (`src/qc_gags.py`).

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Most people can roll their tongue, many can't (65–81 % can, slightly more women than men) | established (surveys) | J. H. McDonald, *Myths of Human Genetics*, "Tongue rolling", University of Delaware, https://udel.edu/~mcdonald/mythtongueroll.html |
| The tongue has no bones; it is muscle that is its own skeleton (a "muscular hydrostat"), like an octopus's arm or an elephant's trunk | established | Kier & Smith 1985, *Zool. J. Linn. Soc.* 83: 307–324; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscular_hydrostat |
| Its muscle runs three ways: lengthways, across, and up-and-down | established (anatomy) | the intrinsic muscles: superior/inferior longitudinal, transverse, vertical; e.g. Visible Body, "The Terrific Tongue"; Stavness et al. 2012 (tongue muscle activation model) |
| Rolling means curling both edges up at once | established (description); exactly which muscles do it is modelled, not settled | Stavness et al. 2012: upward bending by the superior longitudinal muscle, side bending needs transverse and vertical muscles too |
| School books taught that one gene (from your parents) decides it | established (history) | Sturtevant 1940 proposed a single dominant gene; still taught (McDonald; PBS NewsHour, "The genetic myth textbooks get wrong", 5 Aug 2015, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/updates/genetic-myth-textbooks-get-wrong/) |
| Identical twins, same genes: one could, one couldn't | established | Matlock 1952: 7 of 33 pairs of identical twins differed (McDonald; PBS 2015); Reedy et al. 1971: 7 of 61 identical pairs differed, 30 of 132 fraternal pairs |
| The scientist behind it said he was embarrassed | established (his own words) | A. H. Sturtevant, *A History of Genetics* (1965): "embarrassed to see it listed in some current works as an established Mendelian case" (quoted by McDonald and PBS 2015) |
| Kids have emailed a scientist asking if they're adopted | established (anecdote, sourced) | John McDonald to PBS NewsHour (2015): emails from children worried they are not their parents' biological children |
| (description) Genes may still play some part; many kids only learn it as they grow | leading idea / one study | twin data above (identical pairs differ less than fraternal); Komai 1951: rollers rose from 54 % at ages 6–7 to 76 % at 12 in Japanese schoolchildren (McDonald). Said as "may" and "plenty of kids" |
| BBC Science Focus (2026) on the same myth | context | https://www.sciencefocus.com/the-human-body/tongue-rolling-genetics-myth |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
