## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| An octopus has three hearts: two branchial (gill) hearts, one at each gill, and one systemic heart | established | Live Science, "How many hearts does an octopus have?" (Kirt Onthank, Walla Walla University); Smithsonian Ocean |
| "Two of them are just for its gills": each branchial heart pumps blood through the gill it sits on | established | Live Science (Onthank) |
| The blood comes out of the gills at low pressure ("all floppy"), so the systemic heart re-pressurizes it and sends it round the body ("all over again") | established (the reason, per Onthank: the same problem mammals solve with four chambers) | Live Science (Onthank) |
| Octopus blood is blue because it carries oxygen with copper (hemocyanin), not iron (hemoglobin) | established | Live Science; Smithsonian Ocean |
| NOT said: "blue blood is weak, so it needs three hearts". Onthank calls that a naive idea: in cold, low-oxygen sea water octopus hemocyanin is at least as good as hemoglobin | (left out on purpose) | Live Science (Onthank) |
| "Your heart's just two pumps glued together": the right side pumps to the lungs, the left side to the body; three octopus hearts do the job of one four-chamber heart | established | Live Science (Onthank) |
| When an octopus swims, its (big) heart stops for a moment | established observation; why is a leading idea (the pressure of jetting) | Live Science (Onthank: "octopus hearts stop for a few moments when they swim"); Wells 1979, J Exp Biol |
| It "would rather walk": octopuses generally prefer crawling to swimming | established | Live Science (Onthank) |
| The walk on two arms with the rest wrapped up | established for two species (the coconut octopus, Abdopus aculeatus) | Huffard, Boneka & Full 2005, Science; UC Berkeley news 24 Mar 2005 |
| Comment bonus: over 500 million neurons, more than two-thirds in the arms and body | established (widely cited) | OIST press release on Gutnick et al. 2020, Current Biology |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
