**Title:** What Happens When You CRACK Your Knuckles? 💥

**Description:**
You lock your fingers, push... and CRACK. No bones were harmed.

Each knuckle is a joint sealed in a bag of slippery fluid, and that fluid has gas dissolved in it, a bit like a sealed
can of soda. Pull the joint apart and the pressure inside drops, so the gas suddenly comes out as a bubble. In 2015,
scientists filmed a finger being pulled inside an MRI scanner: the bubble appeared at the very moment of the crack.
(Whether the noise is the bubble forming or the bubble partly collapsing is still argued.) Subscribe for a new strange
question every day.

Will it give you arthritis? Dr. Donald Unger cracked the knuckles of his left hand, and never his right, at least
twice a day for 50 years: no arthritis in either hand, and an Ig Nobel Prize in 2009. Bigger studies have found no
link with arthritis either. Bonus: a knuckle can't crack again for about 20 minutes, while the gas dissolves back.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** his room (the computer's fan), inside the hand (a muffled slow pulse), the scanner's knocking (kept under
  360 Hz), the office's hush, the lightbox's mains hum.
- **Hits:** every loud sound sits in a pause of the narration: the door and Mom's wordless scolding (after "CRACK!"),
  the x-ray's ding (after "harmed."), the fizz (after "soda."), the stretch's squeak (after "it..."), the needle (after
  "drops..."), the pop (after "POP!"), the winch's last clicks (after "MRI..."), the scan's crack (after "crack."), three of
  the doctor's cracks, the all-clear (after "hand."), the stamp, Mom's hmph and the door. What happens ON a word is a
  thud under 250 Hz or a tick above 5 kHz. The one exception is the big CRACK: a 90 ms burst on the frame and on the
  word itself (`knuckle_crack`, a new atom in `sfxkit.py`).
- **Score:** a tiptoe while he pushes, cut dead by the CRACK and kept out for Mom and the deadpan answer; a bouncy
  groove for the soda that stops for the fizz; the curious mechanism groove; a held breath and a plucked bass for the
  pull, silent from "and" to the pop; a sneaky walk for the scanner that stops for "the crack"; a stride piano for
  the doctor that runs away with his fifty years; nothing under the verdict and the button.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qa.py` on the delivered file: see "Ship review".

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "No bones were harmed": the crack is not bone on bone and nothing breaks; it comes from the fluid in the joint | established | Roston & Wheeler Haines 1947; Unsworth et al. 1971; Kawchuk et al. 2015 |
| "Each knuckle is sealed in slippery fluid ... full of dissolved gas": the joint is closed in a capsule of synovial fluid, which holds dissolved gas (mostly carbon dioxide) | established | Unsworth et al. 1971; Kawchuk et al. 2015 (introduction) |
| "Stretch it ... the pressure drops ... and POP! A bubble": pulling the surfaces apart lowers the pressure in the fluid until a gas cavity opens suddenly. The soda is a metaphor for gas leaving a liquid when the pressure on it falls | established that a cavity forms (x-rays in 1947, cine MRI in 2015) | Roston & Wheeler Haines 1947; Kawchuk et al. 2015 |
| What makes the NOISE: the cavity forming, or its partial collapse | debated. The MRI shows the cavity appearing at the moment of the sound and staying; a 2018 model reproduces the sound (about 83 dB) from a bubble that partly collapses and leaves a smaller one behind. The script only says what was seen ("right on the crack"); the description says it is still argued | Kawchuk et al. 2015; Chandran Suja & Barakat 2018 |
| "Scientists caught it in an MRI ... right on the crack": a finger was pulled by a cable inside an MRI scanner; the cavity appeared within one frame (under 310 ms) at the time of the crack | established observation (one volunteer, ten joints). The scan on screen is a drawing after the paper's video, and says so | Kawchuk et al. 2015 |
| "He cracked only his LEFT hand, for fifty years. Arthritis? Neither hand.": Dr. Donald Unger cracked the knuckles of his left hand at least twice a day for 50 years (at least 36,500 times, the number on screen) and rarely the right: no arthritis in either hand | one man's self-experiment (n = 1), published as a letter; Ig Nobel Prize for Medicine, 2009 (its citation says "more than sixty years") | Unger 1998; Improbable Research, 2009 winners |
| BIGGER STUDIES AGREE (on screen): no link between habitual knuckle cracking and osteoarthritis of the hand | established (retrospective studies of 28, 215 and 300 people). One of them, in 1990, also found more hand swelling and a weaker grip in crackers; that has not been repeated | Swezey & Swezey 1975; deWeber et al. 2011; Castellanos & Axelrod 1990; J Fam Pract 65(10):725 |
| Not in the script: a knuckle cannot be cracked again for about 20 minutes (description only); forcing a joint has caused the odd injury, so the Short never says "crack away" | established observation; case reports | Kawchuk et al. 2015; Chan, Steinberg & Bozentka 1999 |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 6 minutes; `src/cover.sh` then renders `cover.jpg`. They need
python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile, pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic
chain runs), node with playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries
the scenes use are vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared
CC0 folder `assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`); this Short uses
none. The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
`src/qc_gags.py` prints how loud each joke sound is in its own pause.
