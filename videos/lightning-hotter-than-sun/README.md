## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Lightning can heat the air it passes through to about 50,000 °F (≈ 27,700 °C), five times hotter than the surface of the Sun ("up to five times hotter") | established | NOAA National Weather Service, [How Hot Is Lightning?](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-temperature) and [Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder) ("may reach as high as 50,000 °F, 5 times hotter than the surface of the sun"); Britannica, Thunderstorm: cloud-to-ground lightning ("on the order of 30,000 °C (50,000 °F), about five times hotter than the surface of the Sun") |
| The Sun's surface (the photosphere) is about 5,500 °C (≈ 10,000 °F) | established | UCAR Center for Science Education, [The Sun's photosphere](https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/sun-space-weather/sun-photosphere); effective temperature 5,772 K. The core is far hotter: the comparison is with the surface, and the words say so |
| "The Sun's basically... lukewarm." | a joke (hyperbole) | — |
| Air is a very poor conductor, so it gets extremely hot when lightning passes through it ("the air cooks") | established | NWS, How Hot Is Lightning? |
| The channel heats in a few millionths of a second (description only) | established | NOAA NSSL, Lightning FAQ (archived: the air goes above 50,000 °F "in a few millionths of a second") |
| The hot channel of a return stroke is about 1–2 cm across ("as thin as your thumb"); measurements of the glowing channel range up to several cm | established (the figure varies with the method) | Rakov & Uman, *Lightning: Physics and Effects* (2003), as cited in physics problem sets; a review of framing-camera measurements (mean 6.5 cm for the luminous channel) |
| A bolt is kilometres long ("KILOMETRES LONG" label) | established | NWS, Thunder ("it travels many miles through the air") |
| The heating makes the air expand so fast it sends out the sound wave we hear as thunder ("it explodes... That BOOM? Thunder.") | established | NWS, Thunder; NSSL Lightning FAQ |
| Lightning hitting (quartz-rich) sand can melt it into glass tubes, fulgurites ("it can melt it into GLASS") | established | US National Park Service, [Fulgurites: The Power of Lightning](https://www.nps.gov/articles/grsa-fulgurites.htm) (Great Sand Dunes); Utah Geological Survey, Fulgurites |
| Flash-to-bang: about 3 s per kilometre (5 s per mile) (comment only) | established | NWS, Thunder |

Numbers on screen: SUN'S SURFACE 5,500 °C; LIGHTNING · UP TO 27,700 °C (= 50,000 °F); 5×.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
