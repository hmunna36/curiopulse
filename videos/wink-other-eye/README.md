A night café, a spy rendezvous: the hiker (the house rig in a tan trench coat) has to wink a signal to his contact, a grey cat in a fedora at the next table, and his other eye squints every time. A puppet string ties both his eyelids; he pulls its bar with one hand and props a lid up with the other; the finger slips. The café's chalkboard counts 96 of 100; the cat winks back and squints too. His face can wink and squint one eye at a time (`src/web/woface.js`, his `drawFace` redone); the cat is cats-purr's `purrcat.js` in grey with a hat.

## The plan (written before the build)

- **Spine:** you think a wink shuts one eye, but your other eye squints too, because a blink drives both lids together
  (one string) and a wink means holding one side back, and the hold slips.
- **Button (written first):** "Now try the OTHER eye." (sends them straight back into the test with the other eye; it
  runs into "Wink. Keep your other eye STILL.").
- **Running device:** the cat spy at the next table in a fedora, waiting for his wink signal: set up (it tilts its head
  at his double squint, 3.1-4.5 s), escalate (it loses patience and taps the table, checks its watch, 12.5-14.6 s),
  reverse (it winks back at him and ITS other eye squints too, 22.5-24.3 s). Back at its table for the loop.
- **Opening:** DEMO arm of the opening test (`yt.mjs next-slot`): frame 0 is his face alone, close and big, the
  screen-right eye (the one that must stay still) ringed, "KEEP THIS EYE STILL" on screen; the dare "Wink. Keep your
  other eye STILL." spoken from 0.2 s; the attempt from 0.7 s (his right eye shuts, the ringed eye squints, the ring goes
  red); the café opens up at 1.5 s with him in it mid-attempt.
- **Hook formula:** The Direct Address (the dare itself, an order). Candidates: The Impossible Claim ("Your eyes can't
  wink alone.": overclaims, 4 % can) and The Question ("Can you wink without the other eye moving?": slower, and DEMO
  wants an order, no question). The last three: The Direct Address (sad-eyebrows), The Comparison (uranus-night),
  The Direct Address (finger-flip): the DEMO arm asks for an order, so it is The Direct Address again.
- **Length:** tight arm (25-30 s, 55-64 words): 55 words, 26.83 s; the answer starts at 4.62 s.
- **The test (8-13 s rule):** 5-13 s the viewer blinks with him (9.3 s), winks again (12.6 s) and holds a lid with a
  finger (14.6 s); the mechanism is one picture on his eyelids: one string tied to both upper lids.
- **Flash-forward:** 5.30 s for 0.6 s, a window (800, 700, r 175) showing the cat's reverse wink (23.2 s), in the pause
  after "Relax.".
- **Cover:** his double squint with the ringed eye, "WINK. / ONE EYE?" (decided at the cover step).
- **Caption question:** "Did your other eye move?"

**Title:** Can You WINK Without the Other Eye Moving? 😉

**Description:**
Wink. Keep your other eye still. It moved, right? A blink pulls both eyelids together, like one string tied to both lids. A wink means pulling that string while holding one lid back, and the hold slips: in one study of 89 healthy adults, 96 % had the other eye move too when they shut one eye.

Did your other eye move?

The science: Citron and colleagues (Facial Plastic Surgery & Aesthetic Medicine, 2022) filmed 89 healthy people making one-sided faces; 96 % showed a mirror movement of the other eye during a one-sided eye closure. Winking is a learned skill: many people can wink only one eye, and children under 10 more often can't wink at all (Sutton, 1958); people who could wink only one side learned the other with practice in a 2019 brain-scan study (Lin and colleagues, Brain and Behavior). The string is a picture of the idea, not a real part of your face.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** café room tone (brown noise under 200 Hz) and rain on the glass (only above 5.6 kHz), gated to the café shots.
- **Hits:** a high tink as the right eye shuts and a low thud as the ringed eye squints (frame 1); a whoosh into every cut (the two whips louder); the cat's "mrrp?" in the gap after "right?"; the string's glints as it drops and a twang plus the hitstop's layered hit (snap, a 300–950 Hz kick, a short cymbal) when it snaps taut; a low thud and two glass tinks just after "Blink."; a wooden clack on the yank and a bonk after "drop."; a ding after "A wink?"; a slack-string boing after "UP."; a rising high whine of strain; a slide whistle and a layered hit for the slip; chalk and rolling high ticks on the board; the biggest hit of the Short (boing, low kick, crash) on the cat's squint just after "too.". Every gag sits in the gap after its word; under words only sounds under 250 Hz or above 5.2 kHz.
- **Score:** the loop score: a sneaky minor groove (Dm, Bb, Gm, A) fitted to 12 bars at 107.3 BPM (12 × 4 × 0.559 s = 26.833 s = the Short), his motif a marimba hook every two bars, the cat's a low pizzicato "bum-bum", a shaker, two marimba pickup notes into bar 1 for the loop. Five dead stops: 0–0.71 s (the dare), 8.91–9.77 (the string's sting and "Blink."), 18.51–19.16 (the slip), 22.30–23.85 (full silence before the cat's squint, then the biggest hit), 24.37–24.77 (the button).
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP
  after AAC. `src/qa.py`: see the ship review.

## Science notes

Every claim in the script or on screen, how sure science is, and where it comes from: two independent sources each
(direction sheet, rule 7). Hedged lines are said as hedges; a myth is said as a myth.

| Claim | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| When healthy people wink, the other eye usually moves too: 96 % of 89 healthy adults showed a mirror movement of the other eye during a one-sided eye closure ("ninety-six people out of a hundred... squinted too", said as one study) | established in that study; one study, said as one | Citron I. et al., "Descriptive Study of Facial Motor Cocontractions During Voluntary Facial Movement in a Healthy Population", Facial Plast Surg Aesthet Med 2022 (doi 10.1089/fpsam.2022.0072, PMID 36083281); involuntary "mirror" movements that come with voluntary ones are normal in healthy people (motor overflow): "A Reflection on Motor Overflow, Mirror Phenomena, Synkinesia", UCL Discovery 10171927 (review) |
| A blink closes both eyes together (the "one string") | established | StatPearls, "Corneal Reflex" (NCBI Bookshelf NBK534247: touching one eye closes both, the reflex reaches both facial nerves); Kofler et al., "The blink reflex and its modulation" (review, PMC10978309); voluntary blinks close both lids at once (blink EMG study, Paparella et al., Brain Sci 2022, 12:1228) |
| A wink is a learned, voluntary skill of shutting one side and holding the other back ("holding one lid up" is the metaphor): many people can wink only one eye, children under 10 are more often unable, and one-sided winkers can train the other side | established | Sutton P.R.N., Br J Psychol 1958 (527 Europeans, 262 Polynesians: winking improves with age); Lin, Lee, Huang, Sun, "Cerebral control of winking before and after learning", Brain Behav 2019 (41 of 63 recruits winked one side only; 24 learned the other) |
| WHY the hold leaks (the upper face gets commands from both halves of the brain) | debated: the textbook account, weak evidence in humans | not said in the script; the "one string" picture stands for the shared drive of a blink only. Cattaneo & Pavesi, "Facial paralysis: a critical review of accepted explanation" (PubMed 19910126) |
| How often we blink (on screen: none; background only) | established | about 15-20 a minute (AAO; Cleveland Clinic) |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The cinematic lighting runs on the graphics
chip through Chrome's WebGL2; without one the render falls back to the classic look and says so (`look: ...`). The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
