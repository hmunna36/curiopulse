**Title:** Why Does Ice Cream Give You BRAIN FREEZE? 🧊

**Description:**
One big slurp of something cold and your forehead freezes. But the cold went in your mouth! The cold hits the roof
of your mouth, the blood vessels there squeeze tight and then fling wide open, and blood rushes into an artery at the
front of the brain. In one study, the pain lasted exactly as long as that rush. Scientists think it's your body
flooding in warm blood to protect your brain... it just overdoes it.

The pain travels up a big nerve in your face (the trigeminal nerve), and your brain blames the wrong spot: your
forehead. And yes, gulping makes it worse. A 13-year-old tested it, and the study was published in a real medical
journal in 2002. The fix: press your tongue to the roof of your mouth. Or just slow down.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** diner room tone, fridge hum and rain on the window; icy wind for the title; a pulsing blood-flow rumble

- **Hits:** straw slurps and bubbles, a rising crackle into the freeze, `ice_crack` (new sfxkit atom: crack + glassy

  blips, a creak on SQUEEZE, a big bloop on WIDE OPEN, a monitor tone that follows the traces, a creaking gauge and

  "Your forehead.", stopwatch ticks, gulps, a stamp thump on PUBLISHED, drips as the frost melts. `slurp` is also

  the sigh, "It just... overdoes it", "Your forehead.", and the chuckle.
- **Mix:** voice first (leveler, gentle compression), effects duck under speech, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Brain freeze ("cold-stimulus headache", medically "sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia") comes from something cold touching the roof of the mouth (palate) | established | Wikipedia, *Cold-stimulus headache* (and its references); International Classification of Headache Disorders |
| The palate's blood vessels constrict with the cold and then rapidly dilate | established (the described mechanism) | Wikipedia, *Cold-stimulus headache*; Serrador et al., FASEB J / Experimental Biology 2012 |
| In a study, blood flow through the anterior cerebral artery surged during brain freeze and the pain faded as the artery constricted again | established (one small study, 13 adults, transcranial Doppler) | Serrador et al. 2012 (Harvard / VA New Jersey), reported by ScienceDaily, 22 Apr 2012: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/04/120422231742.htm |
| The rush of warm blood may be protecting the brain from the cold, and the pressure may cause the pain | leading idea (the researchers' speculation) → said as "Scientists think" and stamped ONE IDEA | Serrador, quoted in the ScienceDaily / APS release above; Medical News Today 244458 |
| The pain is carried by the trigeminal nerve and felt in the forehead: referred pain | established | Wikipedia, *Cold-stimulus headache* |
| Eating fast doubles it: 27 % of fast eaters (100 ml in under 5 s) vs 13 % of cautious eaters (30 s+) got a headache | established (one randomised trial, 145 students) | Kaczorowski M & Kaczorowski J, "Ice cream evoked headaches (ICE-H) study: randomised trial of accelerated versus cautious ice cream eating regimen", BMJ 2002;325:1445–6 (PMC139031) |
| The study was run by a 13-year-old (with her father as co-author) and published in a medical journal | established | CBS News and JSTOR Daily coverage of the ICE-H study; BMJ 2002 |
| Pressing the tongue to the roof of the mouth (warming the palate), or a warm drink, relieves it | established advice | Wikipedia, *Cold-stimulus headache* |

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi), node with playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.

The cover is a dedicated shot outside the timeline:
`python3 -c` a copy of `.work/timeline.json` with `shots = [{"id": "cover", "start": 0, "end": 1}]` and no captions,
then `node src/render.js <that copy> .work/cover 0` and `ffmpeg -i .work/cover/f_0000.png -q:v 3 cover.jpg`.
