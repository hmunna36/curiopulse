**Title:** Why Do We Get GOOSEBUMPS? 😱

**Description:**
A scary movie, a cold breeze, a great song... and your arm turns into plucked goose skin. Each hair on your arm has its
own tiny muscle. When you're scared or cold, your nerves fire them all at once: the hair stands up, and the skin around
it bunches into a bump. Subscribe for a new strange question every day.

For furry animals it's a brilliant trick: fluffed-up fur traps warm air and makes a scared cat look huge. We lost the
fur but kept the reflex. Music can set it off too: scientists think a big emotional moment trips the same alarm system.
And a few people can raise goosebumps on command (a 2018 study tested 32 of them).

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The bumps look like the skin of a plucked goose, hence "goosebumps" | established (etymology) | [Wikipedia: Goose bumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose_bumps) ("When a goose's feathers are plucked, its skin has protrusions where the feathers were") |
| Each arm hair has its own tiny muscle (the arrector pili, smooth muscle from the follicle to the upper dermis) | established | [NIH Research Matters: What goosebumps are for](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/what-goosebumps-are); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose_bumps) |
| Scared or cold: the sympathetic nerves ("fight or flight") make all those muscles contract together | established | NIH Research Matters (above); [Scientific American, G. A. Bubenik: Why do humans get "goosebumps" when they are cold, or under other circumstances?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-do-humans-get-goosebu/) |
| The hair stands up and the skin around it is pulled into a bump | established | Scientific American (above); Wikipedia |
| For furry animals it works: raised fur traps a layer of air (insulation) and makes a frightened animal, such as a cat, look bigger | established | Scientific American (above); Wikipedia |
| "You lost the fur but kept the button": in humans the reflex is a leftover | established (the standard view). Not used in the script: a 2020 mouse study found the same nerve and muscle also help switch on hair-follicle stem cells | [Harvard HSCRB: The real reason behind goosebumps](https://hscrb.harvard.edu/news/the-real-reason-behind-goosebumps) (Shwartz et al., Cell 2020) |
| Music can raise goosebumps too; a big emotional moment sets off the same system | leading idea, said as "Scientists think" and stamped ONE IDEA | [Benedek & Kaernbach 2011, Biological Psychology](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Benedek-Kaernbach-2011.pdf) (camera-measured piloerection to music and film, with higher skin conductance); Salimpoor et al. 2011, Nature Neuroscience (dopamine release at the peak moments of music that gives chills) |
| Some people can raise goosebumps on command | established, rare | [Heathers et al. 2018, PeerJ: The voluntary control of piloerection](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6071615/) (32 people) |

Simplifications: "each arm hair" (eyelashes and eyebrows have no such muscle; arm hairs do); the muscle is shown pulling
one follicle in one plane; the hair rising and the bump are shown one after the other for clarity (they happen together).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch in about 10 minutes. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile,
pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi; pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with
playwright-core and an installed Chrome, and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are
vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder
`assets/sfx/` (`git sparse-checkout add assets/sfx`; credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`); this Short uses none.
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
The cover is `SC.cover` in `src/web/scenes.js`, rendered from a one-shot copy of the timeline (shots = one `cover` shot).
