# What Happens If You Stop Eating? (Day by Day) — long-form (N:NN)

A short animated film in the CurioPulse look. A mountain hut in a blizzard (log walls, an iron stove, a candle that
burns down day by day, an orange emergency radio, a tin mug of melted snow, tally marks by the window): the hiker in
his yellow coat at a rough table, with Pip's chocolate bar in front of him (PEAK, from the summit shop) and the rind of
a lemon on his plate; a fox on the windowsill outside, the one that stole his food bag. The other worlds: the mountain
outside (the storm, the summit shop in the sun, the rescue helicopter), his body as a see-through diagram (a stomach
that sweeps itself and growls, an alarm clock on it at mealtimes, the liver as a pantry of sugar jars, the brain with a
face drinking from it through a straw, his muscle, the fat as a packed lunch, the heart), a dining hall in Minnesota in
1944 and a hospital room in Dundee in 1965 (both on old film), and Pip's kitchen at home. The narrator, captions and
bloom are the Shorts'. A clock in the top left counts the hours, then the days, since his last meal.

## The film in five lines

- **The want:** to bring Pip her favourite chocolate from the summit shop, in one piece.
- **The obstacle:** a storm snows him into an empty hut for a week, a fox takes his food bag, and his last meal is a
  lemon. Day by day his own body comes for his attention, his mood, his muscle, and his willpower: the bar is right
  there on the table.
- **The turn:** his body turns out to have packed its own lunch (weeks of it, as fat), and the hunger even goes quiet;
  what breaks is the mind (the Minnesota men, the wrapper read eleven times). On the sixth midnight he unwraps the bar...
  and wraps it back up. At home, Pip's one question every night, "Is my chocolate okay?", turns out to have been about
  him: if the chocolate was okay, so was he.
- **The feeling at the end:** warmth, a lump in the throat, and a laugh: she gives him the bigger half.
- **The final image:** Pip's kitchen, warm afternoon light, the two of them at the table, each with half of the bar; the
  window behind them shows the mountain, clear now, and the fox sitting in the snow with the empty food bag; the theme
  resolves.

- **Motif:** the chocolate bar. In the hut it is the promise (it glows on the table, the thing he must not eat); in act
  two it is the temptation (he reads its wrapper to the fox); at midnight on day six it is the test (unwrapped, wrapped
  back up); at home it is the answer to Pip's question, and she breaks it in two. The lemon is the second motif: the
  viewer's first test, his last meal, and the soup that makes the viewer's mouth water again at the rescue.
- **Wordless beats:** the fox taking the bag and running (after "a fox made it to his food bag", 1.4 s); the lemon rind
  on the plate as the clock starts (1.6 s); midnight on day six, the bar unwrapped in the candlelight, then wrapped back
  up (2.4 s); the helicopter's light flooding the window (1.2 s); Pip breaking the bar and holding out the bigger half
  (2.2 s); the final image.
- **Colour script:** the hut's warm stove and candle against the cold blue window (act 1, a cosy adventure); the body's
  blue x-ray with gold sugar (hunger as a mechanism); the hut cooler and darker as the days go on (the stove low, frost
  on the glass, a blanket, a beanie); green for the new fuel on day three (wonder); sepia for 1944 and 1965; the darkest
  blue at midnight on day six with one candle stub; white light and blue sky on day seven (relief); Pip's kitchen in
  warm afternoon gold (home).
- **Score:** one theme, a simple five-note phrase that keeps asking (it ends on a note that wants to resolve): stated on
  a music box in the hook (the lemon), a bouncy pizzicato for the first day (the alarm clock, the fox), thinner and
  lower as the days go on, a clockwork tick for the body's machinery, an old upright piano for 1944 and 1965, a low
  drone for the edge, nothing at midnight on day six but the wind and the candle, the theme warm on strings when the
  storm breaks, and resolved on the final image.

## The hook (reference/hooks.md, binding since 9 Oct 2026)

Three candidates, checked against the five rules and each formula's "fails when":
- **The Question** (kept): *"What happens if you stop eating? One man lasted three hundred and eighty-two days. But
  first... bite a lemon. In your head. Now."* The first sentence is the title's question word for word, the exact words
  people type (rule 1); one idea, a body without food day by day (rule 2); a specific, sourced number in the second
  sentence (rule 3: Stewart & Fleming 1973); the 382 is on screen at 0:03 and paid in the last minute with the man, his
  calendar and his scale (rule 4: the artifact is shown inside 20 s as the stamped number, and its proof comes in act
  three); no greeting (rule 5). It is also the viewer's first test, by 0:06: they bite a lemon in their head and their
  mouth waters (it does for most people), which the next line explains: the body getting ready for food from a thought.
  Frame 1 is the thumbnail's picture in action: his face, close, mid-bite into a lemon, puckering, by candlelight.
- *The Statistic*: "Your body carries enough food for about a month... and you don't even know where." True and
  surprising, but it is the act-two payoff, and as a hook it promises a fact rather than a story.
- *Someone Else's Result*: "In 1965, a man in Scotland stopped eating. For 382 days." Strong and sourced, but its
  first sentence does not confirm the title's words, and it makes the film about Barbieri instead of the viewer.
The last three films opened on The Question (never-sleep), The Clock (hold-your-breath) and Contrarian Flip
(space-without-suit); The Question is back after two others, so no formula runs three times in a row.

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch. It needs python3 (numpy, scipy, soundfile, pyloudnorm, pillow, certifi;
pedalboard for the studio mix chain, else the classic chain runs), node with playwright-core and a Chrome or Chromium
(`CHROME_PATH`), and ffmpeg with libx264/aac. `.claude/skills/cp-long/bin/cloud-setup.sh` installs all of it on a
fresh Ubuntu machine. The JavaScript libraries the scenes use are vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences
inside). Recorded one-shots come from the repo's shared CC0 folder `assets/sfx/` (credits in `assets/sfx/CREDITS.md`).
The narration takes are committed in `src/voice/`, so no API call is needed unless a line of `src/script.txt`
changes. For a changed line, run `src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
