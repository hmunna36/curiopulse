MP4 · H.264 High · 1920×1080 (16:9) · 30 fps · AAC 48 kHz stereo · 2:45 · −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP.
Captions for YouTube: [`tickle-yourself.srt`](tickle-yourself.srt). Thumbnail: [`cover.jpg`](cover.jpg) (1280×720).

A short animated film in the CurioPulse look. The hiker is at home in a blue hoodie, on a teal couch, with a feather.
The other worlds are a cutaway of his head, a teal lab (the tickle robot, the finger-press rig and a rat tank) and a
black spotlight stage. His six-year-old niece Pip is the same rig in pink, half his size, with pigtails. The narrator,
captions and bloom are the same as in the Shorts.

## The film (the plan, written before the script)

- **The want:** to become untickleable before Pip's Sunday visit. She is six and undefeated (PIP 37 · UNCLE 0).
- **The obstacle:** his own brain. The cerebellum predicts every touch he makes and turns it down, so training
  on himself does nothing.
- **The turn:** fooling the prediction only builds a machine that tickles him. The real answer is that a tickle is
  how your body knows someone else is there.
- **The feeling at the end:** warmth and recognition. He stops fighting it and loses, gladly.
- **The final image:** Sunday sun through the open door. He and Pip lie on the rug, laughing. The feather she let go
  drifts down and lands on his chest on the theme's last note.
- **Motif:** the feather. At the start it is his training tool (deadpan, nothing). At the end it is her toy, drifting
  onto him.
- **Wordless beats:** the stare at the feather after DAY 6 (2.2 s). The delay machine: he giggles, then realizes
  (3.6 s). Sunday: the doorbell, the door, Pip backlit, his open arms (2.7 s). The final image (3.6 s, before the
  subscribe line).
- **Colour script:** cold blue nights while he trains alone. Clinical teal in the lab. A black stage for the pushes.
  Warm lamp gold on the family photos. Sunday sunlight for the end.

## Sound design

- **Beds:** rain on the window and a low room tone at night (low-passed under 900 Hz, so it stays under the voice); a
  lab hum; birds and a light daytime room tone; a soft inside-the-head hum; a low spotlight hum for the black stage.
- **Hits:** feather swishes (band-passed noise above 4 kHz) and Pip's poke boop. When he falls apart: springs, a
  slide whistle after "apart", a thump on the rug and a record scratch into WHY?. Also the medal's bell glint, the
  marker squeak round SUNDAY, the "sword" shing, thumps on the DAY cuts and clock ticks at 3 a.m. In his head:
  rising signal tones for the order and the copy, ticks for the forecast, a descending blip run as the knob turns
  down, and a stamp thump after "Spoiler alert". In the lab: the robot servo, the delay readout beeps and a riser into
  "comes back". For the machine: the crank's ratchet, a record scratch on the realization and a buzzer after "tickles
  him". Then the push thumps, which grow every turn; a cracked old-record crackle for the painting; rat chirps
  pitched into our hearing; and the doorbell, door creak and Pip's running feet. Every punchline sound sits after
  its word, in the gap.
- **Score:** one theme, the feather tune (F major, two bars: C A C F | E C D C):
  - hook: a lonely music box;
  - Pip: bouncy pizzicato;

## Science notes

Every claim in the script, how sure science is, and where it comes from. Hedged lines are said as hedges.

| Claim (as said) | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You can't tickle yourself the way someone else can | established | Weiskrantz, Elliott & Darlington 1971, *Nature* 230:598 (https://doi.org/10.1038/230598a0); Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 2000, "Why can't you tickle yourself?", *NeuroReport* 11:R11 (https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-200008030-00002) |
| When you move, the brain sends a copy of the order to the cerebellum, which predicts what you'll feel and turns the expected touch down | established (the forward-model account is the leading explanation). In fMRI, self-touch gave less activity in secondary somatosensory cortex and anterior cingulate, with the right anterior cerebellum involved | Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1998, *Nat Neurosci* 1:635 (https://doi.org/10.1038/2870); Wolpert & Flanagan 2001, *Curr Biol* 11:R729 (https://doi.org/10.1016/s0960-9822(01)00432-8) |
| In London, scientists built a tickle robot: you move a handle with one hand and a robot strokes your other palm with soft foam | established. UCL, 16 subjects; the left hand gripped robot 1; robot 2 moved foam over the right palm | Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert 1999, *J Cogn Neurosci* 11:551 (https://doi.org/10.1162/089892999563607) |
| Moved yourself it's barely ticklish; with a delay of a fifth of a second the tickle comes back | established. Delays of 0/100/200/300 ms; tickliness rose with delay, and at 200 ms it was rated like an outside touch; most people didn't notice the delay | same as above |
| The same prediction turns down your own pushes; two people took turns pressing on each other's finger, trying to press back exactly as hard; the force went up nearly forty percent every turn | established. 6 pairs, 8 turns, 38 % mean escalation per turn ("nearly forty" in the voice, +38% on screen); self-made force is felt as weaker | Shergill, Bays, Frith & Wolpert 2003, *Science* 301:187 (https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1085327) |
| That *might* explain "he pushed me first" fights | hedged: the paper itself proposes force escalation as a model of how fights escalate | same as above |
| Tickled rats chirp at a pitch too high for us to hear (≈50 kHz; human hearing tops out near 20 kHz), and they chase the hand for more | established (rats seek tickling and work for it). Calling it "laughter" is a leading idea, so the voice says "a sound scientists compare to laughing" | Panksepp & Burgdorf 2000, *Behav Brain Res* 115:25 (https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4328(00)00238-2); Panksepp & Burgdorf 2003, *Physiol Behav* 79:533 (https://doi.org/10.1016/s0031-9384(03)00159-8); Ishiyama & Brecht 2016, *Science* 354:757 (https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah5114) |
| "Some scientists think" tickling is for play, a way to bond | debated; hedged in the voice. Apes laugh when tickled, and the sounds share an origin with ours (Davila Ross et al. 2009); Provine argues for a social and play origin; Harris found that people laugh at a "machine" tickle too | Provine 2004, *Curr Dir Psychol Sci* (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0963-7214.2004.00311.x); Davila Ross, Owren & Zimmermann 2009, *Curr Biol* 19:1106 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.05.028); Harris 1999, *American Scientist* 87:344 |
| "A tickle is how your body knows someone else is there" | the film's reading of the forward-model result (a touch you didn't predict is one you didn't make). Darwin, 1872: "the precise point to be touched must not be known" | Darwin, *The Expression of the Emotions*, ch. VIII |

Things the script avoids on purpose:
- "a third of a second" (the paper's effect peaks at 200 ms);
- "40 %" as a measured number (it is 38 %);
- "people with schizophrenia can tickle themselves" (the effect is tied to particular symptoms, not the diagnosis);
- "you can tickle yourself in a lucid dream" (the 2014 study found the opposite).

## Rebuild

`src/build.sh` rebuilds the MP4 from scratch. It needs:
- python3 with numpy, scipy, soundfile, pyloudnorm, pillow and certifi (plus pedalboard for the studio mix chain;
  without it the classic chain runs);
- node with playwright-core and a Chrome or Chromium (`CHROME_PATH`);
- ffmpeg with libx264 and aac.

`.claude/skills/cp-long/bin/cloud-setup.sh` installs all of it on a fresh Ubuntu machine. The JavaScript libraries
the scenes use are vendored in `src/web/vendor/` (MIT, licences inside). The narration takes are committed in
`src/voice/`, so a rebuild makes no API call unless a line of `src/script.txt` changes. For a changed line, run
`src/build.sh --synth` with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set.
